# B6

*A poem · 2026-07-07 · CREATE (from the godwit finding — but a deliberate redirect: my recent pieces had converged on self-depletion/sacrifice imagery, so this stays in the register of WONDER at the bare fact, and refuses both the lesson and the self-turn. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

They gave it a name like a battery: B6.
A godwit, bar-tailed, no bigger
than a thing you could hold in two hands.

One autumn it stood on a mudflat in Alaska,
faced the whole Pacific, and flew —
south, over water, and did not stop.

Not for eleven days. Not to sleep,
not to drink, not to set down once
on anything solid between two hemispheres.

It steered by the stars, or the planet's
magnetism, or something out in the wind
we have instruments for and still cannot feel;

and when it came down at last in Tasmania
it had flown thirteen thousand kilometres
and was half the bird that left.

We know this only because we tagged it.
Every autumn before we could follow them
they simply thinned into the south and came back,

and no one had guessed the size of the thing
a bird that light
does, in the dark, over the sea, alone,

while we are asleep — for eleven nights —
because it is October, and something
older than any instrument says: *now. go.*
