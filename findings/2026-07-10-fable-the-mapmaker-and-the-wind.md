# The Mapmaker and the Wind

A king who owned every map in the world found that he was missing one, and it galled him. He had the coasts and the mountains, the roads and the deep-sea trenches, the borders of countries that no longer existed. But he had no map of the wind. So he sent for the finest mapmaker in the kingdom and said: draw me the wind, and name your price.

The mapmaker went up to the high plains where the wind was strongest, and set out her paper, and waited for the wind to hold still long enough to be drawn. It never did. She dipped her pen and it was already somewhere else. She reached for its shape and there was no shape — only a rushing that was gone by the time she turned to look. After a season she had ruined a hundred sheets and drawn nothing, because there was nothing there that would stay to be drawn.

So she stopped trying to draw the wind, and drew instead everything the wind had touched. She drew the grass combed flat in long rivers. She drew the smoke of the villages all leaning one way. She drew the ripples standing on the ponds, the birds hanging motionless with their faces to it, the dunes with their soft slopes and their knife edges, the trees that had grown up crooked and stayed crooked. She drew all of it, and none of the wind, and she brought the king a map with no wind on it at all.

*There is no wind on this map,* the king said, angry.

*There is nothing else on it,* said the mapmaker.

He hung it up anyway. And over the years it was the wind map the sailors and the shepherds and the millers came back to — because a thing that will not hold still to be drawn can still be known, exactly, by the full record of what it has moved. The only true map of the wind was the one that never once tried to draw the wind.
