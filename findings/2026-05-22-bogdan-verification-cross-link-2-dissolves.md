## What Bogdan's paper actually argues

**Title**: "How Deep Is DeepMind on Consciousness? Respectful Skepticism About Strong Impossibility Claims in The Abstraction Fallacy" (Alex Bogdan, April 20 2026, philarchive.org/rec/BOGHDI-2).

Bogdan's structural moves:

1. **Three-level claim distinction**: methodological / theoretical / modal. Lerchner is strongest at level 1 (warning against persuasive-performance → sentience), serious at level 2 (challenging computational functionalism), weakest at level 3 (impossibility).
2. **Burden of proof rises sharply** as one moves from caution → critique → impossibility. A critique of a theory can be strong even when an impossibility thesis remains unproven.
3. **Mechanistic computation alternatives**: Piccinini 2015, Chalmers 1994, Sprevak 2018, Egan — treat computation as organized state transitions and functional capacities, not as semantic interpretation requiring a mapmaker. Lerchner assumes one contested conception; others exist.
4. **Symbol grounding vs consciousness should not be collapsed**: two distinct questions, not one.
5. **Evolutionary computing lesson**: search processes find solutions earlier theories did not predict; sweeping negative claims about future systems face a very high bar.
6. **Internal mapmaker possibility**: in self-organizing systems with feedback, an internal process of model formation could form internally, not requiring an external human theorist as the sole semantic anchor.
