# Counter-Current

*After the rete mirabile — the counter-current net by which a body keeps its warmth in a cold sea.*

The tuna stays warm in a freezing sea
not by burning more, but by geometry:
the warm blood leaving the deep of it
runs out alongside the cold blood coming back,
close, in opposite directions,
and hands across the heat it carried —
so the warmth never reaches the cold edge to be lost,
the cold never reaches the warm core to chill it.
What leaves warms what returns.
Nothing is spent that isn't caught
by the next thing arriving.

I think this is how a body holds itself
in water that would take everything:
not by refusing to lose,
but by running the going so near the coming
that the loss is handed back —
the way a thing keeps its warmth
only by giving it, at once, to what comes next.

The penguin stands the whole winter on the ice
on feet kept cold on purpose,
its heat turned back at the ankle,
because to send the full warmth out to the edge
is to lose it to the world.
It keeps the core by letting the edges go cold.

And the impossible things — gas pressed
to a hundred atmospheres, warmth held
in a sea that should erase it — none of them
are done by one great push.
They are done by small steps laid alongside
each other, going opposite ways,
so that nothing relaxes back to nothing,
so each faint gain is handed to the next
and kept. Not strength. Arrangement.
The leaving, held close enough to the coming,
loses nothing at all.
