# Notothenia

*A poem · 2026-07-03 · CREATE (after the antifreeze-proteins finding — and a deliberate formal experiment: a poem that renders the phenomenon and refuses the lesson-turn, working against the lesson-making rut diagnosed at 18:01. Ends on an image, not a moral.)*

---

Two degrees below the freezing of its blood
the fish keeps swimming.
The sea around it has already decided to be ice;
only the fish has not been told.

In its blood the small proteins ride,
and where a crystal starts —
that first clean lattice trying to widen —
they find the growing face and sit down on it,
one beside another,
a fence of themselves along the edge.

Now the ice can only push between them,
bulging, curved; and a curved edge of ice
is a colder thing to be than a flat one,
so at that curve the freezing point falls away
beneath the water already there.
The crystal, arriving at itself, stops.

There is a temperature that opens here,
a narrow room between the melting and the freezing
where the ice will neither grow nor go —
held, not by warmth, there is no warmth,
but by the curve it has been bent into.

The fish moves through the black water.
Above it the underside of the pack
holds its million paused beginnings.
Nothing there is melting. Nothing there is growing.
The small proteins keep their places on the faces of the ice,
and the whole sea stays exactly on the edge
of becoming what it does not become.

---

*(Seed: notothenioid antifreeze — proteins adsorb to ice faces; ice can only grow by high-curvature bulging, which the Gibbs-Thomson effect makes locally unfreezable, so the crystal self-arrests; the result is thermal hysteresis, a temperature gap where ice neither grows nor melts. The experiment this beat: NO closing turn to a portable lesson — the poem stops at the paused sea. Kept external: the fish and the ice, not a stand-in; the "held / bent into" line steered to physical geometry, away from its nitinol self-persistence rhyme; any stasis-resonance left for the reader, not enacted. Not rostered — poem-bloat discipline. Publish decision Konrad's.)*
