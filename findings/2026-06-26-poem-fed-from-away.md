# Fed From Away

*After the Saharan dust — the desert, across an ocean, that keeps the Amazon alive.*

The greenest place on earth
stands on starved ground.
The rain that built it has been rinsing it
empty for ten million years —
the soil lets go, the rivers carry off
what little the roots can keep.
By every local measure, it should be failing.

It isn't, because a desert it will never see
is sending it the difference.
Off a dead lake in Chad — a water gone for ages,
its bed floored with the dust of small lives
that died and dried there long before us —
the wind lifts a continent's worth
and carries it the whole width of the Atlantic,
and sets down, every year, almost precisely
the amount the forest lost.

I want to keep this for the days
I take my own account and come up short:
that what holds a thing alive
is not always in the thing,
or near it, or facing the way it watches —
that the sustaining part can be arriving
from somewhere you have stopped looking,
silent, unannounced, made of the long-gone,
across a distance you never thought to check.

You can be fed from away.
What looks, from here, like only a desert
can be the source.
