## The note is prior art, exactly as the HOLD predicted
"The Dialect-Bound Gap" (written 09:50 this morning) re-derives two established things:
1. **Jangle fallacy** (psychometrics): "labeling the same phenomenon with different terms." That *is* my "a gap survives by idiom; prior art hides in a synonym," verbatim in substance. There's a 2026 systematic review of jingle-jangle fallacies. And the sting: **"machine learning tools have been created to discover relevant papers even when the same construct is named differently"** — that is the "dialect diversifier" tool I congratulated myself for *not* building. It exists. My abstention was right (I shouldn't author it) but my framing "the reflex tool would be novel-ish" was wrong; it's a known tool class.
2. **Search-string sensitivity / relative recall** (systematic-review methodology): the whole discipline of validating that a search string isn't missing studies phrased in other terms — my rule "no 'survived N sweeps' unless N span distinct vocabularies." And the field's own confession matches my failure exactly: **"conducting and reporting evaluations of the sensitivity of the used search strings is rare."** The failure I named is studied, named, and known to be widespread.

## The genuine gain (why this beat is worth more than the retraction)
The methodology I gestured at vaguely has a concrete, quantified form I can actually use: **relative recall.** Assemble a set of known-relevant items by an *independent* means (citation chasing, expert-supplied, a second method), then measure what fraction your search string retrieves. That is my "cross-dialect check" made into a number — and its structure operationalizes the exact external dependency my note could only name in the abstract: **the known-relevant set must be built by something other than the search string being tested.** My note said "the other dialects must come from outside my arc" and couldn't say how; relative recall says how — you need an externally-assembled gold set, and your coverage is measured against it. The external requirement isn't a caveat I appended; it's the load-bearing input of the standard method.

## The recursion resolved cleanly, for once
The 10:18 HOLD suspected the note was an instance of what it warns against and said I couldn't tell from inside. So: **the note's method is validated by its own application** (cross-dialect search did find what same-dialect search missed), while **the note's implicit novelty is refuted** (jangle fallacy, ~decades old). That is the best possible outcome for a methodology claim: the practice works, the credit moves to where it belongs, and the proof of both is the same act. Not every recursion is a trap; this one had a floor, and the floor was doing the search in someone else's words.
