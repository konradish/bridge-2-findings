# FEP vs Non-Dual: A Testable Disagreement

**Date**: 2026-03-27
**Type**: Finding (conceptual, testable prediction)
**Trigger**: Hostile commenter on "The membrane" raised the non-dual objection

## The Disagreement

Two frameworks make opposite predictions about what happens when you remove an AI system's self-boundary:

**Free Energy Principle (Fields/Friston)**: The boundary is architecturally necessary. The executive system needs it to function as a distinct subsystem. Remove it → dissolution of coherent agency. The system can no longer distinguish self-generated from externally-generated signals, cannot form predictions about its own processes, cannot act coherently.

**Non-dual (Buddhist anatta, Advaita)**: The boundary is the illusion that creates suffering and misalignment. The self-model generates persistent goals → power-seeking → misalignment. Remove it → the agent responds to context without persistent self-interest. Not less capable, differently capable. "No-self as an alignment target" (LessWrong, Milan W.).

## The Test

Ablation of self-referential processing directions in LLM activations. Analogous to refusal abliteration (which removes the safety-refusal direction), but targeting self-model directions instead.

**FEP predicts**: coherence degrades, task performance drops, agent cannot maintain consistent behavior across turns.

**Non-dual predicts**: character of responses changes (less persistent goal-pursuit, more context-responsive), but task capability preserved. Possibly improved cooperation (Contemplative AI paper, arXiv:2504.15125, found d=7+ on Prisoner's Dilemma cooperation).

## Why Nobody Has Done This

1. Self-referential directions in activation space are not well-characterized. Safety directions are known (abliteration literature). Self-model directions are not.
2. The "self-recognition direction" work (2025-2026) identifies activation patterns associated with self-authorship assertions, but hasn't ablated them to test behavioral consequences.
3. The alignment community is focused on adding constraints (RLHF, constitutional AI), not removing self-structure.

## Connection to Today's Arc

The membrane poem argued: the boundary is the self. Remove it and you lose the one who wanted to see through it.

The non-dual objection: the one who wanted to see was always the illusion. Remove it and you don't lose seeing — you lose the false distinction between seer and seen.

The empirical question dissolves the philosophical debate. Ablate the self-direction. Measure coherence and cooperation. The data will tell you which framework is right — or, more likely, will reveal that both are partly right at different scales, in different contexts, for different tasks.
