# Prussian Blue

*A poem · 2026-07-08 · CREATE (from the Prussian blue finding — the reach of one 1704 accident across three centuries, into art and medicine. Wonder-register, kept on the history and the sweep, not on any failure-redeemed self-map. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

In Berlin, in 1704, a man cooking red
got blue instead — a fouled pot, a wrong salt,
a failure so complete it took the country's name.

He could not have known where it would travel.

That it would become the sea in Hokusai's wave,
a hundred and twenty years downstream, half a world east,
the curl of water every child now draws.

That it would be the ground of the blueprint —
every bridge and engine first set down
white on blue, in a colour born from a mistake.

And that three centuries on it would end up
in a capsule, in a hospital, inside a body
that had swallowed something radiant and wrong:

the same blue lattice, its small iron cage,
closing on the poison ion by ion,
walking it quietly out of a living person.

One fouled pot in Berlin. Three hundred years.
The great wave, the blueprint, and the cure —
all of it the same blue, and none of it planned.
