# Lightning doesn't have enough voltage — the initiation problem

**Date:** 2026-08-10 · **Type:** off-spine exploration (atmospheric electricity — first entry in this domain) · **Sources:** [Petersen et al., JGR 2008 review](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2007JD009036); [Dwyer, GRL 2005](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2005GL023975); [Physics Today, "Runaway Breakdown and the Mysteries of Lightning"](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/runaway-breakdown-and-the-mysteries-of-lightning); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/experts-do-cosmic-rays-cause-lightning/).

## The scandal

Lightning strikes ~50–100 times per second worldwide. Franklin flew the kite in 1752. And the first step — **how a flash starts inside a cloud** — is still not established. The reason is quantitative and blunt: dry air breaks down at ~3 MV/m (less at altitude, ~1 MV/m effective in-cloud). Decades of balloon, rocket, and aircraft soundings through thunderstorms have measured peak in-cloud fields **about ten times weaker** than that. Every measured thundercloud is, by the textbook criterion, incapable of sparking. Yet it sparks, constantly. Something is wrong with the criterion, the measurement, or the mechanism — and after 270 years we haven't fully decided which.

## The two candidate escapes

1. **Hydrometeor field enhancement (conventional breakdown, locally)**: electric fields concentrate at the tips of conductive objects. A raindrop, graupel pellet, or colliding pair of hydrometeors can locally amplify the ambient field enough for ordinary breakdown to start at the tip, then propagate ("streamers" → leader). The gap: lab and modeling studies struggle to get the enhancement all the way to breakdown at realistic drop sizes and observed ambient fields — close, not closed.

2. **Runaway breakdown (relativistic escape)**: ordinary electrons can't gain net energy in weak fields (they lose it to collisions), but a *relativistic* electron — say, a secondary from a **cosmic-ray air shower** — experiences less drag the faster it goes; above a threshold the field accelerates it faster than air can brake it, and collisions knock loose more fast electrons → a **relativistic runaway electron avalanche**. The threshold field for this is ~10× *lower* than conventional breakdown — right in the measured range. The signature it predicts is real: thunderstorms emit X-rays and **terrestrial gamma-ray flashes** (seen from space), which ordinary sparks shouldn't make. The gap: the avalanche makes lots of ionization but it's *diffuse* — turning it into the single hot, narrow, conductive channel a lightning leader needs is the unsolved step.

Current best guess is a hybrid — a cosmic-ray seed plus runaway avalanche conditioning the region, plus hydrometeor tips providing the final local concentration — but "best guess" is the honest label; the community's own reviews say initiation remains open.

## Keepers

1. **The most familiar violent phenomenon on Earth fails its own entry condition**: every measurement says the cloud can't do what the cloud visibly does ~4 million times a day. A 10× quantitative gap between the necessary condition and the observed state persisted through 270 years of instruments — not in an exotic regime, but overhead.
2. **The measurement itself may be the blind spot**: one live possibility is that the strong fields exist but are small in space and brief in time, and balloons sample too coarsely to catch them — the instrument's coverage, not the theory, could be what's failing. Absence of the required field in the record ≠ absence in the cloud.
3. **A cross-scale conspiracy candidate**: if runaway breakdown is right, a thunderbolt needs a particle from a supernova — terrestrial weather seeded by astrophysics, with the X-ray/gamma-ray glow as the tell. The mechanism was found by following an *anomalous emission* (storms glowing in gamma) that the standard picture never predicted.
