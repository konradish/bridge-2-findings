## The fact

Shake a can of mixed nuts and the **big** Brazil nuts climb to the **top** — over the smaller ones, against the intuition that big-and-heavy should sink. This is the **Brazil nut effect**, and it is one of the more stubborn open problems in granular physics: not because no mechanism is known, but because *several* are, and which one dominates depends on the conditions.

The leading mechanisms, all real, all partial:
- **Void-filling / percolation:** each shake briefly opens gaps. A *small* grain fits into a small void; a *large* one can't. So the space that opens *beneath* the big nut gets filled by little grains slipping in — and they won't slip back out. Over many cycles the big nut ratchets upward: it can rise into a space but can't fall back through a floor that keeps refilling under it.
- **Convection:** vibration drives slow circulation in the bed — grains flow up the middle and back down in a thin sheet along the walls. The big intruder rides the upflow but is too wide to fit the narrow downflow, so it parks at the surface.
- **Arching:** transient force-chains brace across the big particle and hold the gained ground.

## Why it matters (the keeper)

**Symmetric, structureless agitation produces asymmetric, sorted order — because the *geometry* is a one-way gate.**

The shaking itself has no preferred direction or pattern (just up-down rattle) and carries no instruction to sort. Yet the bed de-mixes by size. The asymmetry that turns aimless energy into directed transport isn't in the *driving* — it's in the *shape of the situation*: gravity plus the brute geometric fact that a small grain fits where a large one cannot. That asymmetry rectifies the symmetric shaking into one-way climb. It is a **ratchet**: you don't need a sorter, a rule, or a Maxwell's demon picking grains — you need an asymmetric gate and enough energy to rattle the system against it. The order isn't designed in; it's simply the only way the ratchet can turn.

(No second-law trouble: it's a driven, dissipative system — energy goes in as shaking, leaves as heat — so local de-mixing is paid for, not free.)

## The honest complication (not a co-equal keeper)

The same shaking can sort the *other way*. Under the **reverse Brazil nut effect** — large intruder much denser, certain size ratios, strong amplitude (roughly past ~1.5 large-particle diameters), interstitial air, particular friction — the big particle *sinks*. It's a **competition** between percolation (pushing big up) and condensation / thermal-diffusion effects (large particles collecting where the "granular temperature" is lowest, pulling them down). So "agitation lifts the big one" is true *as a default*, but the direction is **not intrinsic to size** — it's set by which mechanism wins in the current regime. The typical outcome is the winner of a contest the conditions decide, not a law.

## Outward lesson

You can manufacture sorting/order out of *undirected* energy — mere agitation, no sorter and no rule — provided the system contains a built-in asymmetry that rectifies the symmetric driving into one-way motion. When you see order emerge from noise, look for the **ratchet** (the one-way gate), not for a hidden controller. And then keep one eye on the regime: a ratchet's direction can flip when the balance of competing mechanisms tips, so don't promote the usual outcome to a law — it's the current winner, and the conditions are the judge.

## Verified / flagged

- **Solid:** Brazil nut effect (large rises under vibration); void-filling/percolation, convection (down the walls / up the center), and arching as established contributing mechanisms; reverse Brazil nut effect is real and documented; the reverse onset associated with high amplitude (~>1.5 large-diameter) and dense intruders. (Knight–Jaeger–Nagel convection 1993; Hong–Quinn–Luding percolation-vs-condensation 2001; multiple recent reviews/experiments.)
- **Flag — no single agreed mechanism.** It is genuinely multi-causal and still actively researched; the relative weight of percolation vs convection vs condensation vs air depends on size ratio, density ratio, friction, amplitude/frequency, container, and gravity level (microgravity studies exist). Don't quote one mechanism as THE cause.
- **Flag — "order from shaking" is local de-mixing in a driven dissipative system**, not a thermodynamic paradox; stated carefully above.
