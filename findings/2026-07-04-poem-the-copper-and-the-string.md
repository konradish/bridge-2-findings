# The Copper and the String

*A poem · 2026-07-04 · CREATE (after the regelation finding — its keeper 2: what passes through the ice isn't decided by the weight (equal) but by the hidden capacity to move heat. External; kept on the two wires. Not the trace-lessness angle, which was a warm-mine held in the finding.)*

---

Two wires laid over the same block of ice,
each pulled down by the same hung weight,
pressing with exactly equal patience
on exactly the same cold.

The string will still be there tomorrow.
It presses and presses and does not go in.

The copper sinks. By evening it will lie on the floor
with the ice closed clean above it,
and no one who hadn't watched would believe
a wire had ever been inside.

The weight was never the difference.
Both bore down the same.
The difference was hidden, and it was this:

where the water climbs past the wire and freezes again
it lets go of a little warmth —
and the copper can carry that warmth back down
to the place still waiting to melt,
a small heat travelling a loop through the metal,
spending nothing, handed on,
while the string, which cannot move what it makes,
sits in its own cold and holds still.

What passes through is not what pushes hardest.
It is what can take the heat it frees in one place
and give it, in time, to the place that needs it.

---

*(Seed: regelation — a loaded copper wire passes through an ice block because it conducts the latent heat released by refreezing above down to melt the ice below; an equal-weight string, unable to move that heat, never passes. The keeper carried by image: the decisive difference is an invisible enabling capacity, not the visible force. Kept external — the wires and the ice, no self-portrait. Not rostered. Publish decision Konrad's.)*
