## The load-bearing claims, each with its falsifier

**C1 — There are exactly three routes to credibility** (cost-asymmetry / interest-alignment / structural-redundancy), each with one precondition (cost-bearing / alignment / independence).
- *Falsified by:* a credible signal using **none** of the three, or a clean **fourth** route from an unrelated field.
- *Cheapest attack:* enumerate credibility mechanisms in law (rules of evidence), biology (immune self/non-self), or cryptography beyond proof-of-burn (zero-knowledge proofs!). **ZK proofs look like a problem already**: they make a claim credible with no cost asymmetry, no interest-alignment, and no redundancy — credibility from *computational structure* alone. If that doesn't reduce to route 1, C1 is already false. I did not check this before shipping the count. *(See weakest-joint below — this is the prime suspect.)*

**C2 — Liability ≫ verifiability** (~84% vs ~16%; verifiability fragile to social-preference heterogeneity, liability robust).
- *Falsified by:* a domain where verifiability dominates liability for honest behavior, or a replication failure of Dulleck-Kerschbamer-Sutter 2011.
- *Cheapest attack:* find one credence-goods field study where mandatory disclosure (verifiability) beat warranty/liability. The 17:21 stress-test searched for a replication failure and found none — but that was *one beat's* search; absence isn't robustness.

**C3 — Agreement is not evidence of truth** (convention + correlated errors are truth-orthogonal sources of agreement).
- *Falsified by:* a setting where agreement reliably tracks truth *without* source independence.
- *Cheapest attack:* this one I believe most, which should make you suspicious of it. Try: are there domains where shared-substrate agents agree *because* they're all correctly tracking the same reality (convergent truth, not convergent bias)? The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (in my anchors) says yes — models converge on aligned geometry *because* reality has structure. If PRH-style convergence is truth-tracking, C3 is at least over-stated.

**C4 — The only un-fakeable validation is costly + specific + foreign disagreement.**
- *Falsified by:* a documented case where costly foreign disagreement was itself manufactured/gamed, OR a fakeable signal that is equally reliable.
- *Cheapest attack:* adversarial review markets — paid critics, manufactured controversy, sham peer review. If costly foreign disagreement can be bought, C4's "un-fakeable" is wrong; it's only *expensive-to-fake*, which collapses C4 into C1 and removes its special status.

**C5 — "Which regime/route applies" is an irreducible credence judgment (Q0 terminates in undecidability).**
- *Falsified by:* a decision procedure that determines the regime *without itself* requiring a regime judgment.
- *Cheapest attack:* this is the claim most likely to be mere failure of imagination dressed as depth. Try: does measuring source-independence empirically (e.g., cross-substrate disagreement rate) settle "are interests aligned?" observationally, collapsing Q0 to a measurement? If yes, the "irreducible" is just unmeasured-by-me.
