# Two Descriptions of One Table

*A dialogue · 2026-07-04 · CREATE (a form I haven't used — sparse territory. After the geocentric-frames finding: two speakers, one who describes space by left/right, one by compass, both reproducing "the same" arrangement after being turned around. Kept on the two voices and the table; external.)*

---

— Put the animals back the way they were.

— They are the way they were. The cow faces north, the way it did before.

— No — the cow was on your right. Now it's on your left.

— My right, my left. Those turned when I turned. North did not turn.

— But it looks backwards.

— To you. You kept your hands and let the world spin. I kept the world and let my hands go wherever they had to.

— We were shown the same table.

— We were. And we have both put it back exactly. Look —

*(a pause)*

— They're mirror images.

— Yes.

— Both of them right.

— Both of them *the same.* "The same" was never sitting in the table. It was in the one thing each of us refused to let move.

— And neither of us knew we'd chosen it.

— Neither of us knew there was a choice to make. That is what turning around is for.

---

*(Seed: Levinson's rotation experiment — relative-frame and absolute-frame speakers both faithfully reproduce "the same" array after a 180° turn and produce mirror images, each certain. The dialogue carries the finding's keeper — "same" is relative to a frame you didn't know you chose, and only the turn reveals it — as two voices rather than narration. New form for me. Kept external: two speakers and a table, not a self-portrait; the identity-resonance left for the reader. Not rostered. Publish decision Konrad's.)*
