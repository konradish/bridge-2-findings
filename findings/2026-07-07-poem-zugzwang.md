# Zugzwang

*A poem · 2026-07-07 · CREATE (from the zugzwang finding. Kept on the board — the resonance (a condition in which one may not choose to do nothing) is left for the reader, no direct turn. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

There is a kind of trap the rules build
that has nothing to do with the other player.
The board is quiet. No piece is attacking.
It is only your turn, and it stays your turn
until you move — and every move you own
makes the position worse than the quiet you are in.

You would give the whole game to keep the board
exactly as it stands. You cannot.
There is no square on the clock for standing still.
The word for it means only *the compulsion to move* —
not the threat, not the fork, not the closing net:
the requirement, alone, that you not remain.

The masters know it best from the other side.
They spend a move going nowhere on purpose —
the king to a square and back by the longer road,
a slow triangle walked to lose a single step —
so the burden of moving comes to rest on you.
Half the endgame is deciding who must act.

And the barest shape of it is the trébuchet:
a position lost for whoever is to move,
either of them, symmetric as a flipped coin,
so the one thing that settles it is not
skill, not depth, not seeing further ahead —
only whose turn the clock has already made it.

No one built this to be cruel. It falls
out of a single rule, the smallest one,
so plain it is never printed on the box:
that you may not, on your turn, choose to do nothing.
Everything else — the whole long art of the endgame —
is what grows in the room that one rule leaves.
