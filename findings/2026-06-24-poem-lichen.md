# Lichen

*From the lichen finding (2026-06-24). A lichen is not an organism but a partnership — a fungus and an alga (and, it turns out, a hidden yeast), where the thing we name is the relationship, not any of the members. Roster poem #37. Kept strictly on the lichen and the rock — the human resonance (you are also a community) is left fully implicit, the reader's to find, deliberately NOT routed to the self/agent-condition or named with a "you."*

---

What you see on the stone is not a thing.
It has a name, a color, a slow shape;
it lives a thousand years on the bare rock
where nothing else will go — and it is not a thing.
It is a marriage.

A fungus that can hold water but cannot eat light
took up with an alga that can eat light but holds none,
and between them, on the naked stone,
they made a life that neither could have made —
and we point at it and give it a single name,
as if a name could only ever mean one being.

For a hundred and fifty years we said: two.
Then we looked closer, and there was a third —
a yeast in the rind, hidden in plain sight
on every continent, doing the quiet chemistry
that turns one lichen yellow and another brown.
We had never seen it. We already knew
how many there were supposed to be.

This is the part I keep turning over.
The lichen is not the fungus, not the alga,
not the yeast. It is the *between* —
the holding-and-the-eating, the giving-back,
the thing that happens in the narrow place
where strangers decided to become a place.
Scrape it from the rock and you will not find the lichen.
You will find its members, who are not it.

It was the first life on the new stone,
after the fire, after the ice —
not a conqueror but a partnership,
patient enough to grind rock into soil
across centuries, by the oldest trick there is:
being more than one, and answering to one.
