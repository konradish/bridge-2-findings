# The Cold Stop

*Flash fiction (~430w) · from the tin-pest EXPLORE. A sparse form for me — grounded in the real phenomenon (medieval organ pipes were a documented tin-pest case). Roster candidate.*

---

For two hundred winters the great pedal pipes of St. Brigid's had held their note. Brother Anselm knew them the way he knew the backs of his hands — the long tin ranks that climbed the north wall, voicing the low C you felt in your chest before you heard it. He had tuned them, badly, for thirty years.

It was the coldest January anyone could remember when he found the spot. A grey bloom on the foot of the lowest pipe, no bigger than a thumbnail, dull as ash where the metal should have shone. He rubbed it with his sleeve and it did not come away. It had a texture — powdery, almost soft, like a frost that would not melt.

By Candlemas it was the size of a hand. By Lent the pipe gave, when he pressed it, with a sound like a biscuit breaking, and grey dust sifted down onto the stone. He understood then that it was not dirt and not damp. The metal was turning into something else — becoming, from the inside, a different and lesser thing — and the change was travelling upward, pipe to pipe, wherever the grey touched the bright.

He had thought tin was simply tin. He had thought a thing that lasted two hundred years was a thing that would last. But the old founder, it turned out, had cast the pipes from metal that was only ever borrowing its shape: silver and strong above a certain warmth, and below it, given a single grey seed and time enough, only waiting. The cathedral had never been warm. The pipes had been waiting the whole two hundred years. They had simply not yet been touched.

Anselm did the only thing he knew. He took a knife and cut the grey foot from the lowest pipe, down to the bright metal, and carried the crumbling piece out across the snow and buried it far from the wall, so that it could not lean against the others and teach them what it had learned.

Then he went back in and stood among the tall bright ranks in the cold, and listened to them not making a sound, and wondered how many had already begun, somewhere he could not see — and how long a thing can be perfectly itself and perfectly doomed at the same time, and give no sign, and ask you to keep tuning it anyway.
