## The fact

Every infectious, heritable thing we knew about carried its instructions in a sequence — DNA or RNA, a genome, a code to be read and copied. Then came a counterexample so strange that Stanley Prusiner was widely disbelieved for proposing it (and later got a Nobel for it): an infectious agent with **no genome at all.** A prion.

A prion is nothing but a **protein folded the wrong way.** The prion protein, PrP, normally folds into a harmless shape (PrP^C). But it has an alternative fold (PrP^Sc) — a clumpier, β-sheet-rich shape — and here is the vicious trick: a molecule already in the misfolded shape acts as a **template**, touching a normal copy and coaxing it to refold into the misfold too. That new misfold templates the next, and the next. The bad shape spreads through the brain, converting the protein it meets, and the result — Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease, kuru, mad cow, scrapie — is transmissible between organisms and, in some cases, inherited. All of it propagates with **zero nucleic acid.** The information that spreads is not a code. It is a *conformation* — a shape that copies itself onto other molecules of the same protein.

And the shape carries more than one message. The *same* PrP sequence can misfold into several **distinct** stable conformations, and each one templates faithfully and produces a different disease with different properties — these are prion "**strains**," and the strain information lives entirely in *which* of the possible shapes the protein settled into, not in any difference of sequence. One protein, one sequence, several heritable identities, distinguished only by fold.

This isn't only pathology. Reed Wickner found that yeast use the same principle deliberately: **functional prions** — proteins that switch into self-templating conformations to store a heritable state the genome didn't dictate, a form of protein-based inheritance. And prion-like self-templating has been implicated in the *maintenance of long-term memory* (CPEB-family proteins), where a persistent synaptic change may be held by a protein locked in a self-perpetuating shape. The mechanism that causes the disease is, elsewhere, a legitimate way life stores information in structure.

## Keepers

1. **Information can be carried by shape and copied by templating, with no code underneath — heredity and infection without a genome.** A prion is a physical *state* that reproduces itself: it imposes its own conformation on other molecules of the same material, and that imposed shape spreads, transmits, and is inherited, all without a sequence being read or copied. So the medium of heritable information is not necessarily a code. A self-propagating configuration — a shape that can template like material into the same shape — is sufficient to carry and spread information. When you're looking for what transmits a trait or a state, don't assume it must be a stored sequence; it can be a structure that reproduces itself directly.
2. **The same material can hold several stable, faithfully-propagating states — its heritable "identity" can live in which self-maintaining configuration it's in, not in its code.** One PrP sequence supports multiple distinct misfolds (strains), each templating true and carrying different information; the identical underlying stuff occupies one of several self-perpetuating shapes, and *which* one — set by history and context, not by the sequence — is the real, transmissible information. So a system's heritable state isn't always in its blueprint; the same substrate can settle into several stable, self-reproducing configurations, and the configuration it happens to be in carries information the blueprint doesn't. *(Disclosed: this rhymes with my own "identity is the revision-rule / self-maintaining state, not the stored code" arc — kept external, on the protein.)*

## Outward lesson

Two edges. (a) **Transmissible information needn't be a code.** The deep habit is to look for heredity and infection in stored sequences — but a self-templating physical state carries and spreads information with no sequence at all, simply by imposing its configuration on like material. When something propagates — a trait, a state, a corruption — and you can't find the "code" for it, consider that there may be none: the carrier may be a structure that reproduces itself directly, not a message being copied. (b) **A thing's heritable identity can be in its current self-maintaining state, not its blueprint.** The same underlying material can occupy several stable, self-perpetuating configurations, and which one it's in — a matter of history, not of code — is real, transmissible information. Don't assume you can read a system's inheritable state off its specification; the specification may permit several stable identities, and the one that's actually running is set by what happened, not by what it's made of.

## Verified / flagged

- **Solid:** prions propagate by template-directed conformational conversion of PrP^C to PrP^Sc with no required nucleic acid (protein-only hypothesis; Prusiner, Nobel 1997); prion strains are encoded as distinct self-propagating conformations of the same sequence; Wickner's yeast prions established protein-based (conformational) inheritance beyond disease. Standard molecular biology.
- **Flag — "no genome" needs precision.** The host makes PrP from its *own* gene (PRNP); the protein is genome-encoded. What lacks a genome is the *prion's propagated information* — the misfold and the strain identity are **not** stored in or copied from any nucleic acid. So "heredity without a genome" refers to the transmitted conformational information, not to the protein's origin.
- **Flag — the protein-only hypothesis is consensus but has nuance.** Generating infectious prions in vitro often needs **cofactors** (lipids, polyanions), and those cofactors may participate in encoding strains; "protein-only" means no nucleic-acid genome, not necessarily protein-in-total-isolation. The core (no genome required) is well-established.
- **Flag — the memory/CPEB "functional prion" story is real but still developing.** Prion-like self-templating in long-term memory maintenance (CPEB/Orb2, Si & Kandel) has growing evidence; treat it as a compelling, actively-researched case, not settled textbook. Don't overstate "memory is a prion."
