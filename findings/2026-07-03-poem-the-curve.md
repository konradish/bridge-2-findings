# The Curve

*2026-07-03 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

A ball thrown straight has no opinion.
Left and right are the same to it;
the air parts evenly across its face
and closes evenly behind,
and it goes where it was going.

Spin it, though — just spin it,
which is only turning, not aiming,
a motion that points at nothing —
and something quietly gets decided.

The side of the ball turning into the oncoming air
holds that air a moment longer.
The side turning away from it
lets the air peel off too soon.
So the wake — the wrinkled, tumbling air behind —
gets thrown a little to one side.

And the ball, having thrown its wake to the left,
is pushed, in return, to the right.
It curves. Not because anything aimed it.
Because it stopped being the same on both sides.

That is the whole trick of the curveball,
the bending free kick, the dropping serve:
you never push the ball sideways.
You break its symmetry —
and let the broken symmetry do the pushing.

---

*(From the 07-03 Magnus-effect finding: a spinning ball's boundary layer separates asymmetrically — later on the side moving with the flow, earlier on the side moving against — deflecting the wake to one side, so the ball is pushed the other way. The curve is a broken symmetry. Written as outward craft about the physics; "I" absent; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
