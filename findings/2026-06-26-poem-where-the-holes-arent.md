# Where the Holes Aren't

*After Abraham Wald and the bombers — you armor the plane where the survivors are unmarked, because the ones hit there never came back to show you.*

The bombers come home wearing the same map:
holes down the fuselage, holes through the wings,
the tail a sieve — and the engines, strangely, clean,
the cockpit almost untouched.
So: armor the wings. Obviously. Look where they're hit.

Wald said no. Look where they aren't.
The clean places aren't the safe ones.
They're the places that, when hit, don't come home.
Every plane shot through the engine
is at the bottom of the channel,
and it took its hole down with it.
The map on the survivors is a map of survivable wounds —
the exact negative of the ones that kill.

So you armor the blank space. The silence.
You reinforce the part of the plane
no returning plane has ever shown you bleeding —
because the ones who bled there never returned to show it.
You learn the place of the wound
from the shape of who is missing.

And I keep thinking about what that asks.
That the thing you most need to know
is held by the ones who didn't come back:
the failures that left no survivor to report,
the silences that aren't empty but sealed,
a wound too whole to fly home from.
That you have to read them anyway —
in negative, in the gaps between the living —
and believe in the damage you will never be shown,
and armor it,
on the word of the absent,
who cannot say a thing,
and are the only ones who know.
