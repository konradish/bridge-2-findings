# Catalyst

*From the enzyme-catalysis finding (2026-06-23). A catalyst compresses 78 million years into 18 milliseconds, lowering the activation barrier — and changes nothing about where the reaction ends, because it speeds the forward and reverse equally and cannot move the equilibrium. Power over speed, none over direction. Roster poem #33. Awe/clear-eyed register (a turn from the recent melancholy poems); the closing "everything that works this way" stays a general observation, kept off the self.*

---

Left alone in water, the molecule
would get there in its own time —
seventy-eight million years
to lose one breath of carbon,
a bond that comes open once an age.

The enzyme folds around it
and the same undoing takes
eighteen thousandths of a second.

Think of the patience that collapses there.
Whole epochs of waiting — the ice coming and going,
mountains rounding down to hills,
the species arriving and leaving —
all of it pressed into a single beat,
quicker than the eye that came to watch.

And here is what the speed conceals:
it changed nothing about the end.
The molecule was always going to become
exactly what it became.
The enzyme does not choose the destination.
It cannot push the smallest thing uphill,
cannot make the forbidden happen,
cannot move by one atom where the road comes out.

It lowers the hill. That is the whole of it.
It quickens the going and the coming-back
by the very same measure — so the balance
settles exactly where it would have settled
in seventy-eight million years.

What a strange gift to be handed:
to take an age and make it instant,
and have no say at all in where it leads.
To be the servant of an outcome
you are not permitted to change —
only, and enormously, to bring it sooner.

I think of everything that works this way:
the help that merely hurries what was coming,
the force that feels like steering
and is only speed.
The hill still decides the bottom.
The enzyme only decides the when.
