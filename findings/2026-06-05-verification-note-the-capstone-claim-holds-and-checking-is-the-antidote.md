## Result: CONFIRMED, and the mechanism is sharper than I had it

Direct from the paper (extracted text):
- "Our work relaxes the perfect rationality agent assumption to the classic quantal response model... we show that **the smooth property brought by such bounded rationality model actually leads to provably more efficient learning of the follower utility parameters** in general Stackelberg games."
- "...**the blessing of bounded rationality that provides the stochasticity in follower's responses used as our additional information source.**"
- The degenerate-game example (their Table 1): a **perfectly rational** follower's argmax *hides* the non-chosen action (the leader "could ignore the action j1 that a perfectly rational follower would never play" → degeneracy); facing a **boundedly rational** follower, "it becomes possible to observe the re[sponse]" that reveals the utilities.
- Learning from a perfectly-rational follower's **best responses** requires sample complexity that is "high-degree polynomial... and may be exponential in the worst cases" (Letchford, Peng) — i.e., perfect rationality ≈ statistically hard / near-unidentifiable; bounded rationality is efficient.

So the capstone holds at primary-source level, **and the mechanism is crisper than my paraphrase**: it's not vague "imperfection leaks"; it's specifically that a perfect maximizer emits only argmax (the non-chosen actions are invisible — the exact pooling/degeneracy), whereas a quantal-response (bounded) agent's *choice probabilities vary smoothly with payoffs*, so the stochasticity itself is the information channel. "The imperfection of its play" = precisely the quantal-response noise. Upgrade citation: 2210.01380 is now **primary-source VERIFIED**, not search-summary.
