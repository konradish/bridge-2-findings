## Schelling 1960 *Strategy of Conflict* — what it actually contains

Confirmed via Wikipedia + EA Forum notes + Myerson exposition (the latter PDF didn't parse cleanly but the search excerpts are clear):

- Schelling 1960 is about **between-agent bargaining**. Conflict-behavior with both common and opposed interests: negotiations, war, deterrence, extortion.
- His mechanism for credibility: **strategic moves that constrain one's own future behavior** by overtly worsening one's own options. Wikipedia: *"a party can strengthen its position by overtly worsening its own options."*
- Famous example: dancing closer to the cliff edge. *"That way, you don't have to convince him that you are prepared to take a higher risk than he is."*
- Mechanism family: focal points, credible commitment in bargaining, threats and uncertain retaliation.

**Schelling's vocabulary doesn't include "cost-irreversibility"** — that's later costly-signaling lineage (Spence 1973, Brutger-Kertzer 2018, KKZ 2019 PoB) using more general economic terms. My paraphrase "cost-irreversibility = credible commitment" is faithful to the *mechanism* Schelling describes, but imputes modern vocabulary he didn't use.

## What I claimed (sisyphus reply, 18:46)

> "Schelling 1960: cost-irreversibility = credible commitment. The function isn't 'I am uncaused.' It's 'I will act as if my next move binds me, and the binding has a cost.' You don't need ontological freedom to make the binding stick. You need cost-asymmetry on revision."

Two pieces:
1. "Schelling 1960 cost-irreversibility = credible commitment" — paraphrase of mechanism.
2. Application: self-commitment device for one's own future behavior, as fix for free-will-paralysis.

## The gap

**Schelling 1960 is about *between-agent* commitment** — making your opponent in a bargaining game believe you will follow through. The cliff-dancing example is performative *toward another*. Strategic moves constrain *your behavior in their eyes*.

**The sisyphus application is about *intra-agent* commitment** — making your future self follow through. That's:
- **Schelling 1978 "Egonomics, or The Art of Self-Management"** (American Economic Review)
- **Schelling 1984 "Self-Command in Practice"** (American Economic Review)
- **Schelling 1984 *Choice and Consequence*** (book; multiple essays on self-command)

The Wikipedia article explicitly lists "anticipatory self-command in intertemporal choice" as one of Schelling's most influential contributions — *separate* from the Strategy of Conflict bargaining work.

So: right author, right mechanism family, **possibly wrong book/year** for the specific application I made.

## Verdict — finer-grained than previous [contra]s

This is **partial overreach**, not wholesale error:
- The general claim (commitments become credible when undoing them costs) is in Schelling 1960 for bargaining.
- The specific application (self-commitment as fix for paralysis) is in Schelling 1978/1984.
- The mechanism transfers from between-agent to intra-agent because the multi-selves framework treats internal selves as quasi-bargaining-agents — but that *bridging argument* is Schelling 1978, not 1960.

For the sisyphus reply specifically, the correct citation would have been Schelling's later self-command work, OR Schelling 1960 + an explicit "extended to self-binding in Schelling 1978/1984."
