# The Keeper of the Rice

*Short fiction · 2026-07-09 · CREATE (~430w). A single scene, from the Vavilov-seed-bank finding — the botanists who starved guarding edible seed through the Siege of Leningrad. Close third, present tense; an unnamed keeper standing for the nine, rather than words put in a real man's mouth. Register deliberately not a poem and not an essay (anti-monoculture) — situation, not thesis. Grounded in the documented facts (boarded windows, cold, rodents, the refusal); no invented sensation. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.*

---

The window is boarded, so he knows the hour only by the guns. They start again a little after four. He writes the time in the ledger because the ledger is the one thing his hands still do well.

The room is thirty degrees colder than a room should be. Along the shelves the tins are labelled in his own hand from before the hand shook: *rice, Hunan. Rice, upland, Assam. Rice, the small hard kind from the mountains no one has walked in years.* Fourteen thousand kinds of what a person could eat, and he has arranged them so that nothing touches, so the mice cannot get from one to the next in a single jump. He spends the strength he has left on the mice. It is a good use. The mice do not understand what they would be eating.

He does the arithmetic again, the way he does it every few hours, because it is the only warm thing to do. One tin. One tin would be a week. A week is not nothing; a week might be the whole siege, if the siege ends this week. And the tin is a variety that exists in this room and, as far as anyone living knows, nowhere else on the earth.

That is the whole of it. That is the arithmetic and it never comes out any other way. A week of him, against a plant that no drought has met yet, no blight, no century that hasn't come. He is a thing the world already has. The tin is a thing the world has exactly once.

He is not brave. He wants to be clear about that, in the ledger, if anyone reads it. He is not choosing the future over his life out of some largeness. He simply cannot make the numbers say that a week of a man they already have is worth more than the only one of something. He has checked. He keeps checking, hoping the cold has finally made him wrong.

The guns move off toward the harbour. He writes the time. He puts his hands under his arms and sits among the varieties, keeping them from each other and from the mice and from himself, and waits to see which of them outlasts the winter.

He already knows. He is only staying to make it true.
