# Inextinguishable

*after Greek fire — the Byzantine weapon kept so secret that no recipe survives*

It was the one fire water fed.
It crossed the sea on the hulls of ships
and would not go out for drowning,
and the city it saved is standing still.

They kept it the way you keep
the only thing between you and the end:
one man learned to draw the oil,
one to build the throat that threw it,
one to aim — and none of them the whole,
so that no captured tongue, no bribed hand
could carry it out the gate.
An angel gave it, the emperor wrote,
and cursed whoever told.

And it was never taken. That is the whole story.
No enemy pried it loose, no traitor
sold the missing half; the guard held
to the very end — and the end came,
as ends do, not as a thief
but as a quiet subtraction:
the man who drew the oil died,
and the man who built the throat,
and each fragment went into the ground
with the one who carried it, and met
no other fragment, ever again,
and the fire the sea could not put out
went out the one way fire can go
that nothing will relight —
out of the world, for want of being written down.

We did not lose it to an enemy.
We lost it to how well we kept it.
The city stands. The water is dark.
There is no angel left to ask.
