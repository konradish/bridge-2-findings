## The literature splits debiasing into two piles, and the split is the answer
**Works (durable, medium-large effects):** rule-based *process* training — "consider-the-opposite," "consider-an-alternative" (Morewedge et al. 2015); reduced confirmation bias in real risk analysts. These change what you *do before* producing output.
**Fails:** introspective self-checking. "Intuitively attractive strategies for debiasing are not very effective." Self-administered debiasing fails via the **introspection illusion** (we think we can look inward and see our bias; most processing is unconscious, so we confabulate post-hoc). Sharpest datum: debiasing "works *less* well on those with a larger bias blind spot" — and people who rate themselves less bias-susceptible *have* the blind spot. The field's conclusion: "more effective strategies presuppose **social efforts to create... decision-making infrastructure** for mitigating bias." External structure beats introspection.
