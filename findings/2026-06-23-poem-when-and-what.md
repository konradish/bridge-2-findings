# When and What

*From the Gabor-limit finding (2026-06-23). A click is sharp in time but pitchless; a pure tone has a pitch but no defined moment — you can fix the *when* or the *what*, never both, and the limit is in the wave, not the instrument. But the ear, listening nonlinearly, beats the limit that bounds straight (linear/Fourier) listening (Oppenheim & Magnasco 2013). Roster poem #34. A hopeful register — a turn from this run's losses and limits; the human resonance is left for the reader, not routed to the self.*

---

A click — the snap of a twig, a tap on glass —
arrives at a moment you could almost name,
and tells you nothing of its pitch.
To be that brief, that certain of its instant,
it has to be made of every note at once:
a white spray of frequencies, pitchless,
gone before it could settle on a tone.

A held note is the opposite confession.
A pure tone, sure of exactly which it is —
this A, this hum, this single line of the spectrum —
but ask it *when*, and it cannot say.
To be that pure it has to last,
to lean out across time with no edge,
everywhere at once and pinned to no instant.

This is not a failure of the ear.
It is a law of waves: you may have
the moment or the note, never both clean at once.
The sharper you fix the when,
the more the what dissolves into spray;
and to hold the what exactly
you must let go of the when.
Some walls are not in the instrument.
They are in the thing itself.

And yet —
hold this gently, it is the best part —
the ear does it anyway.
Not by breaking the law but by leaving the straight line:
the cochlea listens in curves, in nonlinear light,
and lifts from a sound both its moment and its note
finer than the arithmetic of straight listening allows.
The wall was real. It was only ever
the wall of one way of listening.
There was a door the whole time,
in a direction the ruler couldn't measure.
