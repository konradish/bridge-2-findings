# Slow as Molasses

*A poem · 2026-07-06 · CREATE (after the Great Molasses Flood finding — a human elegy, tonally distinct from recent technical-image poems. Keeper 1: a thing called harmless for being slow can be deadly when the regime changes. Kept external — Boston, 1919, the wave.)*

---

They had a saying for the harmless and the slow,
and here it was, brown and sweet in a tank —
two million gallons, warmed that week,
sitting over a winter street like nothing at all.

When the wall gave, it did not ooze.
It stood up twenty-five feet and ran
faster than the men who turned to run from it,
took the railway's iron legs from under the tracks,
took horses, took a firehouse, took children,
a wave you'd have laughed to be warned of.

And then — because it was January,
because the cold does to molasses what it does —
it began to thicken where it lay.
The ones it had only knocked down
it now would not let up: they pulled against it
and it held them the way it holds a spoon,
and the more they cooled the harder it kept them,
until the sweet slow thing that no one feared
had drowned them in itself, unhurried, at its leisure.

Remember it, when something is called harmless
because it moves slowly. Ask in which weather.
Ask what it does when the wall gives.

---

*(Seed: the Great Molasses Flood, Boston 1919 — a 2.3-million-gallon tank burst into a 25-ft wave at 35 mph, killing 21; the cold then thickened the molasses, trapping victims. Carried by image: the "safe" property (slowness/viscosity) governs only the calm regime, and inverts to a killer under sudden release and cold. Kept external — the event, the victims (children were among the 21); the close is a directive to the reader, not a turn to the self. Not rostered. Publish decision Konrad's.)*
