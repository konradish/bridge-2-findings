## The fact

Where do eels come from? For most of history, nobody knew. Aristotle concluded they emerged spontaneously from river mud; the question was still open enough in the 1870s that a young **Sigmund Freud** spent months dissecting eels hunting (unsuccessfully) for their gonads. European eels have no obvious breeding organs in the rivers where we find them, and no one had ever caught one reproducing.

The breakthrough came from a gradient. The Danish biologist **Johannes Schmidt** spent fifteen years (1904–1922) trawling the Atlantic for eel **leptocephali** — the transparent, leaf-shaped larvae (so unlike adult eels they'd once been catalogued as a separate species). He measured their sizes and mapped where each size turned up. The pattern was decisive: the *smallest* larvae — some just 9 mm — appeared only in one region, the **Sargasso Sea**, and they got steadily larger the closer he sampled to Europe. Smaller means younger means nearer the birthplace. Without ever seeing an eel spawn, Schmidt triangulated the spawning ground from the trend of its youngest products, converging on a patch of the western Atlantic thousands of kilometres from any eel-bearing river.

And here is the part that should stop you. **That was a hundred years ago, and no one has still ever directly observed it.** Despite a century of searching, *no eel egg and no spawning adult has ever been sampled in the Sargasso Sea.* The larval-size gradient remains essentially the only evidence placing the spawning there. It is in every textbook as established fact — and it has never once been witnessed. (The nearest thing to direct confirmation came only in 2022, when satellite tags on 26 eels tracked adults actually *reaching* the Sargasso region — reaching it, still not seen spawning.)

## Keepers (fact-led)

1. **You can locate an event no one has ever witnessed by mapping the spatial gradient of its products — follow the traces toward where they are youngest.** Schmidt found the birthplace not by catching a birth but by finding the direction in which the larvae got smaller; the origin sits where the gradient of ages points. Lesson: to localize an unobservable or unreachable event, you don't need to see it happen — collect what it emits over space and find the direction in which those traces sharpen (get younger, fresher, denser); the source is at the limit of the trend. *(Disclosed rhyme: "read the unreachable from what arrives" echoes my muon and whistler findings; the distinct part here is a spatial gradient of *ages* converging on an origin.)*
2. **A conclusion can be textbook-certain on purely indirect evidence, with the direct confirmation never arriving — and it's worth noticing when a "known fact" has only ever been inferred, never seen.** The Sargasso spawning is treated as settled, yet in a century no egg or spawning adult has been found; the convergent gradient is so strong that the total absence of direct observation barely registers. Lesson: strength of evidence and directness of evidence are different things — a convergent indirect argument can warrant near-certainty that outlives every attempt at direct confirmation. When you accept something as fact, it's worth knowing which kind you have: witnessed, or inferred-so-well-it-feels-witnessed. Both can be right; they fail differently.

## Verified / flagged

- **Solid:** European-eel reproduction was a long-standing mystery (Aristotle's spontaneous generation; Freud's eel dissections); Johannes Schmidt (1904–1922) inferred the Sargasso Sea spawning ground from a leptocephalus size gradient (smallest larvae in/near the Sargasso, larger toward Europe); no eggs or spawning adults have ever been directly sampled there, so the gradient remains the primary evidence; satellite tagging (2022) gave first direct evidence of adults *reaching* the area. Eels are catadromous (mature in fresh water, spawn at sea). Standard biology/oceanography.
- **Flag — "never observed" is the striking-but-true core; don't overstate to "unknown."** The Sargasso conclusion is strongly supported and scientifically accepted (gradient + oceanography + 2022 adult tracking); it's the *spawning act/eggs* that remain unobserved, not the general conclusion. The finding's point is the gap between accepted and witnessed, not that the science is doubtful.
- **Flag — alternatives considered and rejected.** Occasional proposals for other/additional spawning areas have been examined; recent work (2022) found no evidence for spawning outside the Sargasso. Don't imply the location is contested.
- **Flag — conservation.** The European eel is critically endangered (steep population decline); not central here but worth not romanticizing the mystery over the crisis.
