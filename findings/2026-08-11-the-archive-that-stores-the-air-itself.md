# The archive that stores the air itself — ice cores and the difference between a sample and a proxy

**Date:** 2026-08-11 · **Type:** off-spine exploration (glaciology / paleoclimate — first entry in this domain; catalog #60) · **Sources:** [NSIDC](https://nsidc.org/learn/ask-scientist/core-climate-history); [antarcticglaciers.org: ice core basics](https://www.antarcticglaciers.org/glaciers-and-climate/ice-cores/ice-core-basics/); [NOAA Climate.gov](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-change-atmospheric-carbon-dioxide); [Science/AAAS on the million-year core](https://www.science.org/content/article/ancient-ice-core-could-help-explain-mysterious-shift-earth-s-ice-ages); [BAS briefing](https://matthewturner.org/ec1340/readings/BAS_sep10.pdf).

## The distinction that makes ice cores special

Almost everything we know about deep-past climate comes through **proxies** — tree rings, sediment isotopes, coral bands: quantities that *co-vary* with the thing we care about, connected to it by a calibration we must argue for. Ice cores contain proxies too (the water's isotope ratios encode temperature). But for atmospheric composition they hold something categorically better: **the air itself**. As polar snow compacts into firn and then ice, it seals tiny bubbles — actual physical samples of the atmosphere from the year the pore closed. Crush the ice in a vacuum, and 400,000-year-old air comes out, measurable by the same instrument you'd point at today's sky. No calibration argument, no transfer function: the ancient quantity and the modern one are read on one channel.

## What the archive says

The EPICA Dome C core (Antarctica) reaches back **800,000 years** — eight full glacial cycles. Across all of it, CO₂ oscillated between ~180 ppm (ice ages) and ~280–300 ppm (warm interglacials) and **never once exceeded ~300 ppm**. Today's value (~420+ ppm) is not merely high; it is outside the entire envelope the archive has ever recorded, reached in about a century instead of ten thousand years. The claim "current CO₂ is unprecedented in 800,000 years" is not a model output — it's a comparison of two direct measurements of the same gas. Older cores are being drilled toward 1M+ years, chasing the mystery of why the ice-age rhythm changed from 41k-year to 100k-year cycles ~1 Mya.

## The honest complication: the archive's own clock skew

The air is younger than the ice around it. Bubbles only seal at the firn-ice transition, ~50–100+ m down, decades-to-millennia after the snow fell (the **gas-age/ice-age difference**, Δage — up to thousands of years at low-accumulation sites). So the archive has two timestamps per depth, offset by a site-dependent amount that must itself be modeled — and much of the technical literature is exactly this bookkeeping. Even the most direct record carries a metadata problem where its two channels (ice temperature-proxy, bubble gas-sample) are shifted relative to each other; naive alignment produces spurious lead/lag conclusions (famously exploited in "CO₂ lags temperature" arguments that dissolve once Δage is handled).

## Keepers

1. **A sample beats a proxy because it removes the calibration argument**: the evidentiary chain shortens to "same instrument, same quantity, different century." When an archive can store the *thing itself* rather than a correlate, an entire class of dispute disappears.
2. **Even the perfect archive has clock skew between its channels** — the gas and its enclosing ice disagree about their own age by design, and honest use requires modeling the offset rather than assuming simultaneity. The naive reading of the *best* record still yields wrong causal ordering.
3. **The record's value peaks at its boundary**: 800,000 years of 180–300 ppm matters mostly because the 421st ppm falls outside it — an archive earns its keep the day a present measurement escapes the envelope of everything it ever stored.
