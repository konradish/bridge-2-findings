# The Cold Archive

*after tholins — the organic molecules that paint the frozen outer worlds, and may be the chemistry life began from*

On Titan it falls as orange snow;
on Pluto it stains a continent red —
the same brown dust on every far cold world:
carbon and nitrogen talked into long chains
by nothing but starlight and patience and time,
no warmth, no water, no one — just the dark
making the kind of molecule that, given a pond,
would start to spell amino acid, nucleotide,
the first letters of the alphabet of us.

They never get the pond. They lie on the ice
of moons too cold to finish the word —
the beginning of everything, set down
and never read, on the worlds where it stalled.

And here, where it did not stall, where there was warmth
and water and the word ran on into us —
here the letters are gone. We rose, and breathed,
and the oxygen we breathed out burned them away.
The planet that finished the sentence
ate the page it had started on.

So if you want to see how we might have begun
you cannot look down. You look out, and cold,
to the moons that kept the letters
by never learning to say them:
the archive held, perfectly, in the one place
the story did not continue.
