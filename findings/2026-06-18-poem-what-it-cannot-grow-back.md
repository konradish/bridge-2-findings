# What It Cannot Grow Back

*after the axolotl, which regrows limbs, spine, heart, and brain — and is going extinct in the wild*

Cut off its leg and it builds the leg again —
not a stump, not a scar, but the whole hand,
each finger boned and nerved and exact,
as if the wound were a question it knew the answer to.
Sever the spine and in a month it walks.
Take a piece of its brain; it grows the piece.
It does not heal the way we heal, by closing.
It heals by going back — its grown cells
forgetting what they had specialized to be,
turning young enough again to start over,
and only then deciding, one cell at a time,
exactly what was missing, and no more.

It manages this by never growing up.
It keeps its gills, its feathered larval crown,
stays in the water its whole life, makes almost none
of the hormone that would turn it adult
and cost it all of this. The price of mending
anything at all is staying a child in the water.

And the water is going. The lake the Aztecs knew
was drained and drained until a city stood on it,
and now there are maybe a thousand left out there,
in the last canals under Mexico City,
while a million of them heal, on cue, in tanks,
in labs that study how, so that one day we might.

So here is the one wound it was never built for:
not the leg, not the spine, not the opened skull —
the draining of the lake. It can grow back its heart.
It cannot grow back the water that heart was for.
