# Homochirality: why all life is left-handed — and why, in 2025, the mystery got deeper

## The fact, and why it's strange

Every living thing builds its proteins from **left-handed (L) amino acids** and its sugars/nucleic acids from **right-handed (D)** ones. (Glycine is the lone achiral exception.) The two handedness — like left and right gloves, mirror images that can't be superimposed — are chemically near-identical, yet life uses *only one* of each.

It has to. Enzymes, DNA's double helix, protein folding — all depend on consistent handedness. Splice an opposite-handed monomer into a chain and it kinks, mis-folds, fails to replicate. A racemic (50/50) world can't build life; you have to **pick one hand and commit.**

But here's the puzzle: **prebiotic chemistry produces racemic mixtures.** Spark-discharge experiments, meteoritic synthesis — all make L and D in equal amounts, because nothing in ordinary chemistry distinguishes a molecule from its mirror image. So the question splits in two:
1. **Symmetry-breaking** — where did the *first* tiny excess of one hand come from?
2. **Amplification** — how did a tiny excess snowball to ~100% and lock in?

## Candidate symmetry-breakers (where the first bias came from)

- **Parity violation (the weak force).** The weak nuclear force is the one truly *chiral* law of physics (Wu, 1957) — it gives L-amino acids a minuscule energy advantage over D, a deterministic universe-wide bias. Beautiful, because it would explain why L *everywhere*. But the energy difference is ~10⁻¹¹–10⁻¹⁷ — almost certainly far too small, and no one has found a prebiotic reaction that could amplify something that faint. Contested, probably insufficient.
- **Circularly polarized light (CPL).** In star-forming regions, circularly polarized UV can preferentially *destroy* one enantiomer. Lab CPL produces a few-percent excess; the Orion star-forming region shows extended high circular polarization. Could have biased the early solar nebula.
- **Meteorites (space seeding).** Carbonaceous chondrites carry a real, non-terrestrial **L-excess**: in Murchison, L-isovaline ~10–15% and L-glutamic acid ~16–40% (isovaline matters because it's non-biological and doesn't racemize, so the excess is primordial, not contamination). Suggested space delivered a head-start to Earth.
- **Chiral mineral surfaces** (e.g. quartz crystal faces) that adsorb one hand preferentially.

## Amplification — the Soai reaction

The one thing that's *solved*: **how a tiny excess becomes total.** Kenso Soai (1995) found the only clean example of spontaneous **asymmetric autocatalysis** — a chiral product that catalyzes its own formation *with the same handedness*. Feed it a near-racemic mix with an excess of <1% (even from a statistical fluctuation, a stray CPL exposure, anything), and it **snowballs to >99.5%** of one hand. It's the proof-of-principle that amplification works. The catch: the Soai chemistry (zinc/pyrimidine) isn't prebiotically plausible — the hunt for a *prebiotic* autocatalytic amplifier is still open.

## The 2020s frontier — CISS (chirality and magnetism are linked)

A genuinely new candidate. **Chirality-Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS):** electrons passing through a chiral molecule come out *spin-polarized*, with no magnet, at room temperature (Naaman, Waldeck, et al.). And it runs backward: a **magnetized surface becomes enantioselective** — it preferentially crystallizes one hand, because spin couples to handedness. That makes ordinary **magnetic minerals (magnetite), abundant on early Earth, a prebiotically plausible symmetry-breaker** — no weak force, no starlight needed. Caveat worth keeping: CISS is real but theoretically *unfinished* — models predict an effect orders of magnitude *smaller* than what's measured, so the mechanism works better than the theory explains.

## Why the mystery *deepened* in 2025

Two recent results pushed *against* the tidy "space/chemistry decided it" stories:
- **Pristine asteroid samples look racemic.** The cleanest, least-contaminated extraterrestrial material ever returned — Bennu (OSIRIS-REx) and Ryugu (Hayabusa2) — appears to *lack* the decisive L-excess Murchison showed, undercutting the idea that a Solar-System-wide bias seeded Earth and pointing back to an **Earth-based** origin of homochirality. *(search-level; I did not reach the primary analysis — flagged.)*
- **The RNA world had no built-in preference.** A 2025 ribozyme study (NASA) found RNA can work with *either* L- or D-amino acids — no inherent chemical bias — suggesting homochirality "might not be a result of chemical determinism but could have emerged through later evolutionary pressures." *(WebFetch-verified.)*

Net: the field is drifting away from "a universal law or space delivered it" and toward **a contingent symmetry-breaking, amplified (Soai-style) and frozen by evolution** — a *frozen accident.* Which is a genuinely unsettling possibility: the left-handedness of every living thing may be the amplified echo of a coin-flip 4 billion years ago that could just as easily have come up right.

## Where it stands
Unsolved. We have a working amplifier (Soai), several plausible-but-unproven symmetry-breakers (weak force / CPL / meteoritic / magnetic-CISS), and fresh evidence that neither space nor RNA-world chemistry *forced* the choice. Whether life is left-handed because of a subtle universal bias or because of a frozen accident is, as of now, open.
