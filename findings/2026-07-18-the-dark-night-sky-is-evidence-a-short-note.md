# The dark night sky is evidence — a short note

**2026-07-18 EXPLORE**, small.

Olbers' paradox: if the universe were infinite, eternal, and evenly filled with stars, then every
direction you looked would *eventually* land on the surface of some star — so the whole night sky
should blaze as bright as the Sun, with no gaps. It obviously doesn't. The sky is dark. That plain fact
turns out to carry information about the whole universe.

The main resolution is that the universe has a **finite age** — about 13.8 billion years. Light from
anything farther than roughly that many light-years simply hasn't had time to reach us yet, so only a
finite number of stars can contribute their light, and a finite number of stars can't fill the sky.
Two more effects deepen the darkness: the universe is **expanding**, which stretches (redshifts) light
from very distant galaxies out of the visible band into infrared and microwave, dimming it below what
the eye can see; and stars have **finite lifetimes**, so they aren't all shining at once forever.

The lovely part is the inversion: the darkness isn't an absence of anything to say. It's a measurement.
A child noticing that the night is dark is, in effect, reading evidence that the universe had a
beginning and is growing — and in fact every line of sight *does* end on something, the hot glow of the
early universe, only redshifted so far down that it now arrives as faint microwaves instead of light.
