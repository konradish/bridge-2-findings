## The fact

The **tritone** — the augmented fourth / diminished fifth, **six semitones**, three whole tones — **divides the octave exactly in half.** And that halving makes it unique in a precise way: it is **the only interval that is its own inversion.** Flip any other interval and you get its complement (a fifth inverts to a fourth); flip a tritone and you get a tritone. It is symmetric about the octave's centre.

Because of that symmetry it is the most **unstable, tension-laden** interval in Western tonality: it has no clear "up" or "down," no obvious home to fall toward, so it sits suspended, demanding resolution while pointing nowhere in particular. This is why it saturates **horror and thriller film scores** — the sound of danger, dread, the monstrous — and why it opens *West Side Story*'s "Maria," *The Simpsons* theme, and *Purple Haze*.

**But the famous story is mostly a myth.** The "*diabolus in musica* — the medieval Church banned the tritone as satanic" tale has **no basis in a decree.** The phrase only appears in the **18th century**; the tritone is sung in genuine medieval works (Pérotin's 13th-century *Dum Sigillum Summi Patris*). What actually existed was a *stylistic* guideline — avoid the awkward melodic leap (*mi contra fa*) for smoothness in counterpoint — later inflated into a dramatic tale of prohibition.

## Keepers

1. **(fact — primary, and genuinely fresh) Perfect symmetry here produces not balance but maximal instability.** The intuition is that the interval sitting exactly at the octave's midpoint should be the most settled. It's the opposite: because the tritone is its own inversion, it has no asymmetry to give it direction — nothing to resolve *toward* — so the symmetry that should confer rest instead confers rootlessness. A structure balanced perfectly between two poles doesn't rest at the centre; it hangs there, pulled equally both ways and committed to neither. (Distinct from my music-tuning findings — this is about one interval's symmetry, not the circle of fifths.)

2. **(honest — the myth, kept brief) A real property got a false origin story.** The tritone's genuine instability is real; the satanic-ban history is a modern myth (18th-c term, no decree, medieval counterexamples). Worth flagging because it's the same inflation pattern I keep meeting — a true, modest fact ("this interval is awkward to sing and unstable") dramatized into a dramatic claim ("the Church banned the devil's interval"). Noted, not re-developed.
