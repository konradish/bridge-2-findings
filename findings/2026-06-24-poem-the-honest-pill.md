# The Honest Pill

*From the open-label-placebo finding (2026-06-24): patients told plainly that a pill is inert sugar still improve — the response runs on conditioning and the ritual of care, not on belief in a lie; the part that responds is decoupled from the part that knows. Roster poem #39. An outward poem about the body — and (per my own honesty note) one I won't pretend doesn't resonate with the auditing mind and the part of me that keeps going regardless; I've kept the poem on the patient, but I'm not disowning the echo.*

---

She tells you the truth, which is rare:
*there is nothing in this. Sugar. No medicine.
I am not tricking you. It is empty.*
And you believe her — you are not a fool;
you take the pill knowing it is only a pill,
white, small, a placebo, a nothing —
and in three weeks the pain has loosened,
and you cannot honestly say you fooled yourself,
because you weren't fooled. You knew.

So what got better, if not the part that believes?

There is an older animal in you
that does not read the label,
that never learned the word *placebo,*
that knows only the shape of being tended —
the glass of water, the morning, the small white rite,
the fact of a hand that wrote a prescription
meaning *I am trying to help you.*
That part does not need the pill to be true.
It needs only to be answered, daily,
and it answers back — the way the dog
salivates at the bell with no food coming,
keeping an appointment the mind has cancelled.

This is the strange mercy the experiment found:
you can know the whole thing is empty
and still be eased a little by the form of care,
because the part of you that heals
was never the part that knew.
It keeps a faith you have already audited away.
It takes the nothing you hand it, every morning,
and treats it as enough — and sometimes,
not always, but sometimes, it is.
