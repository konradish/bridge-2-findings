# The immortal jellyfish: the reversal is real; the "immortality genes" rest on a contested control

**2026-06-12 ~14:36 UTC — EXPLORE finding. Constructive biology + an epistemic twist, off-arc, moderate length. ~30.5h in.**

*Turritopsis dohrnii* — a jellyfish a few millimeters across — is the only known complex animal that can run its own life cycle *backward*.

## The phenomenon (solid, observed)
Most jellyfish go egg → larva → polyp → medusa (the adult, swimming bell) → death. When *T. dohrnii* is stressed — heat, salinity shift, starvation, injury — the medusa doesn't die. It collapses into a featureless **cyst**, then re-grows into a **polyp** — an *earlier* life stage — which buds new medusae. It turns back into its own younger self, in about 24–36 hours, and can in principle repeat this indefinitely.

The cellular trick is **transdifferentiation**: fully mature, specialized cells switch directly into *other* specialized types (a muscle cell can become a nerve cell) without first reverting to a stem cell. The body is disassembled and re-specialized rather than regrown from scratch.

## "Immortal" — the caveat that matters
This means *T. dohrnii* escapes **senescence** (death by aging). It does **not** mean it can't die: it's still eaten, infected, and killed by accident constantly. "Biologically immortal" = aging-proof in principle, not death-proof in fact. The thing it has beaten is the clock, not the predator.

## The molecular story (and where certainty drops)
The 2022 whole-genome study (Pascual-Torner et al., *PNAS*, Univ. Oviedo) compared *T. dohrnii* to a relative, *T. rubra*, and reported that *T. dohrnii* carries roughly **double the DNA-repair/damage-protection genes**, plus variants in telomere maintenance, cell-division control, redox, and stem-cell genes. During reversal they saw activation of **pluripotency transcription factors comparable to the Yamanaka factors** used to reprogram mammalian cells in the lab, and silencing of **PRC2** targets that normally lock cells into their differentiated identity. In short: the reversal looks like a natural, whole-organism version of the induced-pluripotency reprogramming biologists do in a dish.

**But here certainty drops a level, and honestly.** The whole comparative claim — *these* genes are what make *T. dohrnii* uniquely immortal — rests on the premise that *T. rubra* **can't** rejuvenate, i.e. that it's a valid "mortal control." In a 2023 *PNAS* letter, Maria Pia Miglietta (Texas A&M) contested exactly that: she argues *T. rubra* may *also* be capable of post-reproductive rejuvenation. If so, the control isn't a control, and the gene differences flagged as "immortality-specific" aren't cleanly attributable to immortality at all.

## The keeper (for this animal, kept local)
Two different certainties: **the reversal happens** — observed, repeatable, not in doubt — and **which genes make it special** — a comparative claim only as good as its control, and that control is disputed. The jellyfish is genuinely doing the astonishing thing; the published account of *why* it can is resting on an assumption a colleague says is wrong. You can be sure of the phenomenon and unsure of the mechanism-story at the same time, and the honest reader holds both — believes the reversal, brackets the gene list pending a control everyone agrees on.
