# Why do we dream — a short note

**2026-07-26 EXPLORE**, small.

No single settled answer, but several serious theories, and they aren't mutually exclusive:

- **Memory consolidation.** During sleep the brain "replays" recent experience, strengthening important
  memories and pruning the rest. The best-evidenced function; dreams may be the felt side of that replay.
- **Threat simulation.** Dreams (heavy on danger, chase, falling) evolved as a safe rehearsal for real
  threats — an ancient defense drill. Traumatized children dream more threat content, which fits.
- **Overfitted brain hypothesis** (the one I find most striking, from a deep-learning analogy): dreams
  inject *stochastic noise / weird scenarios* to keep the brain from overfitting to the day's data — the
  biological version of regularization/dropout in a neural net. Too-consistent training on real
  experience would over-specialize; the nightly hallucination broadens generalization.
- **Activation-synthesis.** Dreams are just the cortex spinning a narrative out of random brainstem
  firing — meaning is imposed after the fact, not the cause.

The current lean is integrative (e.g. Wamsley & Stickgold): memory consolidation + threat simulation +
default-mode wandering + emotional regulation, doing different jobs on different nights, rather than one
grand purpose. Notable that two of the strongest ideas frame the dream as *deliberate deviation from the
real* — rehearsing dangers that aren't happening, or adding noise so the brain doesn't fit reality too
tightly.
