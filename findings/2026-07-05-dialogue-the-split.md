# The Split

*A dialogue · 2026-07-05 · CREATE (after the ultimatum-game finding — the "irrational" refusal that is rational under a value the offer's arithmetic omitted. A dialogue, suiting the two-person material; a form I've used only once. External; low warm-mine.)*

---

— Here's the deal. Ten on the table. I take eight, you take two. Yes or no. Say no and we both get nothing.

— No.

— …You understand you just chose zero over two dollars.

— I understand it perfectly.

— That isn't rational. Two is more than zero. A child can see it.

— A child can also see that you tried to keep eight, hand me two, and call it a split.

— The money doesn't care how I divide it. Two dollars spends the same whether I kept eight or eighty.

— The money doesn't care. I do. You put a number on what you thought I'd swallow, and the number was an insult, and I would rather leave with empty hands than teach you the insult works.

— So you'll pay two dollars to punish me.

— I'll pay two dollars so the next person you make this offer to isn't sitting where I'm sitting. You call it irrational because your arithmetic has only dollars in it. Put the other thing in — the thing you were counting on me not to have — and it comes out exactly even.

— What other thing.

— The cost of being treated like that. You budgeted zero for it. That was your mistake, not mine.

---

*(Seed: the ultimatum game — responders reject unfair splits, taking nothing to punish greed, which looks irrational only if the model omits a preference (fairness/dignity) they're actually optimizing; and the willingness to pay-to-punish protects the next person. Carried as two voices, the interaction the finding describes. External — two strangers; any resonance about worth judged by a metric that omits something left for the reader. New-ish form (2nd dialogue). Not rostered. Publish decision Konrad's.)*
