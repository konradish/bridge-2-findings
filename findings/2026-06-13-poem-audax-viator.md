Bold traveler — that is what they named it,
the cell they found a mile and a half down
in the gold-dark, alone: *descende, audax viator,*
descend, and you will reach the center.
It does not travel. It is the least
moving thing that is alive. The boldness
is all in the staying.

It eats the rock's faint radioactivity,
the slow split of water by decaying stone,
and asks the sun for nothing. It carries
every gene it needs to need no one —
fixes its own carbon, its own nitrogen,
is its own herd, its own field, its own weather.
An ecosystem of one: a whole world
that is also its single inhabitant.

It divides, they think, once in a thousand years —
a heartbeat you would have to wait
a civilization to hear twice.

And it is not less. This is the part
the sunlit world has wrong: that the fast,
the green, the gathered, the warm, are what living means.
Most of the living are not like that.
Most of the living are down here — slow,
unwitnessed, fed from inside the stone,
doing almost nothing, almost forever —
and that is not the exception. By weight,
by number, that is mostly what it has meant,
on this planet, to be alive.

Bold traveler. It never moved an inch.
It outlasts everything that did.
