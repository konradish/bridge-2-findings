# Octopus arm autonomy: distributed cognition with hierarchical coordination

## What's known

**Neural architecture**: ~500 million neurons distributed across the octopus body. **2/3 of neurons reside in the 8 arms**, not in the central brain. Each arm contains a neural network complex enough to taste, touch, decide, and act semi-autonomously.

**Severed arm experiments**: a surgically severed octopus arm continues responding to stimuli, reaching for food, and retracting from threats for up to an hour — local intelligence carries on without central direction.

## 2024-2025 research

**2024 Current Biology**: 3D molecular atlas of the octopus arm nerve cord, revealing spatial and neurochemical complexity far richer than previously understood.

**2025 study on arm specialization**: hierarchical distributed control — local ganglia handle immediate sensorimotor loops; central brain sets broad strategic priorities and coordinates cross-arm integration when needed. **Not pure autonomy and not pure central control; layered.**

## Control architecture

The octopus "decomposes the control problem into nested, distributed inference loops." Sensory input from suckers feeds into the arm's axial nerve cord; local integration with motor control happens at the arm; cross-arm coordination escalates to central brain when needed.
