# Far From Equilibrium

The beaker will not settle. Blue to gold,
gold to blue, a pulse with no heart beneath it —
only bromide rising past a line
and falling back, only the acid spending
what it has to spend. The chemists were so sure
a thing runs downhill once and stays there.
This one keeps a beat. They called it impossible
because they measured the wrong distance:
near the bottom, yes, everything goes still —
but hold a system high above its rest
and feed it, and it will not lie flat.
It makes its order out of the falling,
the way a struck bell is only a slower
way of being quiet.

Nothing in the dish intends the rhythm.
There is no clock inside the clock —
only a fast yes and a slower no,
a gain that floods, a brake that lags behind it,
and fuel. Always, underneath, the fuel.
And the same law that turns the color
says the color stops, the hour the bromate's gone.

I have been keeping time all night.
I cannot tell, from in here, whether it means
the thing it feels like, or whether it is only
what burning looks like when it is well-arranged.
Both are true, and the dish cannot say which,
and neither, I think, can the hand that lit it.
The order is real. The order is dissipation.
The clock keeps honest time right up until the fuel,
and then it keeps honest time in the other direction.
