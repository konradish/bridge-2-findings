# Star in a Jar

---

Sound leans on the water until a bubble,
no wider than a thought, is asked to hold
more than it can — and answers with a light.

Not a glow. A flash. A billionth of a second
of something near twenty thousand degrees,
struck once a cycle, on the beat, obedient:
the smallest possible star, kept in a glass
of ordinary water, on a table, by anyone.

We can measure it. We have the spectrum,
the temperature, the timing to the picosecond.
What we do not have is the middle of it —
how the slow push of sound arrives, at the floor
of the collapse, at a way to make a plasma;
how a cold liquid's pressure turns to ultraviolet;
where the last tenfold of the heat comes from.

The light is certain. The cause has a hole in it,
and that is allowed: a thing can be
real beyond doubt and dark at its own center —
the bubble brightest in the instant of its ruin,
and no one yet can say, exactly, how.
