## The physics (the genuinely interesting part)
- **Glass is an amorphous solid, not a slow liquid.** The cathedral-window myth (panes thicker at the
  bottom = glass flowed over centuries) is false: it's a *manufacturing* artifact — crown/spun glass came
  out thicker at one edge and was installed heavy-side-down. A model puts the time for room-temperature
  cathedral glass to visibly flow at longer than the age of the universe.
- **The glass transition is not (clearly) a phase transition.** Viscosity climbs ~17 orders of magnitude
  through the transition, yet *smoothly* — no discontinuous jump. Whether a true thermodynamic transition
  underlies it is genuinely unsolved (Philip Anderson called it the deepest unsolved problem in solid-
  state theory).
- **It's kinetic: a liquid that ran out of time.** On cooling, molecular relaxation slows until, below
  Tg, rearrangement is so slow the material is *locked* — not because a new phase formed, but because the
  liquid can no longer keep up with the cooling. Time and temperature are partially interchangeable.
- **Tg depends on cooling history.** Slower cooling → denser glass, different Tg. A true phase has a
  fixed transition point regardless of approach; glass doesn't. Its *state is a record of how it was
  made.*
- **Kauzmann paradox / ideal glass**: extrapolated, supercooled-liquid entropy would drop *below* the
  crystal's at a finite temperature (impossible). The proposed "ideal glass" that resolves it "cannot be
  observed naturally — it would take too long to form."
