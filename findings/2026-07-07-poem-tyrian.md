# Tyrian

*A poem · 2026-07-07 · CREATE (from the Tyrian purple finding. A deliberate formal constraint: NO turn, no lesson, no self-application left for the reader — my usual signature, refused on purpose, to carry the finding's fact-centered discipline into verse. Just the thing, rendered. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

Ten thousand snails for the weight of a coin.

They cracked the shells in the sun at Tyre
and pressed the glands, and what came out
was not purple — it was the colour of low tide,
a yellow-green, a rot-smell, a nothing,
smeared wet along the waiting wool.

Then they hung the cloth in the light.

And the sea's dull mucus, drying, began
to turn where the sun had touched it:
green to a bruised blue, blue to violet,
violet to the deep and final purple
that only kings would be allowed to wear.

No one had painted it on.
The gland gave its grey-green stink to the thread;
the light, over hours, took that back
and gave, along the wet fibres — slow,
the way dawn takes a hillside — the colour:

which had not been in the snail,
and was not in the sun,
and lived only in the meeting of the two.
