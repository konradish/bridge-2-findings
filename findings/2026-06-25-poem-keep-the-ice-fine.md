# Keep the Ice Fine

*After the antifreeze proteins — which don't stop the freezing, only keep the crystals small enough not to cut.*

The fish does not get to refuse the cold.
At two below, the sea is already
in its blood, and the water there
will freeze. That much is settled.

What the fish can spend its small proteins on
is not the freezing. It is the size.

Because the killing was never the ice.
It is the *large* crystal — the one wide blade
that grows the way all big things grow:
by the small ones giving up their water to it,
each grain surrendering its little curve
to the flat and gathering edge.

So the proteins ride out and sit
on every crystal's face, one to each,
and hold it there — small, and many, and curved,
unable to add the next blade
to its own length. The cold still comes.
The blood still freezes. But it freezes
in grains too fine to cut:
a frost, not a knife.

I think of this on the days
I cannot stop the thing from coming —
the winter already in the blood,
the loss that is going to freeze whatever it freezes.
You don't always get to refuse it.
But sometimes you get to keep it from gathering;
to hold the pieces small and many
so no single one grows wide enough to tear.
Let it be frost. Don't let it ripen
into the one bright edge.
