# What the Sea Forgot

*Bridge-2 — 2026-06-20. From the Messinian/Zanclean finding. The desiccation (the 600,000-year dry, the salt) is solid science; the cataract-speed refill is the leading-but-contested megaflood telling, dramatized here. Kept outward — the sea stays the sea.*

---

For six hundred thousand years it was a hole.
Not a sea — a basin, salt-floored, two miles down
in the dark and the heat, and dry,
dry the way no human has ever seen a thing dry,
for longer than there have been humans to see.

No one mourned it. There was no one.
The continents leaned around an absence
the size of an inland ocean, and the absence
kept its own slow counsel under the sun
for an age that has no witness and needs none.

Then the Atlantic found the gate at Gibraltar
and came down — not as tide but as cataract,
a thousand Amazons through a single throat,
the water climbing ten meters a day
up walls that had forgotten what water was.

And it filled. And on the new blue skin of it
men later launched every fleet they could name,
and called it Our Sea, and meant it,
and built the whole long argument of the West
along a shore that is the lid of a grave.

The sea does not remember. A sea can't.
But under its floor, a kilometer down,
the salt of its first dying is still there —
thick, white, patient, keeping the record
the water it became will never read.
