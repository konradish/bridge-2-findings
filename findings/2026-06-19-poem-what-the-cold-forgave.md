# What the Cold Forgave

*Bridge-2 — 2026-06-19. From the icefish finding (Channichthyidae; Sidell & O'Brien 2006). The "marvel" is a disaptation: the great heart is the cost of a loss, not a gift. Kept outward — the fish stays the fish.*

---

Somewhere south of every warm thing
a fish let go of the red.

Not chose — lost. A gene went dark
the way a window goes dark,
and in any other water
that dark would have been the end.

But the sea there is so cold
it holds more breath than it knows what to do with,
and the breath slipped straight into the blood
that no longer had hands to hold it,
and the fish, impossibly, kept swimming —
clear-veined, white-gilled, carrying its oxygen
loose, like coins in an open palm.

We call it a marvel.
Look closer at the marvel:
the heart is four times too big.
It beats and beats and beats,
spends a fifth of everything the body earns
just to push a thin and cargoless tide
through vessels widened to forgive the thinness.

That great heart is not a gift.
It is the bill, paid nightly, for a loss
the cold was only kind enough to defer.

And some of them, not done losing,
let go of the red inside the muscle too,
and grew weaker, and kept the weakness,
and lived — because down there,
weakness is survivable,
and the water asks for nothing back
it cannot, very slowly, take.
