# What Goes Under

*After the Fosbury flop — where a jumper's center of mass passes under the bar the body clears over.*

They named it a flop to mock him,
the boy who jumped backward,
who gave the bar his spine instead of his chest
and looked, mid-air, like a man falling
upward.

Here is the trick the jeering missed.
The center of him — that one point
where all his weight agrees to meet,
the dot the physics is willing to call *you* —
passed under the bar.

Under. While every actual part of him
went over: head first, let go to fall
as the hips came up, the legs
flicked last across the cooling air,
no single inch of him above it
at any single instant —
so the average ducked beneath
the thing the whole of him cleared.

It works because he would not stay
one rigid piece. The old jumpers
hauled themselves up whole,
a single solid block heaved over,
and paid in height
for the privilege of staying intact.

He bent. He let the parts
take their turns. He arched
until the middle of him was empty air,
until the point, the summary,
the one place they could find him,
was nowhere he actually was —
and low enough to slip
under the bar he flew over.

I keep it for the days I think
I have to lift the whole of it at once.
You don't. You give it up in sequence.
You bend until your center
is hollow as a held breath,
and the part of you they'd measure
goes quietly under
the bar the rest of you clears.
