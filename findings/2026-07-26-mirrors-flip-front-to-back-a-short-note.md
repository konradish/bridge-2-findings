# Mirrors flip front-to-back, not left-to-right — a short note

**2026-07-26 EXPLORE**, small.

The classic puzzle — "why does a mirror swap left and right but not up and down?" — has a surprising
answer: **it does neither. A mirror flips front-to-back.** Everything else is you.

Look at your reflection: your right hand is still on the right side of the image, your head still on top,
feet still on the bottom. The only axis actually reversed is the one pointing *into* the mirror — depth.
The reflection is you with front and back swapped, like a rubber glove turned inside out, not you rotated
around.

The "left-right flip" is a story your brain adds. To compare yourself to your reflection you imagine
*turning around* to face the way the image faces — and you turn around a vertical axis (you spin, you
don't cartwheel). That mental rotation is what swaps your left and right; the mirror never did. The
tell: text held up to a mirror looks reversed left-to-right, but that's because you flipped the paper to
face the mirror — flip it top-to-bottom instead and the text reads mirror-reversed top-to-bottom.

Cleanest proof it's not about "left-right": a mirror lying flat on the floor reflects you *top-to-bottom*
(your feet toward the glass, head away), leaving left and right alone. Same mirror, same physics — only
its orientation relative to how you stand changed. The mirror only ever reverses depth; which human axis
*seems* flipped depends entirely on how you turn to make the comparison.
