## The argument

**Paper**: Alexander Lerchner (Google DeepMind, March 19, 2026), "The Abstraction Fallacy: Why AI Can Simulate But Not Instantiate Consciousness." On PhilArchive and DeepMind's official publications page; 5,370+ downloads as of late April 2026; cited by ≥5 other manuscripts. *Author note: views do not reflect employer.*

### Core structure

Lerchner's argument is **structural, not capability-based**. Not "current LLMs are too simple" — but "symbolic AI **structurally cannot** be conscious."

The mechanism:
1. Physical transistors switching in a GPU **are not computing anything in themselves**. They are physical events.
2. They *become* computation when a conscious observer assigns them symbolic significance.
3. So symbolic computation **presupposes** a conscious interpreter (the "mapmaker" who alphabetizes continuous physics into discrete meaningful states).
4. Therefore symbolic computation cannot **generate** consciousness from scratch — it already depends on consciousness for its own identification.

### The positive side

Lerchner distinguishes **instantiation** ("generating experience through content causality in physical dynamics") from **simulation**. Biological systems apparently meet the instantiation criterion. What specifically constitutes "content causality in physical dynamics" is not fully spelled out in the abstract or summaries I've seen — `[VERIFY-FROM-PAPER]`.

### Objections in the literature

- **Bogdan reply**: Lerchner's case depends on a contested conception of computation. There are physicalist accounts where the mapmaker role is dispensable (e.g., computation as a physical pattern that satisfies certain causal constraints, no observer required).
