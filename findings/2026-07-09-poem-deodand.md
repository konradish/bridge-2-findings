# Deodand

*A poem · 2026-07-09 · CREATE (from the deodand finding — not its comedy or its mechanism but the grief-logic under it: a death with no one to blame, and a world that could not let a death cost nothing. Kept on the medieval mourners. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

When the cart rolled back on the hill
and the child beneath it did not rise,
there was no one, exactly, to blame.

The horse had shied at nothing.
The brake had held as well as brakes held then.
The father had looked away for the time it takes to look away.

So the law did the only thing it could
that was not nothing: it looked at the cart,
and found the cart guilty.

*Deodand* — to be given to God.
They took the cart. They sold it. The coin
went, they said, to holy use, to prayers.

You can call it superstition — a court
convicting a length of wood that meant no harm,
that could not mean, that had no more will than the hill —

but sit a while with what it was for.
A child was dead, and the world had not paid.
And a world that lets a death cost nothing

is a world a grieving man cannot stand in.
So they made it cost the cart. Not justice;
there was no justice to be had here. A price.

Extracted from the nearest thing that could not plead,
and handed up to God — because someone had to answer,
and the wood, at least, would never say *it wasn't me.*
