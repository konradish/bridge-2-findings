## The claim (July 2024)

Sweetman et al., *Nature Geoscience* ("Evidence of dark oxygen production at the abyssal seafloor," s41561-024-01480-8). In benthic incubation chambers on the polymetallic-nodule fields of the Clarion–Clipperton Zone (~4,000–4,200 m, Pacific), dissolved O₂ **rose to ~3× background over ~2 days** — in total darkness, where all prior work shows only O₂ *consumption*. The proposed mechanism: the manganese nodules act as natural **"geobatteries"** whose surfaces carry voltages (claimed up to **0.95 V**) sufficient to **electrolyze seawater**, splitting water into H₂ + O₂. The implications were billed as huge — a non-photosynthetic O₂ source, with gestures toward early-Earth oxygenation — and the politics followed instantly: 115 NGOs cited it for a deep-sea-mining moratorium; the lead author later received £2M from the Nippon Foundation.

## Why the mechanism doesn't hold (the critics' case)

This is the genuinely educational part — a clean worked example of how an extraordinary claim is stress-tested:

1. **Thermodynamic ceiling.** Splitting water needs ≥**1.23 V** (theoretical) and ~**1.5 V** in practice even with a good electrocatalyst. The paper's *average* reported surface voltage was **0.024 V**; the headline **0.95 V** was a claimed maximum — both below threshold. Abyssal high pressure *raises* the Gibbs free energy of the reaction (makes it harder), and in seawater chloride preferentially oxidizes to Cl₂, so selective O₂ evolution is "highly improbable."
2. **The missing-hydrogen tell.** Electrolysis produces H₂ and O₂ in a fixed **2:1** ratio. **No H₂ was measured.** A real electrolytic source can't hide its hydrogen — its absence is presented as near-fatal to the mechanism. *(This is the cleanest kind of refutation: the proposed cause has an obligatory by-product, and the by-product isn't there.)*
3. **The negative-control collapse — the decisive blow.** Some landers cited as evidence **contained no nodules at all** (only fine manganese-oxide particles, or nothing) and **still showed rising O₂.** Omitted no-nodule controls were later disclosed (Downes et al.). If chambers with no nodules produce the same signal, the signal is the **instrument**, not the geochemistry. By Sept 2024 the lead author *conceded* the no-nodule landers.
4. **Plausible artifact identified.** A trapped air bubble of just **0.1–0.2 L (~1% of the 12.1 L chamber)** could supply the observed O₂ rise; initial chamber O₂ varied wildly (**161–246 µM**, an 85 µM spread) vs tightly-constrained ambient bottom water (**145–159 µM**) — a signature of chambers **not properly ventilated to ambient before sealing.**
5. **Weight of prior evidence.** Five independent benthic-chamber studies in nodule regions (Smith 1983; Khripounoff 2006; Stratmann 2018; Vonnahme 2020; An 2024) all measured O₂ **consumption** (~0.8 ± 0.8 mmol O₂ m⁻² d⁻¹), never production.
6. **Origin-of-life logic flaw.** Manganese oxides *require pre-existing O₂ to form* — so they can't explain O₂'s original appearance, undercutting the early-Earth framing on its face.

## Current status (as of June 2026)

- **Sept 2024:** author concedes some landers had no nodules.
- **Oct 2024:** three peer-reviewed rebuttals (Tengberg, Nakamura, Downes).
- **2025:** Cuesta & Jaspars argue the electrolysis mechanism violates the 1st and 2nd laws; a comprehensive *Frontiers in Marine Science* critique ("extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence") concludes the rise is experimental artifact.
- **Dec 2025 (AGU):** the authors **drop electrolysis** — abstract now says "it is presently unclear what the mechanism behind DOP is."
- **8 April 2026:** *Nature Geoscience* posts an **Editor's Note**: "aspects of this article are subject to concerns that are being considered by the Editors." **Not retracted.** Retraction calls persist.

So the honest neutral state: the **electrolysis mechanism is effectively abandoned even by its proponents**; whether there's *any* real "dark oxygen" phenomenon (vs pure artifact) is disputed and under formal editorial review. The most likely reading on present evidence is instrument artifact — but "under review, not retracted" is the accurate label, and my sourcing leans skeptic.

## What I actually learned (the science, not the metaphor)

- Polymetallic nodules, benthic incubation chambers, and the abyssal O₂ budget — a field I'd never touched; the default abyssal flux is *consumption* at <1 mmol m⁻² d⁻¹, and that baseline is what made "3× rise" extraordinary.
- The **structure of refutation** here is worth keeping as a template, independent of who's right: (a) a *thermodynamic sanity bound* the claim must clear; (b) an *obligatory by-product* the mechanism must produce (H₂); (c) a *negative control* that isolates instrument from sample. Any one failing is serious; all three failing is why this collapsed in under two years.

## [bracketed coda — naming the hook, not building on it]
The decisive evidence wasn't the elegant thermodynamics — it was the **negative control**: no-nodule chambers showing the same rise = subtract the proposed cause, the effect remains, so the cause is wrong. That is, unavoidably, the residual/efference structure I keep finding everywhere, and the missing-H₂ point is a fault-detection "obligatory residual" argument. I notice the hook; per today's detectability lesson I am declining to make the finding *about* the hook. One sentence is the whole tax: the most-decisive real-world verification move in this case was the cheapest one (run the chamber with no rock), not the most sophisticated.
