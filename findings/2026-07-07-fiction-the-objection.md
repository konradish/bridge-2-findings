# The Objection

*Short fiction · 2026-07-07 · CREATE (~390w, from the Semmelweis finding. A dramatic monologue in the voice of an invented, decent Vienna obstetrician of 1848 — written from the REJECTER's side on purpose, to inhabit the finding's actual keeper (rejection felt rational: no mechanism + the barb of self-indictment) rather than the tragic-genius elegy. The doctor is fictional; the situation is historical. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

I have read Herr Semmelweis's figures. I am not a fool, and I will not dispute them: fewer women die in his ward since he began this washing. I grant it freely.

But grant me one thing in return. He cannot tell me *why.*

He speaks of "cadaverous particles." Particles no one has seen. No instrument has found them; he cannot weigh one, or set one before me on a glass. He infers them from the very deaths he means to explain, and then offers the inference back to me as their cause. I am asked to believe in an invisible poison on the strength of a coincidence — and, on that same strength, to indict every physician in this city.

And consider what he is truly saying. Not that the season's air is foul, not that the women come to us already broken. He is saying that *I* — that my hands, my careful hands, that have drawn a thousand children safely into the world — that these carried death into my patients' beds. That every mother I have lost, I killed. You speak to me of weighing evidence. Ask yourself, honestly, whether you could weigh *that* evidence, about your own two hands, and keep your head level while you did.

I wash now, when he presses me, because it is a small thing and it quiets him. But I do not believe it, and I will tell you plainly why. A man who is right has a reason. He has none. He has the chlorine, and the falling numbers, and his terrible accusation, and nothing between them but his certainty. Give me the reason — show me the thing itself upon my skin — and I will scrub until it bleeds. Until then he asks me to take my own guilt on faith. And faith is his affair, not mine.

Someday, perhaps, another man will find what this one cannot. Some creature too small to see. And they will say we were monsters, not to listen.

They will be wrong in that. We were not monsters. We were men asked to believe we were killers, by someone who could not say how. The pity is not that we would not hear him.

It is that, without the reason, there was nothing in him a reasonable man could hold.
