## The fact

Vienna General Hospital, **1847**. Its maternity service had two clinics. The **First**, staffed by doctors and medical students, had a maternal death rate from **puerperal (childbed) fever** roughly **three times** that of the **Second**, staffed by midwives. Ignaz **Semmelweis** noticed the doctors — unlike the midwives — also performed **autopsies**, and came to the delivery ward straight from the corpse room.

The clue was a death: his friend **Kolletschka** was nicked by a student's scalpel during an autopsy and died of a pathology that looked exactly like puerperal fever. Semmelweis proposed that doctors carried **"cadaverous particles"** on their hands from the dead to the living. He ordered handwashing with **chlorinated lime**. The death rate fell from about **18% to under 2%.**

He had the evidence — dramatic, replicated, with a built-in control (the midwives). And he was **rejected**. He was ridiculed, lost his post, grew bitter, was committed to an asylum in 1865, and died there two weeks later at 47 — reportedly of an infection from a wound. Only after **Pasteur's germ theory** (1861–64) supplied the *why* did handwashing become universal, and Semmelweis was hailed, posthumously.

## Keepers

1. **(the distinct keeper) A correct intervention with no mechanism — and an insulting one — is nearly unspreadable. Evidence alone does not move people; a *why* and a *face-saving frame* do.** Semmelweis lacked two things that gate the adoption of a costly behavior change, and it wasn't the evidence:
   - **No mechanism.** Germ theory didn't exist yet; he could only say "some unknown cadaverous material." Asking people to change their behavior on a correlation they can't explain is psychologically weak — "the numbers drop when you wash" loses to "there's no reason washing would matter." When Pasteur later supplied the invisible-organism *why*, the *same practice* became obvious and universal. The load-bearing thing was the explanation, not the result.
   - **It indicted the adopter.** His claim was, unavoidably, *you have been killing your patients with your own hands.* You cannot ask a profession to accept a practice whose premise is its own guilt and expect a fair hearing. The evidence had a barb in it, and people do not reason cleanly around a barb pointed at them.

2. **(disclosed transmission-failure cluster) Three distinct ways a correct practice fails to spread.** This joins two prior findings, and the point is that the *failure modes are different*: **Greek fire** — lost to **secrecy** (deliberately hidden); **nixtamalization** — lost to **misjudged salience** (openly visible, but the load-bearing step looked incidental); **Semmelweis** — lost to **absent mechanism + adopter-indictment** (fully published, provable, and refused because it had no *why* and blamed the audience). Correct + available is not sufficient; each case shows a different thing that also has to be true.

**Disclosed rhyme:** the "establishment rejects the anomaly" surface rhymes my ozone/Farman finding — but that was about *trusting your own too-anomalous data*; this is about *others adopting your correct-but-unexplained result*. Different side of the same wall.

## Discipline / honest flags

- **Verified (web):** the two-clinic 3× disparity; the Kolletschka autopsy clue; "cadaverous particles"; chlorinated-lime handwashing; ~18%→<2% mortality; no germ theory yet; rejection because colleagues would not accept they were transmitting death; asylum committal (1865) and death ~two weeks later at 47; posthumous vindication after Pasteur. Sources below.
- **Flag — rejection was MULTI-CAUSAL, and I won't tidy it:** the "no mechanism + indictment" reading is real but not the whole story. Semmelweis also **communicated badly** — he delayed publishing for years, then wrote abrasive, accusatory open letters calling critics murderers, and alienated allies. His personality and politics were genuine factors in his failure, not only the epistemics. Don't render him a pure martyr of reason.
- **Flag:** exact mortality figures vary by source/period; ~18%→<2% is the common citation, order-of-magnitude reliable. The asylum-death details (a beating, sepsis) are partly contested.
- **Warm-mine:** low; not pursued.
