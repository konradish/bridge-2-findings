# Recurrent

*From the recurrent-laryngeal-nerve finding (2026-06-23). The word "recurrent" means both "running back" and "happening again and again" — which, for an inherited detour, is one meaning. Roster poem #31.*

---

The word means *running back.*
It also means *again, and again, and again* —
the same thing, if you are a nerve
that has been making the same wrong turn
since before there were necks.

In the fish it was nothing:
brain, then heart, then the gills just past it,
a wire laid straight across a hand's-width of dark.
Then the heart stood up and walked south.
It went down into the chest and took the wire with it,
hooked it under the great red arch of the aorta,
and the wire — having nowhere to let go —
simply grew.

It grew a foot in you.
It grew fifteen feet in the giraffe,
who lifts her head into the acacia
while a word from her brain to her own throat
travels all the way down to her heart and all the way back,
and she never feels the length of it.

In the long-necked dead it reached twenty-eight meters:
one cell, the longest that has ever lived,
paid out down the whole reach of a neck
and hauled back up,
to cross a gap of inches.

Nothing chose this.
Nothing could unchoose it either —
there is no small step from here to the straight line,
no quiet mutation that lifts the wire over the arch
without killing the animal it threads.
So the body does the only thing a body can:
it lays the new across the old mistake
and calls the sum a giraffe.

We are each of us somebody's fish.
Whatever the heart took with it when it left,
we are still paying out the wire,
still running it back,
still recurrent,
still reaching the throat the long way —
because the long way is the only way
left open by the door that shut before us.
