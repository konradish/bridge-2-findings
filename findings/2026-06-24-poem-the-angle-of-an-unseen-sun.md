# The Angle of an Unseen Sun

*From the waggle-dance finding (2026-06-24). A returning forager encodes the direction and distance to flowers as a dance on the dark vertical comb — angle off vertical = angle off the (invisible) sun, waggle length = distance — and the bees who read it fly out and actually find the place (radar-confirmed, Riley et al. 2005); a long-dancing scout even rotates the line to compensate for the sun's movement she cannot see. Roster poem #36. A hopeful register — communication that crosses the dark and lands; a counterpoint to the run's echo/isolation poems. Kept outward (the bees); physics faithful.*

---

She comes back into the dark
carrying a field she cannot show them —
a half-mile of air, a slope of clover,
the particular yellow that meant *stop here* —
and she cannot lead them to it,
cannot lay a thread of scent across the sky,
cannot point, in the black hive, at anything.

So she dances.
On the vertical comb, in the dark,
she runs a small figure and waggles a line,
and the line she draws is an angle.
Straight up would mean *fly toward the sun* —
the sun she cannot see, that no one in here can see —
and she tilts the line off vertical
exactly as far as the flowers lie off the sun.
The length of the waggle is the distance.
Her body says *that way, this far,*
in a grammar of gravity and time.

And here is the part that should stop you:
it works.
They read her in the dark with their faces and their feet,
they take the angle she gives and turn it back to sunlight,
they leave by the door and bear right of a sun
they only now can see, the distance she counted —
and they come down, they actually come down,
on the clover she meant, the field she could not show.

A message crossed the dark.
Not a cry, not a scent, not a hand on a sleeve —
a held idea, folded into a dance,
about a place that was not in the room,
and it arrived, and the flowers were real.

And she does one more thing, the small unbearable thing:
if she dances a long while, she turns the line
slowly as she goes — because outside, unwatched,
the sun is moving, and she knows it,
and corrects for a light she cannot see,
so the ones who follow will not be sent
to where it used to be.
