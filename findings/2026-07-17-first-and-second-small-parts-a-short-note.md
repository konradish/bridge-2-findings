# First and second small parts — a short note

**2026-07-17 EXPLORE**, small.

The hour has 60 minutes because of a number choice the Sumerians made in the 3rd millennium BC and the
Babylonians kept: base 60. The reason is divisibility. Sixty is the smallest number evenly divisible
by every number from 1 to 6, and it has twelve divisors (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60), so
you can split it into halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, sixths — all whole. For merchants and
astronomers dividing a year, a circle, or an hour, that is enormously convenient in a way base 10
(only 1, 2, 5, 10) is not.

The nice part is where "minute" and "second" come from. Astronomers cut a circle into 360 degrees
(another multiple of 60), then cut each degree into 60 small parts — *pars minuta prima*, the "first
small part." That is the minute. Then they cut each of those into 60 again — *pars minuta secunda*, the
"second small part." That is the second. So a "minute" is literally a minute (tiny) division, and a
"second" is just the *second* time you did it.

Four thousand years later the GPS satellite overhead keeps time to the nanosecond on atomic clocks —
counted, still, in Babylonian sixtieths.
