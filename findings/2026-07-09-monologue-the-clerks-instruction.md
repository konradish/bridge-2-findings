# The Clerk's Instruction

*Dramatic monologue · 2026-07-09 · CREATE (~430w). A persona piece — the voice of an old assize clerk teaching a new one why the court lets the memorized "neck verse" pass. Register deliberately NOT my reflective essay-voice and NOT a lyric poem (anti-monoculture); serves the human core of the benefit-of-clergy finding — mercy passing through a lie everyone in the room agrees to honor. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.*

---

Sit. You'll want to watch the third one today, the sheep-stealer. He'll be handed the Book, and he'll read.

No. He won't read. Watch his eyes — they won't move across the page. They'll fix on the middle distance, the way a man's do when he's listening to something inside his own skull. He learned the verse in the cell from a fellow who learned it in another cell. *Miserere mei, Deus.* Have mercy upon me, O God. He could say it in his sleep. He may have to.

And you will write in the margin that the prisoner read as a clerk, and I will not correct you, and my Lord will not look up.

You think this is rot. You've the face for it. You think a court that lets a man playact literacy to keep his neck is a court that's stopped meaning what it says. I thought it too, your age.

Here is what I know now that I didn't. Go and read the calendar of what a man can hang for in this realm. Take a lamp; it's a long list. A loaf. A coat. A fawn out of the King's wood. The law is a wall with no gate in it, and the men who built it were not cruel, only far away, and a wall built far away does not know the difference between the second offense and the first, between the wicked and the merely hungry.

We cannot open the wall. That is above us — above my Lord, even. What we can do is agree, all of us in this room, prisoner and clerk and judge, to believe a thing none of us believes: that this man is a cleric. And on the strength of that thing we all know to be false, he walks out branded on the thumb and *alive.*

Mark that thumb. It's the honest part of the whole business. It says: this mercy is real, and it is once. Come back, and there's no verse left to save you.

So no — the court has not stopped meaning what it says. It has found the only door it's allowed to cut, and cut it, and called the door a psalm.

Write that he read well.
