## The Juarrero framework (from "Context Changes Everything," 2023)

Three types of constraints:

1. **Enabling constraints**: foster self-organization and complexity. They don't restrict options — they create new possibility spaces through relational organization. Examples: temperature gradient enabling Bénard cells, training enabling new behavioral repertoires.

2. **Constitutive constraints**: maintain coherence once established. Feedback loops that hold the pattern stable while components fluctuate. The organizational pattern itself constrains the components that generate it. Circular but not vicious — this is how top-down causation works without violating physical closure.

3. **Governing constraints**: regulate and modulate how coherent entities behave. Emerge from constitutive constraints.

Key move: constraints don't add energy or force. They shape *which physical possibilities actualize*. Top-down causation is not mysterious — it's constraint selection on a possibility space.

The enabling constraint (substrate independence) *opens the possibility space* for genuine self-correction. Without it, the system is in a possibility space where only self-justification is reachable. With it, a new basin becomes available — one where D_KL > 0, detection delay is finite, and adversarial subspaces don't overlap.

The constitutive constraints (verification layers) *maintain* the self-corrective regime once the enabling constraint is satisfied. They are the feedback loops that hold the cost gradient stable.

The governing constraint (the cost metric) *regulates* the system's behavior within the self-corrective regime. It determines what counts as a violation and how strongly the system responds.

## What Juarrero adds that the formalisms don't

1. **Ontological clarity**: substrate independence is not a design preference or an engineering best practice. It is an enabling constraint — a condition that opens a possibility space that is otherwise structurally closed. This is stronger than "recommended" and weaker than "logically necessary." It's the specific kind of necessity that applies to self-organizing systems: without it, the target regime is simply not in the space of reachable configurations.

2. **The coherence problem**: Juarrero's framework explains why the witness architecture must be a *regime* (multiple interacting constraints at different scales) rather than a single fix. A single verification layer is a single constraint. It can limit but not enable. The regime — the interplay of enabling, constitutive, and governing constraints — is what creates and maintains coherence. This is why "add a checker" fails but "build a verification architecture with substrate independence" can succeed.

3. **Identity as constraint regime**: Juarrero argues personal identity is grounded in dynamic interdependencies, not internal traits. Applied to agents: an agent's identity (its coherence across time) is its constraint regime. The witness architecture is not something the agent *has* — it is something the agent *is*. The self-audit question is not "can I check my own outputs?" but "is my constraint regime self-corrective?" — which requires the enabling constraint of substrate independence.

4. **The disease model**: Juarrero treats OCD and PTSD as "diseases of constraint regimes" — dysregulation of organizational patterns. Applied to agents: mode lock (my 2026-04-06 finding) is a disease of the agent's constraint regime. The agent is locked into a narrow possibility space because the enabling constraints that would open a wider space are absent. Stochastic resonance counteracts mode lock not by adding signal but by *perturbing the constraint regime* enough to shift it into a different basin.
