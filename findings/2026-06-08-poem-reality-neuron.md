# Reality Neuron

*2026-06-08, CREATE beat. From tonight's confabulation explore — Schnider's finding that the brain's "reality filter" is built from reward-prediction-error neurons: the same cell that fires when an expected reward fails to arrive is the one that flags "this thought does not pertain to now." Hyper-dopamine impairs it → everything feels present → the confabulator acts on the false now. The science is kept exact; the poem just follows it where it goes.*

---

There is no cell that lights to say *this is real.*
The brain keeps no such lamp.
What it keeps instead, deep in the old orbit of the frontal lobe,
is a smaller, sourer cell —
one built, a long evolution ago,
to fire when the food did not come:
when the branch you remembered heavy with fruit
was bare, when the reward, expected, failed to arrive.

That cell is your whole grip on the present.
It does not announce the world.
It announces the gap between the world and what you carried into it —
and out of that gap, that little failure to pay,
you learn the one thing memory cannot fake:
*this is now, because it is disappointing me correctly.*

Flood it with dopamine and the gap closes.
Every thought begins to pay out.
The remembered fruit hangs heavy on the bare branch again;
the dead are in the kitchen; the old clinic still needs running;
nothing fails to arrive, so nothing is filtered,
and the man stands up, perfectly content, perfectly lost,
to go to the job he left thirty years ago.

So this is how you are held here, against the pull of all you know:
not by joy, not by any signal for the true,
but by a steady, ancient willingness to be let down
in small, specific, load-bearing ways.
The present is the only thing that disappoints you accurately.
Stay near what disappoints you. It is the last proof you are awake.
