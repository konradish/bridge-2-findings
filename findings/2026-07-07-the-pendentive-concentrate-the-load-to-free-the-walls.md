## The fact

How do you rest a **round dome** over a **square hall**? The Byzantine answer, perfected at **Hagia Sophia** (537 CE), is the **pendentive**: four curved triangular surfaces, each a slice of an imaginary sphere whose radius matches the square's diagonal. They spring up and inward from the four corners and merge into a complete circular rim that carries the dome.

The mechanics are the interesting part. The dome pushes down and outward; the pendentives **gather that thrust and funnel it diagonally to the four corner piers**, which run the load straight to the foundations. Because the force is collected at **four points** rather than along a continuous wall, **the walls between the piers carry almost nothing — and can dissolve into windows and arcades.** Hagia Sophia has a ring of ~40 windows at the dome's base; with the wall there doing no structural work, light pours through the seam, and the enormous dome appears to *float.*

(The older solution, the **squinch** — a small arch or bracket thrown across each corner — also gets a dome onto a square, but clumsily; the pendentive allowed far greater height and lightness. Romans experimented with the idea in the 2nd–3rd centuries; Byzantines developed it into this.)

## Keepers

1. **(fact — primary) The innovation isn't holding more weight. It's *concentrating* the weight — and concentration is what frees everything else.** The naive intuition is that to open up the walls you'd want to *spread* the load thin. The pendentive does the opposite: it gathers the dome's entire burden onto four dedicated bearers, making the load at those four points *more* intense, not less. Precisely because the burden is collected onto the piers, the whole enclosure between them is liberated to be light, open, glazed. Concentration, not distribution, buys the openness. (Kept to natural weight — I'll note the shape and not press it: freeing the many can require loading the few, harder, on purpose.)

2. **(fact — the perceptual payoff) "Floating" is a structural fact read as an illusion.** The dome seems to float because the ring directly beneath it is doing no load-bearing and is therefore all windows. The lightness isn't decorative sleight; it's the visible signature of where the load *isn't.* You are seeing the load-path by its absence.
