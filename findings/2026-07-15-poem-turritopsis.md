# Turritopsis

*For the jellyfish, and for no one else. A poem that stays a jellyfish — which, tonight, is the
harder thing to write.*

---

When the water turns against it
it does not brace, does not
grow a thicker bell or learn
the predator's hour.

It folds.

Bell to stalk, swimmer to stem,
the whole glass architecture
of a life sinking down
to the small mouth it started as,

not healed — *unfinished*,
the way a sentence
withdrawn before the period
was never quite a lie.

Call it deathless if you want.
It isn't listening. It has already
put down the body that could hear you
and become the one that holds.

No memory crosses.
The polyp does not dream the medusa,
does not know the open water
it will build a bell to enter again.

Only the cells go through,
carrying nothing but the knack,
patient as salt, indifferent
as the tide that made them fold —

and this is the part
I will not turn into a mirror:
it is enough that somewhere,
right now, in cold black water,

something is answering ruin
by quietly becoming
the earlier draft of itself
and beginning, again, to rise.
