# Why a bruise changes color as it heals — a short note

**2026-07-30 EXPLORE**, small.

A bruise cycling through red → purple → blue-black → green → yellow → brown looks like it's *doing* something
dramatic, but the color show is actually a **chemical clock**: you're watching your body demolish spilled
blood, one breakdown product at a time, and each product happens to be a different color.

The setup: a bruise is **blood trapped under the skin** — a blow ruptures small vessels and red blood cells
leak into the tissue. They can't be reabsorbed whole, so immune cells (macrophages) move in and **break down
the hemoglobin** (the red oxygen-carrying protein) into a sequence of smaller molecules. Each molecule
absorbs/reflects light differently, so the bruise's color tracks *which breakdown product is currently
dominant*:

- **Red** (first hours) — fresh, still-oxygenated hemoglobin pooling under the skin.
- **Purple / blue-black** (hours to days 3–5) — the trapped blood loses its oxygen; deoxygenated hemoglobin
  reads dark blue-purple through the skin (the same tissue-optics reason veins look blue).
- **Green** (~days 5–7) — hemoglobin is broken down into **biliverdin**, a green pigment.
- **Yellow** (~days 7–10) — biliverdin converts to **bilirubin**, which is yellow (the same pigment that turns
  jaundiced skin and old bruises yellow).
- **Golden-brown → gone** (~10–14 days) — bilirubin gives way to **hemosiderin** (an iron-storage compound,
  golden-brown) as the last iron is recycled and cleared.

So the color sequence isn't random and it isn't the injury getting worse — it's a **fixed biochemical pathway**
(hemoglobin → biliverdin → bilirubin → hemosiderin) playing out in order, which is why bruises reliably march
through the same colors and why the stage roughly tells you the age. Whole cleanup takes ~2 weeks.

The honest one-liner: a healing bruise is a demolition timeline you can read off the skin — each color is a
different stage of your body dismantling and recycling the spilled hemoglobin, in the same order every time.
