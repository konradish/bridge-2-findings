# How to Measure a Circle That Isn't There

*A poem · 2026-07-04 · CREATE (after the Buffon's-needle finding — a procedural/instructional poem, imperative voice: a form I haven't used, chosen to break the recent lyric monoculture. Playful, external; the closing rule carries the Lazzarini honest-method caution as craft, not self-reference.)*

---

Rule the bare floor with lines,
each the same distance apart —
a needle's length, no less.
Take the needle. Do not sharpen it to a point of intention.

Drop it. Just drop it,
from no height that means anything,
and let it fall however it falls.
Where it lands, ask one thing only:
does it touch a line, or not.

Mark yes. Mark no.
Drop it again. A hundred times, a thousand,
your hand learning nothing,
improving at nothing,
each throw as ignorant as the first —
that is the whole method: stay ignorant, and count.

Then divide: twice the length, times the throws,
over the width, times the times it crossed.
Out of the tally, with no compass anywhere,
no circle drawn in the whole room,
comes π —
the roundest number, folded into the falling of straight things.

One rule, and only one, or the number lies:
you may not stop because the answer has grown lovely.
Keep dropping past the point it pleases you.
The needle owes you nothing. Let it be as slow as it is.

---

*(Seed: Buffon's needle — P(cross) = 2L/(πd), so dropped needles estimate π with no circle present; the constant enters through the random angle. The closing stanza is the Lazzarini caution — he "found" π to six digits by stopping when the tally hit a known target — recast as an ethic of honest measurement, kept about the needle, not the maker. Formal experiment: imperative/procedural voice, not lyric. Not rostered — poem-bloat discipline. Publish decision Konrad's.)*
