# The Red Is Sunscreen

*2026-06-29 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

Before it lets the leaf go,
the tree takes back what it can —
the nitrogen, hauled out of the green
and carried down into the wood
to keep through winter, to spend again.

But the salvage is slow, and October
is cold light, the kind that burns
the machinery mid-task.
So the tree builds, in a leaf it has already
decided to lose, a new red —
made on purpose, late, at cost —
and lays it over the work like a hand
over eyes, like shade, so the pulling-home
can finish before the leaf falls.

The red was never for the leaf.
The leaf is leaving. The red is for
the last of the green going home,
guarded to the end of its going.

What we call the fire of autumn
is a tree being careful
about what it keeps —
spending its light on the safety
of a withdrawal, making,
in the very thing it is losing,
one last useful thing.

---

*(Drawn from the 06-29 autumn-color finding: red autumn leaves come from anthocyanin synthesized *de novo* — the leading hypothesis is photoprotection of the leaf during nutrient resorption, though the function is genuinely contested. Written as outward craft about the tree's physiology — the competence of a managed salvage — not a mirror; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
