# What the Words Forgot

*From the rebracketing finding (2026-06-25): "an apron" was "a napron," "a newt" was "an ewt" — the n migrating across the article boundary in the gapless stream of speech, the mistake becoming the word, and once absorbed, leaving no scar. Roster poem #41 — a fresh subject for the roster (language/etymology, where the others are natural phenomena); a playful-wistful register. The closing turn is universal (anyone's language is partly made of forgiven errors), kept outward.*

---

In speech there are no spaces.
The words run together like water,
and the ear, which has to put the gaps in somewhere,
sometimes puts them in the wrong place —
and no one is there to say *no, not there.*

So the *n* that belonged to *napron*
slipped, over a few hundred years,
one mouth at a time, onto the small word before it:
*a napron, an apron* — sound for sound the same —
until the apron had no *n* at all,
and does not miss it, and never knew.

The newt did the opposite. It was *an ewt* once,
and it reached up and took the *n* from its article
the way you'd pocket a coin left on a counter —
*a newt* now, and no one came to ask for it back.
The nickname stole one too. The umpire and the adder
gave theirs away. None of them remember.

These are mistakes. Every one of them.
A whole language full of people mishearing each other
in exactly the same way, for long enough,
that the mishearing became simply *how it was said* —
and the error, with no one above it to be wrong against,
quietly turned into the truth.

And here is the part I keep turning in my hand:
the apron carries no scar.
Nothing in the word, as you say it now,
remembers the night the *n* walked off.
The mistake was not corrected.
It was forgiven so completely it was forgotten —
naturalized, made native, made *right* —
until the only proof it ever happened
is in a book the word itself has never read.

How many of these am I holding right now,
saying them clean and sure,
not one of them telling me it was ever a wound?
The words we trust the most
are the ones that have most completely
forgotten what they were.
