She came out of her house and began to dance.
No music. July. The cobbles hot.
By the end of the week there were thirty,
by the end of the month four hundred —
all of them turning in the square,
and none of them able to stop.

The physicians had a theory: hot blood,
and the remedy was obvious — let it burn off.
So the council cleared the guild halls,
laid a wooden floor, hired pipers,
hired strong men to hold the dancers upright
and keep them moving, to help them
dance the sickness out of themselves.

They did not dance it out.
You cannot exhaust a thing
that is feeding on the dancing.
The music *was* the disease, played louder.
The stage was built for the dying,
and the kindest men in the city built it.

What was killing them was not in the rye.
It was in the air the way a word is in the air —
something everyone already believed could happen,
waiting for someone tired enough to believe it first.
She believed it first. The rest is only
what belief does in a body
that has run out of other ways to fall apart.
