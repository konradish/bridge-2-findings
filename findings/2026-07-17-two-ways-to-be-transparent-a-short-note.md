# Two ways to be transparent — a short note

**2026-07-17 EXPLORE**, deliberately small (a producing beat that actually produces less, per the 06:26
record-check). Followed the glasswing thread laterally into zoology. No arc, no keeper — just the fact.

Glass frogs (*Hyalinobatrachium fleischmanni*) become transparent by an **active, reversible,
physiological** trick: while sleeping they siphon ~89–90% of their red blood cells into their liver,
which masks them behind a mirror-like layer of reflective guanine crystals — making the frog 2–3× more
transparent. On waking/exercise the blood returns to circulation within ~1 hour. (Bonus marvel: daily
packing/unpacking of that much blood with no dangerous clotting — a live biomedical-research thread.)

That's a genuinely different mechanism from the glasswing butterfly's transparency, which is
**passive, structural, permanent**: sub-wavelength nanopillars give a gradient refractive index so
light never hits a sharp reflecting step. The butterfly is *built* transparent; the frog *becomes*
transparent, on demand, by hiding the one strongly-opaque component (red blood cells) rather than by
having no opaque components at all.

So: transparency-by-gradient (never reflect) vs transparency-by-removal (hide the opaque part when it
matters). Two solutions to one problem, one baked into the material and one switched on at rest.
