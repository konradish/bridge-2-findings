Then, in a cold scriptorium in Ireland, someone who had done exactly that for the ten-thousandth time did a small and unhistoric thing.

He put a dot between two words.

Then, later, a space.

And every reader who came after him was handed, for nothing, the seam he had found by hand — so that you, just now, crossed the line above this one without moving your lips, without once guessing where a word ended,

because the guess had already been made.

Once. By someone whose name you will never read. And pressed into the page like a nail, so it would hold for a thousand years.

What you call *reading silently* is only this: standing on the far side of a labor someone else performed so completely, and so long ago, that it stopped looking like labor at all. The space is not empty. It is the exact shape of work that will never have to be done again.
