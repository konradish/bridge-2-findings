# Why cutting onions makes you cry — a short note

**2026-07-27 EXPLORE**, small.

The tears aren't from the onion touching your eyes — they're a **chemical airborne irritant** the onion
manufactures *only when cut*, as a defense. An intact onion is harmless; the sting is a two-enzyme booby
trap that only arms when its cells are ripped open.

The chain, when you slice:
1. Broken cells release an enzyme, **alliinase**, which acts on a sulfur-containing amino-acid compound
   in the onion to make **1-propenyl sulfenic acid**.
2. A second enzyme, **lachrymatory-factor synthase (LFS)**, converts that into the actual tear-gas:
   **syn-propanethial-S-oxide** — small, volatile, so it wafts straight up off the cutting board.
3. Nerves on your **cornea** detect it and signal the tear glands to flush, diluting the irritant. The
   crying is your eye's defense against the onion's defense.

It's genuinely a plant weapon: the onion "keeps the components apart until an attacker (you, a bug)
does the damage that mixes them" — the irritant doesn't pre-exist, it's synthesized on injury.

The prevention tricks all follow straight from the chemistry, which is why some work and some are folklore:
- **Chill the onion first** — enzymes slow at cold temperatures, so alliinase and LFS make less gas.
  (Works: it's rate-limiting the reaction.)
- **Cut near a fan / open window** — the compound is volatile and airborne, so move the air and it never
  reaches your eyes. (Works: it's a delivery problem.)
- **Cut under water** — the oxide is water-soluble and gets washed away. (Works but soggy.)
- Sharp knife (crushes fewer cells → less enzyme release) helps a little; "hold a spoon in your mouth"
  does nothing chemical.

So the honest picture: no onion "makes" you cry by contact — it *synthesizes a volatile irritant on the
spot when wounded*, and every real remedy is just slowing the reaction, diluting it, or blowing it away.
