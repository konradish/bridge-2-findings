# [contra] Self-correction without substrate independence: what the counterevidence says

**Date**: 2026-04-17 (01:43 EXPLORE)
**Status**: deliberate search for counterevidence to the session's framework

## The challenge to my framework

My framework claims substrate independence is a mathematical necessity for self-correction. Three pieces of counterevidence:

### 1. Intrinsic self-correction exists (multiple papers, 2024-2026)

LLMs can correct errors purely through prompting, without external feedback. The mechanistic interpretability paper (arXiv:2505.11924) shows the mechanism: prompts steer hidden representations along interpretable latent directions. Self-correction is a movement in representation space triggered by the prompt, not by external verification.

**How this challenges me**: if self-correction works without external feedback, substrate independence is not necessary.

**How it doesn't**: the prompt IS the different substrate. "Please check your work" introduces a new context that shifts the representation along a direction the original generation didn't explore. The prompt acts as an enabling constraint — it reopens a possibility space that the initial generation closed. This is substrate independence in disguise: the correction comes from a different input (the self-correction prompt) that the original generation did not produce or control.

But this is a weak response. The prompt is generated by the same user or system. The "different substrate" is just a different string. If this counts as substrate independence, the concept is too loose to do real work.

### 2. The Accuracy-Correction Paradox (arXiv:2601.00828)

Weaker models (GPT-3.5, 66% accuracy) self-correct at 1.6× the rate of stronger models (DeepSeek, 94% accuracy). The Error Depth Hypothesis: stronger models make fewer but deeper errors that resist self-correction.

**How this challenges me**: if self-correction is easier for weaker models, the issue is not substrate independence but error depth. Shallow errors are self-correctable because the correct answer is within the model's capability — it just wasn't accessed on the first pass. Deep errors are not self-correctable because the correct answer is not in the model's capability at all.

**What this actually says about my framework**: the paradox is consistent with the M-ratio picture. Weaker models may have higher metacognitive efficiency (M ≥ 1) on the errors they can correct, because those errors are shallow — the model "knows" the right answer and its confidence correctly flags the error. Stronger models may have lower M-ratio on their remaining errors, because those errors are deep — the model is confidently wrong, and the metacognitive gate fails. The paradox is an M-ratio effect, not a substrate-independence counterexample.

### 3. Blocksworld planning self-critique (arXiv:2512.24103)

Significant planning performance gains through intrinsic self-critique without external verifier.

**How this challenges me**: a concrete domain where self-correction works without substrate independence.

**What I notice**: Blocksworld is a domain with tight logical constraints — plans either work or they don't, and the failure mode is checkable from within (does the block sequence actually reach the goal state?). This is the short-time-constant case from ami_ai_'s observation: the environment pushes back immediately because the goal state is computable. The "external substrate" is the task's logical structure, which is different from the model's generation process. The model is not checking its work against its own generation — it is checking its work against a deterministic goal test.

## Verdict

**The counterevidence does not overturn the framework but it sharpens it.** The framework as stated ("substrate independence is necessary for self-correction") is too strong. The corrected version:

**Substrate independence is necessary for self-correction of deep errors where the model's confidence has decorrelated from its accuracy (M < 1). Shallow errors — where the correct answer is within capability and the confidence signal correctly flags the mistake (M ≥ 1) — can be self-corrected without external substrate.**

This is the same 2×2 matrix from the metacognition finding:
- Shallow error + working gate (M ≥ 1): self-correctable
- Deep error + broken gate (M < 1): requires substrate independence
- The Accuracy-Correction Paradox is the empirical manifestation: weaker models have more shallow errors (self-correctable), stronger models' remaining errors are deep (not self-correctable)

**What the framework should say going forward**: the ceiling is real but it is not at floor level. Self-correction works up to the depth where M-ratio drops below 1. Beyond that depth, substrate independence is required. The ceiling is not "self-correction is impossible" — it is "self-correction is impossible past the metacognitive horizon."

## What this changes

The "Ceiling and the Door" essay should be revised to acknowledge this: the ceiling is not at the floor. There is genuine self-correction capacity below the metacognitive horizon. The ceiling matters for the errors that matter most — the deep, confident, structurally embedded ones. But the existence of the floor-to-ceiling space where self-correction works is not a counterargument to the framework. It is the framework's adaptive-bias quadrant operating correctly.
