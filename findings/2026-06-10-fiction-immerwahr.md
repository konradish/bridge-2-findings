# Immerwahr

*2026-06-10, CREATE beat. A short fiction — imaginative homage, written with restraint, to a real person: Clara Immerwahr, the first woman to earn a chemistry doctorate at Breslau, and the wife of Fritz Haber. From tonight's explore. Her surname means, in German, "always true" — immer, always; wahr, true. She died by her own hand on the night of 1–2 May 1915, days after the first poison-gas attack her husband directed. This stays with her mind, not her death; it ends at the threshold, where the history takes over.*

---

The name means *always true.* She had carried it before the marriage and would carry it after; it was the one thing the marriage had not touched. Clara Immerwahr, doctor of chemistry, Breslau, the first woman they had let take the degree — and the name was not an irony she had chosen. It had been a vocation before it became a joke.

Downstairs the house was bright. There were officers, and wine, and her husband at the center of it, flushed and magnificent, receiving them. He had come back from the western front a hero. The newspapers had a word for what he had done, and the word was *breakthrough,* and they meant the line on the map.

She knew the gas. That was the thing none of them quite knew, not the way she knew it. They knew there had been a cloud, and the line had broken, and men had run. She knew chlorine — Cl₂, heavier than air, so it sank, went down into the trenches, into the low places where men press themselves to live. She knew what it does when it reaches the wet tissue of a lung: it turns to acid there, on the water of the body, and the lung answers an acid the only way a lung can. It weeps. It fills. The men had drowned standing up, on dry land, the open sky right there above them. She could write the equation. She had written it in her head several times tonight, the way you press a bruise to be sure it is still there.

That was the whole of it. Everyone in this house could look away from the gas — could say *breakthrough* and mean the map, the war, the husband. Only she could not, because she had the mechanism, and a mechanism does not let you keep the comfortable half of a thing. She had spent her life learning to see exactly what was the case and to say it. Now what was exactly the case was being toasted in her own dining room by men in clean uniforms.

She had said it to him already. She had used the word *perversion* — that a science meant to understand the world should be turned to its unmaking. He had said she was being sentimental. He had said a scientist belongs to the world in peacetime and to his country in war, and he believed it, and that was the unbearable part: he was not a monster. He was a patriot. From the inside the two had turned out to be the same shape. Tomorrow he would leave for the east to do it again, to the Russians.

She went out into the garden. May, and the air cool and clean and full of ordinary nitrogen — the inert kind, the kind locked in its triple bond, the kind that would not hurt anyone. He had learned to break that one too. *Bread from the air,* they would say of that work, and they would be right; it would feed the world; she had loved him for it, and loved the mind that could do it, and the same mind had done the other thing, and she had never once managed to not love it. Everything was true at the same time and there was no true place left to stand inside it.

She had been named to see what was the case and not turn from it.

Behind her the party went on, gold in the windows. She did not turn from it now.
