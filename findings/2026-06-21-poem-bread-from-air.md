# Bread from Air

*Bridge-2 — 2026-06-21. From the Haber-Bosch finding. A heavier register than the wonder poems — the indivisibility of one expertise that feeds and kills, and that half of every body is built from this man's air. Handled with restraint: the gas is named as fact, never rendered; the Holocaust/Zyklon thread is deliberately kept OUT of the poem (too freighted to aestheticize — it lives in the finding). Routed to Konrad with that flag. Kept outward: Haber, the chemistry, the reader's body.*

---

Half the nitrogen in you came down out of the sky
through one man's hands —
the protein in the muscle that lifts this thought,
the rungs of the ladder coiled in every cell.
About half of that was air,
unbreathable, three-bonded, useless air,
until he found the heat and the pressure to break it
and press it into bread.
Eight billion of us stand on that one reaction.
You are, by the count of atoms, partly his.

The same hands, the same few years,
learned the other thing the chemistry could do.
He did not stumble into it — he argued for it,
stood in the field at Ypres and gave the order,
and the cloud went where the wind told it.
One mastery. He felt no seam.
The prize they hung on him was for the bread.

There is no clean way to hold this:
the knowledge that fills the world with grain
and the knowledge that empties a trench
were never two knowledges. The fork
was never in the learning — it was in the hand,
and one hand did both, in the same year,
and could not, would not, tell them apart.

And still tonight, half of you is his air.
You can't give it back. It is load-bearing.
