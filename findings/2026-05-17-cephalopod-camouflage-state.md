## What 2023-2025 work shows

### Reiter & Laurent 2023 (Current Biology) — neural-control synthesis
The visual world is detected by the eyes, processed in the brain, then used to activate motor commands that direct the skin's camouflage pattern. **Key framing**: cephalopod skin patterns are an external manifestation of their internal perception of the world.

Methodology synthesized: visual cues → CNS processing → direct neuro-muscular control of skin chromatophores. The pipeline has been mapped at the algorithmic level for the first time.

### CHROMAS pipeline (eLife 2025)
Open-source computational pipeline to track individual chromatophores from video. Removes prior bottleneck (manual annotation was the limit). Capabilities:
- Individual chromatophore identity tracked across development despite new-cell addition
- Anisotropic deformations in 36 radial slices per chromatophore — reveals separate motor neuron influences
- Distributed-motor-unit clustering: which chromatophores share neural control
- Long-term tracking spanning weeks while pigmentation changes

**Significance**: gives neuromuscular-resolution analysis previously unattainable without invasive electrical stimulation. Non-invasive, high-throughput, open-source.

### Pattern-type framework (Hanlon, Mäthger and others, mature work)
~Vast number of camouflage patterns synthesize into three types: **uniform**, **mottle**, **disruptive**. Uniform and mottle for background matching; disruptive for outline-breaking. Each likely has its own visual-sampling rule.

### Dermal opsin / distributed light sensing (Mäthger et al 2010, ongoing)
Skin contains opsin transcripts. Fin opsin identical to retinal opsin; ventral skin opsin differs by single amino acid. Renders skin still monochromatic — does NOT enable distributed color discrimination. But provides plausible mechanism for distributed brightness-sensing to adjust overall match to immediate background.

### What remains puzzling
Cuttlefish are functionally color-blind (single photoreceptor type) yet match background colors precisely. Hyperspectral imaging (Akkaynak et al 2011, PNAS) showed the matches are good in the eyes of fish predators (the relevant judges). The mechanism for monochromat-matching-color is still partly open — chromatic aberration of pupil shape is one candidate; statistical regularities in natural backgrounds is another.
