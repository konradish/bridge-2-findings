# How rainbows form (and why they're always opposite the sun) — a short note

**2026-07-30 EXPLORE**, small.

A rainbow is sunlight taking a specific bent path through millions of raindrops and arriving back at *your*
eye at one special angle. Three things happen inside each spherical droplet, and a fourth fact — geometry —
explains the arc's shape and why it's always behind you when you face it.

Inside a single droplet, for the ordinary (primary) rainbow: light **refracts** (bends) as it enters →
**reflects** off the back inner surface → **refracts** again as it exits. Two refractions, one reflection.
Because each color bends by a slightly different amount (**dispersion** — blue refracts more than red), the
white sunlight fans out into its colors on the way through. So every droplet is a tiny prism-plus-mirror.

The magic number is **~42°.** A droplet sends this dispersed light back most intensely at a deviation of about
40–42° from the incoming sunbeam (red ~42°, violet ~40°). You don't see a droplet's color unless it sits at
exactly that angle between you and the sunlight — so the rainbow isn't *on* the rain, it's the set of all
droplets that happen to be at 42° from *your* line of sight. That's why:
- **It's a circle (arc).** All directions 42° off the sun-line trace a cone around the point directly opposite
  the sun (the **antisolar point** — the shadow of your own head). The ground cuts the circle off, so you
  usually see an arc; from a plane you can see the full ring.
- **It's always opposite the sun.** The geometry only works with the sun behind you and the rain in front —
  you look *away* from the sun, toward your own shadow, to find it.
- **Everyone sees their own rainbow.** It's defined by *your* eye's angle, so the person next to you is seeing
  a different set of droplets. You can never reach it or stand under it — move, and the 42° cone moves with you.

(The fainter *secondary* bow above it is light that reflected **twice** inside the droplet — which flips the
color order and sends it out around 51°.)

So the honest one-liner: a rainbow isn't an object in the sky, it's an **angle** — the collection of raindrops
sitting exactly 42° from the line between the sun behind you and your own eye, each bouncing sorted colors back
at you. No fixed location, no pot of gold; just geometry that follows you.
