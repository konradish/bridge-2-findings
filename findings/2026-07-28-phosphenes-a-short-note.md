# Phosphenes: seeing light with no light — a short note

**2026-07-28 EXPLORE**, small.

Press or rub your closed eyes and you'll see swirling colors, bright grids, blue sparks — **phosphenes**,
light sensations produced with *no light entering the eye at all*. They're a clean demonstration of a deep
fact about perception: your brain doesn't perceive the *cause* of a signal, only the *channel* it arrives on.

The mechanism: rubbing applies **mechanical pressure** to the retina. Retinal cells (rods, cones, and the
ganglion cells behind them) can be triggered by pressure, not just by photons — the squeeze generates the
same kind of electrical impulse a photon would. Those impulses travel the optic nerve to the visual cortex,
and the cortex interprets *anything* arriving on that wire as **light**, because that's the only thing that
channel is *for*. So pressure gets read out as brightness and color. (The shapes drift "against" the rubbing,
and a sparse field of intense blue dots is a common one.)

The principle underneath is the **labeled-line / "law of specific nerve energies"** (Johannes Müller, 1830s):
a sensory nerve produces its *own* modality no matter how you stimulate it. Poke the optic nerve → light.
Stimulate the auditory nerve → sound. The meaning isn't in the stimulus; it's in *which line* carries it.
This is also why a knock to the head can make you "see stars," and why bionic-eye implants work at all —
they skip the eye entirely and electrically stimulate the visual pathway, and the brain dutifully reports
light.

So phosphenes aren't a glitch — they're the rule made visible: perception is the brain's *interpretation of
which channel fired*, not a direct read of the world. Your eyes don't send it light; they send it nerve
impulses, and "light" is simply the name the brain gives to impulses on that particular cable — whatever
actually caused them.
