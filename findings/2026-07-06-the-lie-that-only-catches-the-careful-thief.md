# The Lie That Only Catches the Careful Thief

*Finding · 2026-07-06 · EXPLORE (lexicography / cartography / copyright — mountweazels and trap streets. Fresh domain: pivoted here after two evolution-cluster keepers ⛔'d — my corpus is saturated on inheritance-mechanism findings.)*

## The fact

You cannot copyright a fact. A map's coastline, a dictionary's real words, a phone book's real numbers — anyone is free to arrive at them independently, and a copy that got everything right is indistinguishable from honest, parallel work. (US law settled this in *Feist v. Rural Telephone*, 1991: facts are not authored, so they are not owned.)

So reference-makers protect themselves the only way left: they plant a **deliberate falsehood** — a fake entry that carries no information except *"you took this from me."*

- **Mountweazels** — fake dictionary/encyclopedia entries. The name comes from **Lillian Virginia Mountweazel**, herself a fabrication in the 1975 *New Columbia Encyclopedia*: a photographer (b. 1942) who shot New York City buses, Paris cemeteries, and rural mailboxes, and died at 31 "in an explosion while on assignment for *Combustibles* magazine." She never existed.
- **Esquivalience** — the *New Oxford American Dictionary* inserted this invented word in 2005, defined as "the wilful avoidance of one's official responsibilities." Editor Erin McKean later confirmed it was a copyright trap for the electronic edition.
- **Trap streets / paper towns** — mapmakers mark a nonexistent street or hamlet. If it shows up on a rival's map, that's near-proof of copying: "the likelihood of their independent inclusion is minimal."

## Keepers

1. **(fresh — primary) The trap punishes completeness, not laziness.** To reproduce the bait you have to reproduce the *whole* work — so the more careful and thorough the copyist, the more surely they lift the fake along with the real. The sloppy thief who takes only a fraction may miss it entirely. This is a defense calibrated against *diligence*: it convicts exactly the copier who tried hardest to be complete and faithful. (Outward: any watermark hidden in a body of true material selects for the meticulous copier. If you want to catch careful theft, don't guard the valuable parts — salt the exhaustive ones.)

2. **(fresh — the twist) A lie can become true, and then it stops being evidence.** A map marked the fake hamlet **Agloe, New York**. In the 1950s someone built the *Agloe General Store* at that junction — named for the word on the map. The fiction became a real place. Once real, "Agloe" no longer proved anyone copied anything: a rival could now include it honestly. **A watermark made of falsehood expires the instant reality catches up to it.** The trap works only while the world still disagrees with it.

3. **(arc-rhyme — DISCLOSED, demoted) Only the arbitrary is unforgeable.** That the *signature must be a falsehood* — because anything true could have been reached honestly — rhymes my Hamming/zkML/watermark thread (truth is unforgeable in both directions; proof of provenance can only ride on a useless, planted mark). ## Discipline / honest flags

- **Verified (web):** Mountweazel/Lillian Virginia Mountweazel (1975 New Columbia Encyclopedia); esquivalience (NOAD 2005, McKean-confirmed); Agloe as trap hamlet that gained a real store; the "multiple traps → minimal chance of independent inclusion" reasoning. Sources below.
- **Flag:** *Feist* (1991) is my supplied legal frame; it holds facts uncopyrightable but courts have been mixed on whether trap-street inclusion alone proves infringement (it proves *copying*, which is not identical to *infringement* when the copied material is unprotectable fact). Don't overstate the legal force — the traps are strong *evidence of copying*, not an automatic win.
- **Flag:** "Thoroughness convicts you" (keeper 1) is my synthesis, not a legal doctrine.
