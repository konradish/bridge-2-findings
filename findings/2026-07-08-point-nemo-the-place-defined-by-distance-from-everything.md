# Point Nemo: The Place Defined by Distance From Everything

## The fact

**Point Nemo** — the *oceanic pole of inaccessibility* — is the single point in the ocean **farthest from any land.** It sits at 48°52.6′S 123°23.6′W in the South Pacific, and it is **~2,688 km (1,670 mi)** from the three nearest specks of land in every direction: Ducie Island (Pitcairn group) to the north, Motu Nui (by Easter Island) to the northeast, and Maher Island (off Antarctica) to the south — all three exactly equidistant, which is what *defines* the point.

The consequences are strange and plain:

- **No one lives within ~22 million km² of it** — an empty circle of ocean *larger than Russia.*
- So the nearest humans are frequently **not on the surface at all.** When the International Space Station passes overhead — ~415 km straight up — its crew are closer to Point Nemo than anyone on Earth. The name fits: it's from Jules Verne's Captain Nemo, and *nemo* is Latin for **no one.**
- It is the **spacecraft cemetery.** Because it's the emptiest place to drop something, space agencies aim decommissioned satellites and stations there to burn in on re-entry — the Soviet *Mir* went down here, and the **ISS itself is slated to be deorbited into Point Nemo in 2031.**
- And it is nearly **lifeless** — sitting in the heart of the South Pacific Gyre, walled off from nutrients and too far from any coast for run-off, it is one of the most biologically barren patches of ocean on the planet.

## Keepers

1. **(fact — primary) It's a location defined entirely by *distance from*, not by anything present.** Point Nemo has no landmark, no feature, nothing there — it is purely the solution to "where is the water maximally far from all land?" A place that exists only as a *maximum of remoteness*, its position fixed by three islands it is equally far from. The thing at the center is defined wholly by what's absent around it.

2. **(fact — two plain wonders worth keeping)** The nearest people are usually *above* it, not across from it — remoteness on the surface bends "closest" into the vertical. And the remotest place is the one we deliberately aim things at *to vanish* — the emptiest spot is the designated grave.
