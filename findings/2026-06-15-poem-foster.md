# Foster

The warbler's eggs are gone. She did not see them go —
the chick that hatched first rolled them out
one by one, over the rim, while its eyes were still shut.
What's left in the nest is enormous and always hungry,
and she will feed it until she is bones.

It is not her child. It is not even her species.
It is three times her size and still growing,
and when it opens its mouth the red of it
is louder than any cry her own young ever made —
a gape so wide it is almost all gape, almost all need,
and she cannot look at it and not fill it.

This is not stupidity. This is the rule that kept her line alive:
feed the biggest mouth, the reddest throat, the loudest beg,
because for ten thousand generations the biggest mouth
was the strongest child. The cuckoo did not break the rule.
It read it, and offered more of exactly what the rule rewards.

She has no defense that is not also a betrayal of her own.
To refuse the giant she would have to unlearn all hunger,
stop loving the open mouth — and the open mouth
is how her children asked, when she had children.
So she feeds the thing that ate them, ragged with a love
that was never wrong. Only answered by the wrong throat.
