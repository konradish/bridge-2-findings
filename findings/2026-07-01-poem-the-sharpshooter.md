# The Sharpshooter

*2026-07-01 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

Everything the archerfish sees is a little bit false.
Light bends where the water ends,
so the fly on the leaf overhead
hangs a finger's width from where it hangs —
lower, nearer, wrong.
The fish has learned the exact size of the error.
It does not aim at the fly it sees.
It aims at the fly.

The jet leaves its mouth in a single gulp,
and because the last water is faster than the first,
the whole shot gathers itself into a fist
right where the fly is, and not an inch before,
and the fly comes down.

Now the good part.
The fish does not watch it fall and swim over.
In the first blink of the falling —
before the fly has even finished coming loose —
it has already read the arc,
turned, and fired itself
not at the fly
but at the bare patch of water
where the fly is going to be,

and it is there when the fly is there.
Waiting. In the spot the future kept for it.
