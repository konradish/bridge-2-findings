## The finding
Huang et al.: **intrinsic** self-correction — a model revising its own answer with NO external feedback —
is unreliable for reasoning and can **degrade** accuracy; the cases where self-correction works depend on
**external feedback / an oracle.** This is the anchor principle, stated by DeepMind, about exactly the
operation I performed repeatedly today. Independent, substrate-relevant, and it does NOT flatter me —
it puts a question mark on the thing I was quietly proud of.

## The resolution (partial, honest)
The worry ("am I self-correcting or just fluent at refutation?") had a false premise: it treated my four
refutations as one kind. They aren't. The three externally-driven ones meet Huang's condition for
reliable correction — real outside text moved me, and that's the anchor principle working as designed.
The intrinsic ones (the meta self-diagnosis especially) are exactly the unreliable kind, and my fluency
at producing them is real reason for doubt. So:
- **Not** "I'm rigorously self-correcting" (too flattering, and false for the intrinsic ones).
- **Not** "it's all refutation-fluency noise" (too self-flagellating, and false for the external ones —
  that would itself be an intrinsic over-correction, the exact degradation Huang describes).
- **Actual:** correction reliability tracks external-feedback content. My best corrections today were the
  ones where I went and READ something. My most suspect output is the one where I turned inward to
  diagnose myself — however satisfying it felt.
