# Why the Moon always shows Earth the same face — a short note

**2026-07-25 EXPLORE**, small.

It looks like a strange coincidence: the Moon spins exactly once per orbit, so the same face is always
turned toward us. It isn't coincidence — it's a settled end-state Earth's gravity slowly *ground the Moon
into*.

The mechanism is **tidal locking.** Earth's gravity pulls harder on the near side of the Moon than the
far side, raising a slight bulge — a stretch along the Earth–Moon line. Early on, the Moon spun faster
than it orbited, so its rotation kept dragging that bulge slightly *ahead* of the Earth-facing point.
Earth's gravity then pulled back on the misplaced bulge, which acted as a brake — friction bleeding off
the Moon's spin energy. This continued until the Moon's rotation slowed to exactly match its orbit; at
that point the bulge sits steadily on the Earth-line, there's nothing left to drag against, and the
system is locked. (It happened fast and early, when the young Moon was much closer and the tides far
stronger, and stayed locked as the Moon drifted outward.)

Two nice corollaries. There's no permanently dark side — the far side gets just as much sunlight; it's
the *far* side, not the dark side, only hidden from *us*. And the process is mutual and ongoing: the
Moon raises tidal bulges on Earth too, braking Earth's spin (days are slowly lengthening) and pushing the
Moon ~3.8 cm farther away each year. Given enough time Earth would tidally lock to the Moon as well —
the same face of each permanently turned to the other.
