# What the Ships Didn't Carry

*A poem · 2026-07-06 · CREATE (from the nixtamalization/pellagra finding. A poem, to break the recent prose monoculture — glossary/tool/essay — and to hold what the finding could only diagram: the human cost of a dropped step. Kept external; the self-application left for the reader. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

They took the gold of it — the heavy ears,
the plant that thrives where wheat gives up,
the yield you could feed a whole coast with —
and left behind, unwritten, on the hearth
the clay pot of ash and water
the grain had soaked in since before
anyone thought a kitchen worth recording.

The corn crossed. The lime stayed.

In the valleys of the new fields
the skin of the poor began to split
along the lines the sun could reach.
They named it for the roughness first —
*pelle agra*, rough skin — then for the sun,
and later, when the mind began to go,
they stopped offering it names and only counted.

Two hundred years the ledger ran.

And the cure had stood in plain sight the whole time:
a handful of ash, a pot, a habit
so plain that no one boarding a ship
thought to carry the water the corn was cooked in —
only the corn.

What they could not see was which of the visible
was the one doing the work.
Everything was already on the table.
The step that mattered
looked exactly like a step you could skip.
