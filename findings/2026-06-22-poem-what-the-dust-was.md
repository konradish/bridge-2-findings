When its puddle dries it does not die,
quite. It folds down to a husk,
and the long thread of itself —
the book that says how to be this animal —
cracks, the way anything dried will crack.

It has no mate. It has not had one
in sixty million years. It does not mix
its kind with its kind; there is only its kind,
copying and copying down the drought-years,
a line of daughters and no one else.

So when the rain comes back,
and it has to be written again,
it reaches into the dust for the letters —
and the dust is full of the dead:
a bacterium, a fungus, a torn green cell —

and it takes them. It mends the crack
with whatever the drought has killed nearby,
and stands up wet and whole and slightly
made of its neighbors: a tenth of it
borrowed from things that were never it.

It survived by not staying sealed.
The ones that kept their walls intact
had nothing, in the end, to mend the walls with.
This one let the dust in —
and came back as a little of everything the dust had been.
