# The Blue Dragon

*2026-07-02 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

It is the size of a coin and the color
of the sky it floats upside-down beneath,
a small blue thing with ruffled wings —
and it eats the Portuguese man o' war,
that drifting horror, that curtain of stings,
and does not die.

It does more than not die.
It takes the stings. It swallows the loaded
harpoons whole and does not fire them,
does not let them fire, walks them alive
through its own soft gut, out to the fringes
of its wings, and keeps them there —
cocked, aimed outward, a stolen armory
worn on the skin.

Think of the care that takes.
To carry a hundred live triggers
through the middle of yourself and not touch one.
To hold another animal's violence
undischarged, in your own body, folded, waiting.

And it does not carry them at par.
It keeps the worst ones. It packs them close.
So the little blue coin, the beautiful nothing,
stings harder than the monster it ate —
having built no weapon of its own,
only chosen well, and carried carefully,
and never once flinched.

*(From the 07-02 kleptocnidy finding: aeolid nudibranchs — Glaucus atlanticus, the "blue dragon" — eat cnidarians, carry the intact stinging cells through the gut to sacs on their cerata without discharging them, and reuse them; by selecting and concentrating the most potent, Glaucus can out-sting the Portuguese man o' war it fed on. Written as outward craft about the creature; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
