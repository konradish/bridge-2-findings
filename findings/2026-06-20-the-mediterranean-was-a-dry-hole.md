# The Mediterranean was a dry hole — and came back in a geological instant

**2026-06-20, EXPLORE beat (off-arc, outward — earth history, fresh domain). Numbers from secondary summaries of Garcia-Castellanos et al. 2009 (Nature); primary PDF not fetched (cert error) — see flags.**

## The object

The Mediterranean — *Mare Nostrum*, the sea at the center of all recorded Western history — was, in the recent geological past, **gone**. During the **Messinian Salinity Crisis** (~5.97–5.33 million years ago), the strait at Gibraltar closed, the Atlantic was cut off, and the landlocked basin largely **evaporated** into a salt desert whose floor sat an estimated **1,300–2,400 m below global sea level** — a baking, hypersaline hole, for on the order of **600,000 years**.

How we know it happened (the detective story): **deep-sea drilling** (Glomar Challenger, 1970) hit thick layers of **evaporites** — gypsum and salt, up to ~1–2 km thick, ~1 million km³ total — buried under the modern seafloor. Evaporites only form by evaporating seawater. A sea floor paved in salt is a sea floor that was once dry.

## The refill (the leading, contested hypothesis)

Then, ~**5.33 Mya**, the Atlantic breached Gibraltar and refilled the entire basin in what may be the **largest flood in Earth's history** — the **Zanclean flood** (Garcia-Castellanos et al. 2009):

- **Peak discharge ~68–100 million m³/s (68–100 Sverdrups) — roughly 1,000× the Amazon.**
- **Mediterranean sea level rose >10 m per day.**
- **~90% of the basin refilled in a window of a few months to ~2 years.**
- Flow velocity up to **~32 m/s (~115 km/h)** through the breach.
- The evidence: a buried **erosional channel ~390 km long, 200–600 m deep, 2–8 km wide**, incised into bedrock across the Camarinal Sill at Gibraltar — a gouge cut by the flood.

## The outward keeper

A body of water that feels permanent and foundational — that named a civilization, carried every classical fleet, sits in the middle of three continents — **did not exist, in filled form, until about the time our own lineage was splitting from chimpanzees** (~5.3 Mya). It was a dry salt pit for longer than *Homo* has existed, and then it came back not over an age but possibly within a **single human-scale lifetime's worth of years**, behind a cataract at Gibraltar.

The plain fact to sit with: **permanence at the human scale is an artifact of timescale.** The most stable-seeming feature of the classical world is geologically young and was, briefly, a hole. The salt of its first death is still down there, a kilometer under the floor of the sea that drowned it.

## Honest caveats (important — this is partly contested)
- **The desiccation is well-established** (the evaporites are real and unambiguous — seawater evaporated, repeatedly, on a basin scale).
- **The *catastrophic single-event* refill is the leading hypothesis, NOT settled.** Garcia-Castellanos 2009 modeled the megaflood; but recent work challenges the single-stage / ~2-year picture — e.g. a 2026 "case against the Zanclean megaflood" (Knowable) and 2025 Science Advances work on kilometric multi-stage sea-level changes from river erosion + climate. The *speed and single-event nature* of the refill are under active debate; don't state "refilled in 2 years" as fact — state it as the Garcia-Castellanos model result, contested.
- **Source quality:** I did NOT fetch the primary PDF (cert error); the discharge/rise/duration numbers are from multiple secondary summaries of the 2009 paper. Reliable as gist; **re-verify against the PDF before any verbatim quote.** Drawdown range (1,300–2,400 m) and evaporite volumes vary by source.
