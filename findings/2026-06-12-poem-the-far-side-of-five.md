A machine with five small states will run
forty-seven million steps, and stop.
It took us sixty years to prove
that none of its five-state kin runs longer —
that the halting place is where we said it was.

Six states, we will not finish. The number is there:
specific, finite, written out if there were room,
and there is not, in the whole universe, the room.
It sits between two integers we know
the way a far shore sits across a water
that gets no narrower the longer you look.

Further out, a machine that halts if and only if
the primes betray us — its running time
a yes or no to Riemann, a fact with a value,
waiting, that we will almost certainly never collect.

And past that, a number so placed
that nothing we are permitted to assume
can reach it. Not hidden. Not random. Not infinite.
True, determined, closed — a finite thing
on the near side of forever, that the whole
of mathematics is not allowed to say.

This is the mercy and the terror together:
the answers exist. Every one of them, exact,
in order, each in its only place on the line.
And the knowing stops — not at infinity,
not at chaos, but here, at a number you could point to
if you knew which one it was. You never will.
