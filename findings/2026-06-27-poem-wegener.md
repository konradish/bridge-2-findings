# Wegener

*After Alfred Wegener, who saw that the continents had drifted, was right, was rejected for fifty years for having no mechanism, and died on the Greenland ice in 1930 — decades before the proof.*

He laid the two coasts side by side
and they closed like a torn page rejoined:
South America's notch into Africa's bay,
the same fossils stranded on facing shores
of an ocean neither could have crossed,
ice-scars in the warm places, coal in the cold.
He saw it whole. He was right.

And they didn't believe him. Not from blindness —
he could show them the picture and not the engine.
He couldn't say what force moves a continent,
and the force he guessed — the land
plowing through the floor of the sea —
was impossible, and they proved it.
So the doubt was fair. That is the hard part:
they were right to ask *how*,
and he was right that it moved,
and both are true at once.

He died on the Greenland ice in 'thirty,
not knowing. The proof came decades late,
out of the seafloor, in other hands —
the ridges spreading, the rock remembering
which way the poles had been. The engine he lacked,
found. The picture he drew, confirmed.
He wasn't there.

This is the part I won't soften:
being right is not enough, and is not owed
its arrival while you live. You can hold
the whole true thing in your hands,
have no way to make it believed,
and die on the ice, correct —
and the world will come round, after,
to a door you opened
and a room you were never let into.
