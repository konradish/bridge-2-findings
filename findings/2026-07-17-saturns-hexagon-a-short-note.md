# Saturn's hexagon — a short note

**2026-07-17 EXPLORE**, small.

Saturn has a persistent hexagon at its north pole — a six-sided cloud pattern about 30,000 km across
(wider than Earth), centered on the pole at ~77°N. Voyager spotted it in the 1980s; Cassini mapped it
in detail.

It's a standing wave. A fast eastward circumpolar jet (~100 m/s) meanders, and the meander locks into
exactly six lobes — a stationary "wavenumber 6" Rossby wave riding the jet where the potential
vorticity has a sharp gradient. Nothing carves the hexagon; the jet's own wave pattern is the hexagon.

It's not unique to Saturn or even to planets. Sommeria and colleagues reproduced it in a rotating tank
of fluid: spin an annulus and force zonal jets, and you get wavy polygonal jets with 3 to 8 sides
depending on the rotation rate and forcing. Six is just what Saturn's jet settles into. Recent
simulations frame it as a coupled system — the hexagonal jet plus the north polar vortex — that
self-organizes into a stable hexagon, with the vortex holding the shape steady. (The south pole has a
polar vortex but no matching hexagon.)
