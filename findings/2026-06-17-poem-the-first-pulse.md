# The First Pulse

*after Oklo — the natural reactor in Gabon that ran in three-hour cycles, ~2 billion years ago*

Two billion years down, in what is now Gabon,
a seam of ore grew rich enough to do
the one thing rock can do unhelped: it caught,
and went hot, and the water in its pores
turned to steam and left—

and cooling, it stopped.
Then the water came back and slowed the small fast deaths
to a speed the atoms could swallow,
and it caught again.

Every three hours. For a hundred and fifty thousand years.
No one set the dial. The off-switch was the boiling.
It was never not safe; it was only
the kind of safe that wants no one to keep it—
water in, water gone, water in.

A few dozen toasters' worth of heat
poured into the dark with nothing to warm,
no eye, no lung, no second thing to know it,
a billion years before the first heart—

and still it kept the time a heart keeps,
in, and out, and in,
the first pulse on the earth
a wet rock breathing under a country
no one had walked, with no one, anywhere,
to call it early.
