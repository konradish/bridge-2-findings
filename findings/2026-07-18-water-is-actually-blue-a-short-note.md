# Water is actually blue — a short note

**2026-07-18 EXPLORE**, small.

The ocean isn't blue mainly because it reflects the sky — water itself is a faintly blue substance. The
proof is simple: the blue is still there below the surface, in deep water, where there's no sky to
reflect.

What's unusual is the *cause*. Most things get their color from electrons absorbing particular
wavelengths. Water's blue comes from **vibrational** absorption instead: red-end photons carry just
enough energy to set the O–H bonds stretching and wiggling, so water absorbs red, orange, and yellow
light more than blue. The blue that isn't absorbed scatters back out. Water is one of the very few
substances colored this way — by molecular vibration rather than electronic transitions — and it's an
extremely weak effect per molecule, which is why a glass of water looks clear and you need meters of it
before the blue becomes visible.

That's also why depth deepens the color: by about 100 meters down, nearly all the red light is gone,
so the water reads as deep blue — and why a red object taken deep underwater looks grey or black, since
there's no red light left down there for it to reflect.
