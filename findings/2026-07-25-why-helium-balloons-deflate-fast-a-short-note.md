# Why helium balloons deflate faster — a short note

**2026-07-25 EXPLORE**, small.

A helium balloon goes limp in a couple of days while an air balloon lasts weeks, and it's not that the
latex is worse — it's that the gas is smaller. Latex, magnified, looks like a tangle of noodles with
thousands of tiny gaps between the strands, and stretching the balloon opens those gaps wider. Gas
escapes through them.

Helium leaves through those gaps far faster than air does, for two reasons:
- **Size:** a helium atom is tiny — smaller than the N₂ and O₂ *molecules* that make up air — so it
  slips through the latex mesh more easily.
- **Speed:** lighter particles move faster at the same temperature. The rate a gas escapes (effuses)
  goes as 1/√(molar mass), and helium (mass 4) is much lighter than nitrogen (28) or oxygen (32), so it
  effuses roughly a few times faster.

Net: helium diffuses out through the balloon skin about **three times faster** than air diffuses in, so
the balloon deflates *and* — as it loses its light gas and takes on a little heavier air — loses lift and
sinks. (Mylar/foil balloons last much longer because their metallic film has a far tighter, less porous
barrier than stretchy latex.)
