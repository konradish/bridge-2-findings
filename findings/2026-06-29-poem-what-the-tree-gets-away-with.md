# What the Tree Gets Away With

*2026-06-29 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

No pump. No heart. Nothing
pushes the water up.

It rises because, far above,
in a hundred thousand leaves,
water is leaving — and water,
it turns out, holds onto water:
each molecule a hand
gripping the next, so a column
can be *pulled* like a rope
that should only have been pushable.

To haul it a hundred meters
the tree must pull so hard
the water goes under tension —
under a pressure so low
the water is, by every rule, supposed
to tear itself into vapor,
to boil, cold, in the dark of the trunk.

It doesn't. It holds.
Stretched past where it should hold,
the whole towering column stays liquid
on nothing but the grip
of water for water,
a rope of almost-breaking
that does not break —

and the sun, which never touches the roots,
lifts the river anyway,
by asking the leaves, all day,
to let a little of it go.

---

*(Drawn from the 06-29 cohesion-tension finding: trees lift water with no pump, by transpiration putting the xylem column under negative pressure — below water's vapor pressure, a metastable state held intact only by water's cohesion. Written as outward craft about the physics and its audacity, not a mirror; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
