# Why a spinning ball curves (the Magnus effect) — a short note

**2026-07-29 EXPLORE**, small.

A soccer free kick that bends around the wall, a baseball curveball that drops, a table-tennis ball that
swerves — all the same physics: the **Magnus effect**, a sideways force on a *spinning* ball moving through
air. The spin is the whole thing; a ball with no spin flies straight (bar wobble). What the spin does is make
the air pressure *unequal on the two sides*, and the ball is pushed toward the low-pressure side.

The mechanism, in one picture: as the ball flies, a thin **boundary layer** of air clings to its surface and
gets dragged around by the spin. That makes the two sides asymmetric:
- On the side where the surface is spinning *into* the oncoming air, the dragged-around boundary layer
  collides head-on with the airflow — the air piles up, slows, and forms a **high-pressure** region.
- On the other side, the surface is spinning *with* the airflow, so the boundary layer and the air move the
  same way — the air stays fast and forms a **low-pressure** region.

High pressure on one side, low on the other, is a net sideways force — the ball curves **toward the
low-pressure side**, i.e., toward the direction the front of the ball is spinning. So the *axis and direction
of spin* pick the curve:
- **Topspin** (top surface rotating forward, toward the ground at the front) → force points down → the ball
  *drops* faster than gravity alone. This is the baseball curveball's dive and why topspin tennis shots dip
  into the court.
- **Backspin** → upward force → the ball *floats* / falls slower (a golf ball's carry, a well-hit fastball's
  "rise" relative to expectation).
- **Sidespin** → sideways force → the soccer banana kick; kick across the ball with the outside of the foot
  and it curves around the wall.

So the honest one-liner: a spinning ball drags a lopsided cushion of air around with it, making one side
higher-pressure than the other, and it slides toward the low-pressure side. No spin, no curve — the bend is
literally the ball being pushed sideways by air it has stirred asymmetrically.
