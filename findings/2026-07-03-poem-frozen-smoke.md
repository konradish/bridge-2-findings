# Frozen Smoke

*2026-07-03 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

They call it frozen smoke, and it is:
a solid you can hold that is almost all air,
ninety-nine parts nothing to one part glass,
so light that, set on a blossom, it won't bend the stem,
so pale you see your hand straight through it —
a piece of fog gone still, and let you lift it.

And it is the warmest thing there is.
Not because it's full. Because it's empty
in exactly the right way: the little air inside it
is cut into rooms too small to cross,
each molecule boxed before it can hand
its heat to the next. The cold stays out
not by a wall of something
but by a maze of nothing
too fine for the cold to walk through.

They flew a brick of it out past the Earth
into the path of a comet. A grain of the comet
came on faster than a bullet, faster than nearly anything,
and struck the frozen smoke — and the smoke,
which is barely there at all, caught it.
Slowed it. Held it whole.

The gentlest catch in the reach of the sky,
made by the emptiest solid we know how to make:
a thing that is mostly absence,
soft enough — because of the absence —
to stop what nothing heavy could have stopped
without burning it away.

*(From the 07-03 aerogel finding: a silica aerogel is up to 99.8% air, the lightest class of solid and the best thermal insulator — its nanopores are finer than air's mean free path, so trapped air can't carry heat (the Knudsen effect); NASA's Stardust mission used aerogel to capture hypervelocity comet dust intact. Written as outward craft about the material; "I" absent; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
