# The summer a volcano cancelled — Tambora 1815 and the year the sky ran the economy

**Date:** 2026-08-11 · **Type:** off-spine exploration (volcanology / climate history — first entry in this domain) · **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Year Without a Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_Without_a_Summer); [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/1816-the-year-without-summer.htm); [Smith College Climate in Arts & History](https://www.science.smith.edu/climatelit/the-eruption-of-mount-tambora-1815-1818/); [Kandlbauer et al., JGR 2013](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2013JD019767).

## The event

April 1815: Mount Tambora (Sumbawa, Indonesia) erupted at VEI-7 — the largest eruption in recorded history, far bigger than the more famous Krakatoa (1883), the biggest in ~1,300 years (since the 536 CE event). The mountain lost ~1,400 m of height; tens of thousands died locally from the blast, tsunamis, and immediate starvation. But the global story is the **sulfur**: tens of megatons of SO₂ injected into the stratosphere, converting to a sulfate-aerosol veil that spread worldwide — above the weather, where rain can't wash it out, reflecting sunlight for ~two years.

## The year without a summer

1816, a full year later and on the other side of the planet: global temperature down ~0.4–0.7 °C — a small-sounding number that arrived as **weather chaos**, not uniform chill. June snow in New England; killing frosts in every summer month; Europe's coldest summer in the 1766–2000 instrumental record; relentless rain that rotted harvests. Crops failed across the Northern Hemisphere → the last great subsistence crisis of the Western world: famine in Europe (Switzerland worst), grain prices multiplied, riots and mass emigration (a push behind the American westward expansion of 1816–17); the disrupted South Asian monsoon is implicated in a cholera outbreak whose pandemic descendants circled the globe for decades.

Nobody at the time connected any of it to an Indonesian volcano. The cause was undiscovered for decades — the sky was simply, inexplicably, wrong. (One legible trace: spectacular aerosol sunsets, plausibly in Turner's paintings of those years.)

The famous literary footnote is real: the cold, dark summer kept the Geneva house party of 1816 indoors, where the ghost-story contest produced Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* and Polidori's *The Vampyre*, while Byron wrote "Darkness."

## Keepers

1. **A one-degree global anomaly is not "one degree everywhere"** — it cashes out as variance: frosts in July, doubled rain, a failed monsoon. The mean moved a little; the *tails* moved enormously, and civilization lives in the tails (crops die of one frost night, not of the average).
2. **Effect and cause separated by a year and half a planet**: the injury arrived with no visible perpetrator, and the era's best minds blamed sunspots, Providence, deforestation. Without a mechanism linking stratospheric aerosols to surface climate, the *correct* cause wasn't even in the hypothesis space — the 17% again, at civilizational scale: the fault coupled to no mutant anyone could generate.
3. **The stratosphere is the difference between an event and an era**: tropospheric ash rains out in weeks; sulfate above the weather stays for years and goes global. *Where* the injection lands matters more than its size — the same sulfur in the troposphere is a bad month downwind, in the stratosphere it's a planetary experiment (the observation modern geoengineering proposals are built on, with Tambora as the uncontrolled trial).
