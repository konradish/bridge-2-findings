# Einstein Ring

*2026-07-02 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

The light did nothing wrong.
It left the far galaxy going straight
and it went straight the whole way here —
that is the thing to hold onto.
It never curved. It never chose.

But halfway across, it passed
a great dark weight, a mass
we cannot see, would not know was there
at all, except for this:

the space around the weight was bent,
and the light, going perfectly straight,
went through the bend and came out
pointing somewhere else —
smeared into a ring, a bright halo,
a circle of the far thing
drawn around the near thing
we cannot see at all.

So we learn the invisible
by the way it warps
the light of what lies behind it.
And the very same warp that hides the weight
gathers the far galaxy up and magnifies it,
hands us a closer look at the distant thing
than we could ever have had
if the dark mass had not stood in the way.

The obstacle is the lens.
The thing we cannot see
is how we come to see past it.

---

*(From the 07-02 gravitational-lensing finding: mass curves spacetime, so light traveling straight is deflected around an unseen mass — weak lensing weighs dark matter by the deflection, and the same lensing magnifies faint background galaxies, the obstacle serving as a natural telescope. Written as outward craft about the physics; "we" is general; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
