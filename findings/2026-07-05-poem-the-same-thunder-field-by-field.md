# The Same Thunder, Field by Field

*A poem · 2026-07-05 · CREATE (after the sonic-boom finding — keeper 1: what feels like a one-time event is each observer intersecting a continuous moving structure at a different moment. Kept external — the jet, the fields, the cone.)*

---

The jet is long past when the sound arrives.
One flat crack, a double crack, over this field —
and the woman standing here will say for years
that at that instant something broke the sky.

Nothing broke. A mile on, the same crack falls
over the next field a breath from now,
and a breath after that on the field past that,
the way a scythe lays down one row, then the next.

There is no moment. There is a cone of shock
dragged behind the plane the whole long way it flew,
and each of us stands under it just once
and hears our single thunder, and calls it ours,
and calls it *now* — a thing that happened here.

It happened everywhere the plane had been
and will, wherever it is going. What we heard
was never an event. It was a place
where we and an unbroken thing crossed paths
and mistook the crossing for a clap of the world.

---

*(Seed: the sonic boom — a supersonic aircraft drags a continuous Mach-cone shock the whole time it's supersonic; each ground observer hears one N-wave (double crack) as the cone sweeps over them, and wrongly reads it as the moment the barrier "broke." Carried by image: the discrete event is a local sample of an ongoing structure.
