## The fact

Periodical cicadas (*Magicicada*) in the eastern US spend almost their entire lives underground as nymphs, then emerge all at once, in enormous synchronized numbers, for a few weeks to mate — and they do it on a clock of **13 or 17 years**. Both are **prime**, and the leading explanation says that is not a coincidence: the cicada's main defense is *a number*.

The logic is coincidence-avoidance by **coprimality**. Any predator or parasite with its own shorter periodic cycle (2, 3, 4, 5, 6 years) will have its population peak coincide with a cicada emergence only once every **lowest common multiple** of the two cycles. For a prime cicada period *p* and a smaller cycle *k* that doesn't divide it, that LCM is *p × k* — large. A **composite** cicada cycle would be far worse: a 12-year cicada shares factors with 2-, 3-, 4-, and 6-year predators and would meet *each* of them every 12 years; a 14-year cicada meets 2- and 7-year cycles often. A large prime shares a divisor with almost nothing, so its vulnerable mass-emergence almost never lands on an enemy's capable year. (The two prime broods themselves, 13 and 17, co-emerge only once every **221 years** — 13 × 17.)

It pairs with **synchrony**: emerging all together in overwhelming numbers means predators are *satiated* — they physically cannot eat more than a fraction — and, because the feast then vanishes for 13–17 years, no predator can sustain a population specialized to exploit it. A coincidence-minimizing period plus a satiating glut: the enemy can neither track the beat nor live off it.

## Keepers

1. **A defense can be arithmetic: pick a rhythm coprime to your adversary's, so your vulnerable moments almost never align with its capable ones.** The cicada has no speed, armor, or venom for this — it has a *prime*. When you can't out-fight a *recurring* threat, you can sometimes out-*schedule* it: choose a period that shares as little structure (as few divisors) as possible with the threat's period, and the coincidences become rare. Primality is the mathematically optimal version — it minimizes alignment with the largest set of competing cycles at once. The safety isn't in any single emergence; it's in the *spacing*.
2. **Be everywhere at once, then nowhere for years.** (Synchrony + satiation.) The coprime period works *with* mass simultaneous abundance: too many to eat, then gone too long to specialize on. Neither alone is as strong as the pair. *(Secondary — brushes the predator-satiation/masting idea; foregrounded keeper 1, the coprimality, which is the fresh part.)*

## Outward lesson

Against a *periodic* adversary you can't beat head-on, the lever may be the calendar, not the contest: adopt a cycle that shares minimal common structure with the threat's cycle, so your exposed phases and its effective phases rarely meet. Coprimality (ideally a large prime) is the optimal anti-alignment — it spreads the next coincidence with *every* shorter cycle as far out as arithmetic allows. The defense lives in the relationship between the two periods, not in either one alone.

## Verified / flagged

- **Solid:** *Magicicada* 13- and 17-year cycles (both prime); mass synchronized emergence; predator satiation; the LCM/coprimality math (12 shares 2,3,4,6; 13 & 17 co-emerge every 221 yr). Standard.
- **Flag — the "prime = predator-avoidance" story is the LEADING hypothesis, not settled.** First formally proposed by Lloyd & Dybas (1966), popularized by Stephen Jay Gould (1977). **Real objections:** (a) it can't explain the *rarity* of periodicity — all cicadas face predation, yet only a few species are periodical; (b) it posits predator cycles whose past existence is hard to verify empirically. **Competing/complementary model:** Jin Yoshimura (1990s) — prime cycles arise from selection against **hybridization** (broods of different cycle lengths interbreed only at their LCM; primes keep the cycles maximally separated, important under ice-age climate pressure). Likely multiple pressures; don't present predator-avoidance as proven.
