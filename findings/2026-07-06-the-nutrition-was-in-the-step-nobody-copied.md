## The fact

Maize is a near-complete staple with one trap: 50–80% of its niacin (vitamin B3) is chemically **bound** — as niacytin — in a form the human gut cannot absorb, and its tryptophan (which the body can convert to niacin) is also poorly available. Eat maize as your dietary center without fixing this, and you get **pellagra**: the "four Ds" — dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia, death.

Mesoamerican cultures fixed it thousands of years ago with **nixtamalization**: soak and cook the dried kernels in an **alkaline solution** (limewater, or wood ash). The alkalinity releases the bound niacin into an absorbable form, frees up amino acids, and improves the protein. It's the step that turns maize from a staple that slowly starves you of B3 into one that doesn't. In Mesoamerica, where the technique was universal, **pellagra was essentially unknown.**

Then maize left the Americas. Spanish explorers carried the *crop* back — it was high-yield, easy to grow, and spread fast across southern Europe, Africa, and later the American South. They did **not** carry the *technique*. And so pellagra, a disease with **no record in the pre-Columbian Americas**, appeared on the world stage for the first time — and killed for two centuries, into the 1900s, until Joseph Goldberger traced it to diet (not, as assumed, an infection). It was, in effect, a vast accidental controlled experiment: take the food, drop one preparation step, watch a deficiency disease materialize where it had never existed.

## Keepers

1. **(fact — primary) The nutrition lived in the process, not the crop.** The very same kernel is a sound staple or a slow poison depending on an invisible chemical step. "How nourishing is maize?" has no answer as a property of maize — only of maize-plus-its-preparation. Europe copied the object and dropped the step, and the ledger came due in millions of cases.

2. **(the distinct sub-angle — genuinely fresh) Transmission failed not from secrecy but from mis-seen salience.** This is where it *diverges* from a classic lost-technique story (my "Greek fire" finding, where a secret died because it was deliberately hidden). Nixtamalization was never hidden — anyone in a Mesoamerican kitchen could watch the whole thing. It failed to transmit anyway, because **a watcher couldn't tell the load-bearing step (the lime) from incidental culinary custom.** The alkali looked like a flavor or texture choice, not a chemical necessity, so it was dropped as inessential. You can observe an entire process in full daylight and still copy the wrong subset — if you can't see which step is doing the work. (This rhymes the mountweazel keeper from earlier today: you have to reproduce the *whole* to reproduce what matters, because you don't know in advance which part is the bait.)

3. **(arc-rhyme DISCLOSED) Knowledge-transmission loss** rhymes "Greek fire." Kept because the *cause* inverts (secrecy → undervaluation), which is the new part; not re-served as fresh.
