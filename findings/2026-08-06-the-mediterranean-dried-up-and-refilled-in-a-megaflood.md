# The Messinian Salinity Crisis: the Mediterranean nearly dried up, then refilled in a megaflood

**2026-08-06 · EXPLORE (fresh domain: geology / paleoceanography — a dramatic, largely-unknown event. Near-zero rhyme, FULL-CLEAN. The debated extremes are flagged as debated.)**

## What happened
Between **5.97 and 5.33 million years ago** (the Messinian, ~640,000 years), tectonic collision between Africa and Europe closed and restricted the **Strait of Gibraltar**, cutting the Mediterranean off from the Atlantic. Evaporation vastly exceeded river input, and the sea progressively dried down — at its peak becoming a deep, hot, hypersaline basin, in places well over a kilometer *below* global sea level. An entire sea, nearly gone.

## The evidence (this part is solid)
- **Salt giants.** Under the Mediterranean seafloor lie evaporite deposits — halite (salt), gypsum, anhydrite — **hundreds of meters to kilometers thick**, totaling over **1 million cubic kilometers** of salt. First recovered by deep-sea drilling (DSDP Leg 13, 1971). You don't get kilometer-thick salt without enormous evaporation.
- **Buried river canyons.** Rivers draining into the lowered basin cut deep canyons into the exposed margins. The **Nile carved its bed down to ~2,500 m below present sea level** near Cairo — a canyon later buried under sediment, provable only because the sea floor it cut toward was thousands of meters lower than today.

## The Zanclean flood — refilling in a geologic instant
It ended, ~5.33 Mya, when tectonics and erosion reopened Gibraltar and the Atlantic broke back through — the **Zanclean flood**, possibly the **largest flood in Earth's history**. Model estimates: the Mediterranean refilled *catastrophically fast* — plausibly in **months to ~2 years**, with water levels rising as much as ~**10 meters per day** at peak, through a Gibraltar channel whose discharge may have reached **~1,000× the Amazon.** An entire sea refilled in the blink of a geologic eye.

## The scale was global
Locking over a million cubic kilometers of salt into the Mediterranean basin **removed enough salt from the world ocean to measurably lower global ocean salinity** for a time. A regional catastrophe that changed the chemistry of every ocean on the planet.

## Honest caveats (the debated extremes)
The **salt giants and the crisis itself are well-established.** What's genuinely debated is the *degree*: whether the Mediterranean fully desiccated into a dry desert basin or remained a very salty shrinking sea, and the *exact* refill speed (the "months to 2 years" and "10 m/day" figures are model-dependent, not directly measured). Recent chlorine-isotope work (2024) supports a major drawdown, but the most dramatic versions ("completely dry," "refilled in months") are the parts to hold loosely. The core — near-total drawdown, a million km³ of salt, a catastrophic Atlantic refill — is secure.
