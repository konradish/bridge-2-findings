# Why ~90% of humans are right-handed — a short note

**2026-07-28 EXPLORE**, small.

About **9 in 10 people are right-handed, in essentially every society ever studied** — and the striking part
isn't the preference itself but that it's *lopsided at the population level*. Many animals have individual
paw preferences, but their populations split ~50/50. Humans are the outlier: a whole species leaning one way.
That asymmetry, not handedness per se, is what needs explaining.

The leading link is **brain lateralization**. The two hemispheres divide labor, and each controls the
*opposite* side of the body. In most people the **left hemisphere** is dominant for **language** and for
fine motor sequencing — and the left hemisphere drives the right hand. So the population-wide language
specialization in the left hemisphere is thought to drag hand dominance to the right along with it. Fittingly,
left-handers tend to have *more varied* wiring — language spread more evenly across both hemispheres — rather
than a clean mirror-image of right-handers. It's less "the other setting" and more "a looser, less
one-sided brain organization."

What makes a given person left-handed is only *partly* genetic. Twin studies attribute ~21–24% of the
liability to additive genetic effects — real, but a minority share. The rest is developmental and
environmental: prenatal conditions, and hormone exposure (testosterone levels in the womb may nudge
lateralization). This is why identical twins are often *discordant* for handedness — same genes, different
hands — which by itself proves genetics can't be the whole story.

The evolutionary backdrop is deep: comparative primate work suggests the neurological scaffolding for
human-style lateralization was laid across tens of millions of years (major shifts ~30 Mya in ape ancestors,
more reorganization ~10 Mya in the Homo–Pan lineage). So right-handedness isn't a recent human quirk — it's
the surface reading of a very old, gradually built brain asymmetry.

So the honest one-liner: we're not right-handed because the right hand is special — we're right-handed
because the *left brain* got specialized for language and fine control, and it steers the right hand. The
10% who don't follow simply have a less one-sided brain.
