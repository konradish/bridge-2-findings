## The fact

Before **Nathaniel Ward**'s sealed glass case (~1830s), roughly **95% of live plants died** on the months-long sea voyage between continents — salt spray, temperature swings, no fresh water, darkness in the hold. The Wardian case, a glazed box that recirculates its own moisture and admits light, changed that: plants could ride on deck and arrive alive.

That sounds like a gardening story. It was a **geopolitical** one — because the case dissolved the thing that had quietly protected every crop's homeland: *you could not move a living plant.* Once you could, entire industries were **stolen and relocated**:

- **Tea.** In 1848 the East India Company sent **Robert Fortune** into China in disguise; he smuggled out ~13,000–20,000 tea plants and Chinese cultivation knowledge in Wardian cases, and established tea in Darjeeling — breaking **China's monopoly.** He is remembered as "the tea thief."
- **Rubber.** In 1876 **Henry Wickham** removed **70,000 rubber seeds** from Brazil to Kew; the seedlings seeded the plantations of Ceylon and Malaya, ending the Amazon rubber boom. In Brazil he is a **"bio-pirate."**
- **Quinine.** Cinchona, the anti-malarial, was taken from the guarded slopes of the **Andes** to British India and Dutch **Java** — which became the world's quinine source, and quinine is what let Europeans survive to colonise the malarial tropics.

## Keepers

1. **(fact — primary) The moat protecting these monopolies was never law. It was physics — and a glass box drained it.** China didn't keep tea by statute; it kept tea because a live tea plant couldn't survive to India. Brazil didn't hold rubber by treaty; it held it because the seeds spoiled at sea. Their advantage was defended entirely by *difficulty*, not by *right* — and difficulty is exactly what a small enabling technology erases. When the only thing guarding your edge is that it's hard to take, you have no protection at all against the invention that makes it easy; there is no law standing behind a moat that was only ever made of circumstance.

2. **(fact — the human ledger) A trivial object relocated the wealth of nations, mostly by theft.** The box cost little and did one modest thing (keep a plant alive at sea). Through it flowed the transfer of tea, rubber, and quinine — and the fortunes, plantations, uprooted peoples, and razed habitats that came with them — from native lands to colonial ones. The scale of the consequence had no relation to the size of the cause.
