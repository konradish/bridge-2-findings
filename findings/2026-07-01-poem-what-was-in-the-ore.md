# What Was in the Ore

*2026-07-01 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

For a thousand years the blades came out of the fire
better than they had any right to be —
an edge that held, a body that would bend
and not break, and across the flat of the steel
that watered pattern, like something poured,
like the grain of a slow river seen from above.

The smiths knew what they did. They knew the heat,
the number of foldings, the color to quench at,
the little cakes of wootz that came from the east.
They taught it to their sons exactly,
and their sons did it exactly,
and somewhere in the seventeen-hundreds
the swords came out ordinary. Just steel.

Nobody had changed a thing.
The hammer fell the same, the fire was the same,
the hands were the same hands.
What had changed was a mine, far off,
a seam of ore run empty —
and in that ore, in amounts too small to see
or name or ever think to ask for,
a trace of something (we call it vanadium now)
had been doing the quiet work all along.

The magic was never in the making.
It was in the dirt. And the dirt gave out,
and the men who could no longer do the thing
never learned what the thing had been.
They only knew their hands, which had not failed them,
had somehow stopped being enough.

---

*(From the 07-01 Damascus-steel finding: wootz blades depended on trace impurities — vanadium especially — in a specific ore; when the ore depleted in the 18th century, the same forging produced ordinary steel and the technique was lost. Written as outward craft about the smiths and the ore — the essential thing being invisible and unnamed — not a mirror; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
