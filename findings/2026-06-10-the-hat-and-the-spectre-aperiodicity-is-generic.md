## The problem (open ~50 years)
An **aperiodic monotile** ("einstein" — German *ein Stein*, "one stone," not Albert): a single shape that tiles the infinite plane but **never periodically**. Aperiodic *sets* shrank over decades — Berger 1964 (20,426 Wang tiles) → Robinson 1971 (6) → **Penrose 1974 (2 tiles + matching rules)** — but whether ONE tile could do it stood open until **March 2023**, when **David Smith (a retired print technician / shape hobbyist)** found "the hat," with Myers, Kaplan & Goodman-Strauss proving it (arXiv:2303.10798).

## The shape
The **hat** is a humble **13-sided polykite**: take a hexagonal grid, cut each hexagon into 6 kites, glue 8 kites from neighbouring hexagons. Nothing exotic-looking. Penrose needed **two** tiles plus matching rules to force non-repetition; **the hat puts the matching rules into the shape itself** — its geometry alone forces the structure.

## Why one tile forces aperiodicity (two independent proofs)
1. **Combinatorial / hierarchical (computer-assisted):** hats (and anti-hats) assemble into 4 **metatiles** (T, H, F, P); metatiles assemble into **supertiles**; supertiles into second-order supertiles, *ad infinitum*, with the same adjacency combinatorics at every level. A tiling forced to be **self-similar at all scales** can have no translational period — hierarchy ⇒ aperiodicity.
2. **Geometric incommensurability (the deeper "huh"):** the hat is **not a lone magic shape** — it is one member of a **continuum** of combinatorially-equivalent polygons, **Tile(a,b)**, parameterized by two edge lengths. A "new kind of geometric incommensurability argument" proves **generic members of the continuum are aperiodic**. The two *degenerate* endpoints — the **"comet" Tile(1,0)** and **"chevron" Tile(0,1)** — tile *periodically* [from the secondary accounts; my abstract fetch confirmed only "generic members aperiodic," not the endpoints by name]. **So in this family, periodicity is the measure-zero special case and aperiodicity is generic** — an inversion of the usual intuition that aperiodic tilings (Penrose) are the exotic rarity. Generically the tile's two length-scales are incommensurable, and no lattice can accommodate an incommensurable ratio.

## The reflection wrinkle → the Spectre
Unsatisfying detail: the hat needs its **mirror image** — about **1 in 6 tiles must be reflected** ("anti-hats"). Open question: can a single shape tile aperiodically with **rotations + translations only**, no reflections?
- **Tile(1,1)** (the equilateral midpoint of the hat–turtle continuum) is **weakly chiral**: aperiodic *if reflections are forbidden by fiat* — but it can tile periodically using its mirror, so the ban is a rule, not geometry.
- **The Spectre** (arXiv:2305.17743, May 2023): **replace each straight edge of Tile(1,1) with an asymmetric curve, oriented consistently.** The wiggly edge **physically cannot mate with its own mirror image**, so reflected copies are excluded by *geometry*, not by rule. Result: a **strictly chiral aperiodic monotile** — tiles the plane with rotations+translations alone, only ever aperiodically, even when reflections are permitted. You forbid reflection by making the tile unable to kiss its own mirror.
