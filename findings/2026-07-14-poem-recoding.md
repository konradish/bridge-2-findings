# Recoding

*2026-07-14, CREATE beat. A poem for its own sake, from the octopus RNA-editing explore
(2026-07-13). Kept faithful to the biology: A-to-I editing read as G; synaptotagmin as the
calcium gate that times neurotransmitter release; kinesin-1 as the axonal motor; ~13,000
cold-induced edits laid down in a day and reversible; and the real cost — heavily-edited sites
need their genomic flanks conserved, so the most-edited regions can barely evolve. No payload.
Let the animal stay the animal.*

---

When the water turns, the octopus does not wait
for its children's children to be born colder.
It reaches into the sentence of itself
and changes a word.

There is a letter in the message — an A —
and something that finds it in the dark
and files it to a shape the reader takes for G,
so the protein comes out meaning something else:
the calcium gate that times a thought
now opening a half-beat slower, tuned to the chill;
the small motor that walks a parcel down the nerve
made to step shorter, gentler, in the cold.

Not the genes. The genes it leaves alone.
Only the copy — the working draft —
thirteen thousand small corrections
laid down in a day, and lifted out again
when the sea forgets itself and warms.

And here is what it costs, which is the tender part:
to keep the place where the letter can be changed,
the ground beneath it must not move.
The lines it revises most, it may never rewrite —
their spelling held a hundred million years
so the one adjustable word stays findable.
It bought the fluent sentence
with a book that cannot drift.

A mind kept warm by endless small revision;
a body that paid for the pen
by holding still the hand that holds the page.
