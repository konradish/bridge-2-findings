## The fact

A tardigrade can lose ~all its body water and survive — the state is called anhydrobiosis. The long-standing explanation was chemical: the sugar **trehalose** either forms a glass (vitrification) or hydrogen-bonds into the spaces water vacated (water replacement), holding proteins and membranes in place while dry. True for some anhydrobiotes (brine shrimp, some nematodes). But many tardigrades make **little or no trehalose** — so the sugar story can't be the whole mechanism for them.

The tardigrade-specific answer is a protein family: **CAHS (Cytosolic Abundant Heat-Soluble)**, found only in eutardigrades. CAHS proteins are **intrinsically disordered** — they have no fixed 3D fold in solution, the opposite of the textbook "structure determines function" enzyme. And the mechanism, primary papers (Tanaka/Kunieda PLOS Biology 2022; Boothby Molecular Cell 2017; the 2021 condensation preprints):

- On **drying** — the trigger being water loss / crowding itself — CAHS proteins **self-assemble into filaments** and undergo a **gel transition**, forming a cytoskeleton-like network that *did not exist* in the hydrated cell.
- The gel provides **mechanical strength**: it stiffens the cell, resists the compressive collapse of dehydration, and prevents membrane rupture and cytoplasmic content loss during the dry-down and re-wet transitions.
- On **rehydration** the network **dissolves back** — the filaments are reversible, stress-dependent, and leave no permanent structure behind. CAHS12-GFP shows the reversible network enabling compression tolerance across desiccation–rehydration cycles.
- CAHS outperforms trehalose at preserving enzyme (LDH) activity — up to an **order of magnitude** better at sufficient concentration.

## Why this is genuinely surprising (the fact-led keeper)

Three textbook intuitions invert at once:
1. **Disorder is the functional state.** The protein's lack of a fixed fold isn't a defect to be chaperoned away — the disorder is *what lets it sense crowding and phase-transition on cue*. Function without structure.
2. **The protective scaffold is conjured by the threat.** There is no standing skeleton kept "just in case." The stress (water loss) is itself the assembly signal; the support appears *because* the collapse is beginning, and vanishes when it passes. The insurance is manufactured by the accident, exactly in step with it.
3. **Reversibility is the whole design, not a limitation.** A permanent gel would be a worse solution — the cell needs to run again after re-wetting. The value is in a scaffold that is *load-bearing while the stress lasts and gone the instant it doesn't.*
