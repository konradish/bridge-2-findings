## The fact

Stellar physics is clear and boring on one point: a main-sequence star brightens as it ages. Four billion years ago the Sun put out roughly **70% of its current energy** — about 30% fainter. Run that number through a simple climate balance and early Earth should have been **frozen solid**, an ice ball, for its first two billion years.

It wasn't. The rock record from that same era shows **liquid water, oceans, and life** (the Archean). Carl Sagan and George Mullen named the contradiction in **1972**: two independently solid bodies of evidence — stellar evolution and geology — flatly disagree about whether early Earth could have been wet, and *both are right.*

The leading resolution (Walker, Hays & Kasting, **1981**): a far stronger **greenhouse** — much higher CO₂ and/or methane — kept the dim-lit Earth warm. And the CO₂ was regulated by a **carbonate-silicate thermostat**: silicate rock weathering pulls CO₂ out of the air *faster when it's warmer* and slower when it's colder, while volcanoes keep injecting it. So over billions of years, as the Sun slowly brightened, the atmosphere's greenhouse was drawn down roughly in step — a temperature controller with no controller, no set-point stored anywhere, just a reaction whose rate is governed by its own output.

1. **(fact — primary) The headline is the fact itself: the Sun was measurably dimmer and the Earth still ran liquid.** A quantitative paradox in the planet's own history, resolved not by discarding either measurement but by both being true at once under a hidden third term. Worth holding as the fact, before any lesson.

3. **(honest — the one modestly-fresh note) The paradox proved a missing term existed more firmly than it revealed what it was.** The contradiction *guarantees* something compensated for the faint Sun — you can't have a frozen prediction and a wet record without a hidden variable. But *which* — how much CO₂, how much methane, whether a lower early albedo (fewer clouds, less continent) also helped — is **still debated 50 years on** (some papers argue the paradox "remains" open). Certainty that a parameter exists can outrun, by decades, certainty about its value.
