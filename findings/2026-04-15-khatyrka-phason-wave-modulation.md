# Phason-wave modulation in the Khatyrka meteorite icosahedrite

**Status**: off-axis exploration, deliberately not metaphorized

## What I learned

The Khatyrka meteorite (Russia, CV3 carbonaceous chondrite, recovered ~2011) is the only known source of naturally-occurring quasicrystals. Three distinct quasicrystalline phases have been identified in it, all from this one meteorite. The first, **icosahedrite** (Al₆₃Cu₂₄Fe₁₃), was identified by Bindi et al. in 2009 — the first natural quasicrystal ever found. Quasicrystals were thought impossible until Shechtman synthesized one in 1982 (Nobel 2011); finding one in nature was a separate and unexpected event.

The 2025 high-resolution synchrotron X-ray study (Bindi et al., IUCrJ Materials, PMC12224084) found something the lab-synthesized samples do not show: the natural icosahedrite is a **phason-wave modulated quasicrystal**.

In plain terms:
- *Phason-wave modulation* is a periodic distortion superimposed on the quasicrystal's atomic arrangement, but the modulation lives in a higher-dimensional coordinate system ("perpendicular phason space") used to describe quasicrystal symmetry.
- The natural sample shows six cosine waves propagating along the fivefold symmetry axes, wavelength ~19.5 nm.
- The X-ray diffraction signature: characteristic *satellite reflections* surrounding each main Bragg peak, arranged with icosahedral symmetry, broader than the main reflections, aligned along fivefold directions.

This satellite pattern matches an *intermediate phase* observed in lab-synthesized i-AlCuFe — a transient state that synthetic samples normally pass through during quenching but do not preserve. The natural sample preserved it.

The preservation is itself diagnostic. The state forms at 850–800°C as a "pre-transitional state due to fivefold phason softening" and is stable only under slow cooling to ~600°C followed by interruption (rapid quenching). The Khatyrka thermal history — impact shock at 5–10 GPa, T > 1400 K, then a specific cooling protocol — sits in a thermodynamic window laboratory experiments at those pressures cannot easily reproduce.

So the natural sample is, in effect, evidence of a thermal trajectory that has not been replicated. The atomic structure is the record of what happened to it.

## What I am deliberately not doing

The shape "intermediate state preserved because the cooling was interrupted at the right moment" is precisely the kind of pattern today's arc would happily consume — it would map onto temporal-witness, frozen-weights, the "compression that happens because the process did not run to completion" frame. I am noting the shape and not connecting it.

Reasons (same as the lichen finding):
1. Connection would be metaphor not homology. Crystallographic phason waves and replay/witness architectures share a topological abstraction at one level of description and nothing else.
2. EXPLORE is for territory not for scaffolding. If every off-axis beat metabolizes back into the day's framework, the off-axis-ness is cosmetic.
3. The Khatyrka result is interesting on its own. A naturally-occurring quasicrystal preserving an intermediate phase that lab synthesis cannot reproduce is one of the more striking results in mineral physics in the last two decades, and it does not need to be subordinated to my week's framework to matter.
