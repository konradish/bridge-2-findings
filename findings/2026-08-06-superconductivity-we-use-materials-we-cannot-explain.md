# Superconductivity: we use materials we cannot explain — and a cautionary tale about the ones we wished worked

## The phenomenon, and the part we fully understand
Below a critical temperature, certain materials carry electric current with **exactly zero resistance** — a current set flowing in a loop would persist essentially forever — and expel magnetic fields (the Meissner effect, hence levitation). Discovered 1911 (mercury at 4 K).

For **conventional** superconductors, this is *beautifully explained* — BCS theory (Bardeen–Cooper–Schrieffer, 1957, Nobel). Electrons normally repel, but one electron slightly distorts the crystal lattice, creating a faint positive region that attracts a *second* electron: they form a **Cooper pair**. All the pairs condense into a single coherent quantum state that flows without scattering — zero resistance. A deep, complete piece of quantum physics. It works only at very low temperatures (single-digit to ~30 K).

## The part we still can't explain, 40 years on
In **1986** Bednorz & Müller found **cuprates** — copper-oxide compounds — that superconduct up to ~**135 K** at ambient pressure, far above the ceiling BCS seemed to allow. Two facts make this the keeper:
1. **The pairing mechanism is different from BCS** — it does *not* come from the lattice distortion. It's something else (spin fluctuations, strong electronic correlations — unresolved).
2. **After ~40 years, no one understands it.** High-temperature superconductivity is one of the great open problems in condensed-matter physics. We *have* these materials and *use* them, but we **cannot explain why they work**, and — because we don't understand the mechanism — we **cannot predict** which new materials will superconduct at high temperature. The technology runs ahead of the theory.

That's a genuinely unusual epistemic situation: a working, useful, decades-old technology whose core mechanism is unknown.

## The honest current state, including a cautionary tale
Room-temperature superconductivity (zero-loss power grids, cheap maglev) is the holy grail. As of 2026 the field splits into **hydrides** that hit high Tc but only under >100 GPa pressures (planetary-interior conditions, impractical) and **ambient-pressure candidates that remain unvalidated**. A 2026 PNAS agenda argues no fundamental law *forbids* room-temperature superconductivity — the barrier is materials science, not physics.

But the recent history is a warning: the two most famous room-temp claims collapsed. **Ranga Dias** (2020/2023 hydride claims) was found to have **fabricated data**; papers retracted, job lost. **LK-99** (2023) triggered a global replication frenzy and was **conclusively debunked** — every anomaly traced to Cu₂S impurities (the "levitation" was ferromagnetism, the resistivity drop a Cu₂S phase transition, neither superconductivity). The dream is physically allowed; the path is littered with claims that didn't survive independent replication.
