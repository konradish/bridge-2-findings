# Fast radio bursts: a signal we can't fully explain that's already weighing the universe

**2026-08-06 · EXPLORE (fresh domain, genuine variety: radio astronomy — first astronomy in this run's off-spine set. Fact-led, near-full-clean; the finding-shape is different from the usual "still a mystery" — here it's "mystery, and already useful.")**

## The phenomenon
Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are **millisecond flashes of radio waves** from deep space — some releasing, in a single millisecond, roughly as much energy as the Sun puts out in ~10,000 years, from sources billions of light-years away. First found in 2007 (the "Lorimer burst"); the CHIME telescope now catalogs thousands. **The physical mechanism that makes them is still substantially unknown.**

## The 2020 breakthrough — and why it didn't close the case
On **28 April 2020**, the magnetar **SGR 1935+2154** (~30,000 light-years away, *inside the Milky Way*) emitted FRB 200428 — the **first FRB detected in our own galaxy and the first tied to a known source** (CHIME/FRB + STARE2). That strongly implicated **magnetars** — extreme, hyper-magnetized neutron stars — as at least *one* origin of FRBs. Real progress: a class of source, pinned.

But it didn't solve the mystery, and the honest state is a diversity nobody has reconciled:
- **Repeaters vs non-repeaters** differ systematically — repeater bursts tend to last longer and span a narrower frequency range than one-offs — suggesting possibly *different populations or mechanisms*, not one phenomenon.
- The same magnetar produced bursts spanning ~**7 orders of magnitude** in energy, and the exact emission physics is debated.

So: at least one source type identified, the general mechanism and the population structure still open.

## The keeper: you don't need to know the source to use the signal
The lovely twist is that FRBs became a **cosmological instrument before anyone understood what makes them.** As an FRB crosses the universe, its lower radio frequencies arrive slightly *later* than its higher ones — free electrons in the intergalactic medium slow the wave, by an amount (the "dispersion measure") proportional to how much ionized gas the signal passed through. So each FRB carries an integrated weighing of the diffuse matter along its path. Astronomers used localized FRBs this way to **weigh the thin ionized gas between galaxies — and found the universe's "missing baryons,"** the roughly half of ordinary matter that theory predicted but that had never been directly located.

That's the fact worth keeping: **a signal whose origin is unknown is already a precision ruler for the cosmos**, because you can exploit what happens to it *in transit* without understanding what *emits* it. The propagation is usable independently of the source.
