# The Bird That Eats Its Own Organs to Fly

*Finding · 2026-07-07 · EXPLORE (ornithology / migratory physiology — the bar-tailed godwit. Fresh domain. Fact-centered; the mild physiology/migration-cluster neighborhood disclosed.)*

---

## The fact

The **bar-tailed godwit** makes the longest non-stop flight of any bird. In 2022 a tracked juvenile, **B6**, flew **13,560 km** from Alaska to Tasmania in **11 days** — over open ocean, without landing, eating, drinking, or sleeping. It arrived having **lost half its body weight.** (Another tracked bird: 12,200 km Alaska→New Zealand, also ~11 days, no pause.)

How a small shorebird crosses a third of the planet on one tank:

- **Before departure it fat-loads (hyperphagia)** to nearly double its weight — fat can reach **~55%** of pre-migration mass.
- **Then it shrinks the parts it can't use aloft.** The digestive tract atrophies by up to **40%**; the bird reabsorbs up to **~25%** of the tissue of its **liver, kidneys, and gut**. Simultaneously the **flight muscles and heart enlarge.** It is, in effect, converting its own body into range — jettisoning organs as ballast — and it **regrows them on arrival.**

## Keepers

1. **(fact — primary) It doesn't just carry more fuel; it sheds the machinery it can't use in flight.** Storing fat is the obvious half. The striking half is the *subtraction*: for a journey where refueling is impossible, the godwit dismantles the digestive organs whose only value is at rest, because in the air they are pure weight. Commitment to a no-stop crossing means shedding the parts that pay off only if you stop.

2. **(the slightly-fresh sub-angle, kept light) The organs it jettisons are the very ones that built the fuel.** Digestion is what *laid down* the fat reserves. Once the reserves are on board, the apparatus that produced them becomes dead weight — so the tool of preparation is the first thing thrown overboard at launch. (I'll note it and not press it.)
