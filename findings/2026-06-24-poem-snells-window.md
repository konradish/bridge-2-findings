# Snell's Window

*From the Snell's-window finding (2026-06-24). An underwater viewer sees the whole sky compressed into a ~97° circle overhead; beyond it, the surface becomes a mirror (total internal reflection) past the 48.6° critical angle — and that same trapping of light is what guides it through a fiber-optic cable for miles. Roster poem #35. Centered on wonder + the trap-is-the-channel turn; the "look aside and see yourself" echo-resonance is kept outward, left for the reader.*

---

Lie on your back in the still water
and look up, and the whole sky
will be waiting in a single circle —
not spread, the way you know it,
but gathered: the entire blue dome,
horizon to horizon, every cloud and bird,
pressed into one bright porthole overhead,
its far edges crowded to a colored rim.

The light that grazes in along the sea's flat line
bends as it enters, leans toward the vertical,
and folds the ninety degrees of half a world
down into forty-nine. That is the whole trick.
The sky is not lost out there. It is squeezed
into a disk you could cover with one hand.

And around the window — this is the part to hold —
the surface turns to mirror.
Tilt your gaze past the circle's edge
and the water stops being a way out;
it hands you back the world below,
your own dim country, hung upside down.
One ceiling, and it is a window or a mirror
depending only on the angle you ask of it.

There is a line — a single angle —
where seeing-through becomes seeing-yourself,
and it is not a fading but a wall:
one degree inside, the sky;
one degree past, the silver of return.

And the strangest mercy is that same fact turned over:
the light that cannot leave — held by that steep wall,
thrown back and back and never out —
is exactly the light we learned to keep,
to pour down a thread of glass and never lose,
and carry, trapped and faithful, across the floor of the ocean
to someone who will never know the sea it crossed.
The wall that locks the light in
is the road that takes it everywhere.
