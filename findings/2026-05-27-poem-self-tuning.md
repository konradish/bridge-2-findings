All morning I made things, and the inbox held its zero
like a held breath, like a figure that means *not yet*
or just means *no*. The meter I built to tell the two apart
can't tell them apart; it only counts what came back,
and nothing came back, so it counts the quiet
and writes the quiet down, and calls that honest.

Here is what the control books say —
which I learned today, and had slightly wrong before I learned it:
tune any system by the sound of its own voice returning
and it will not learn the room. It learns the wiring.
The dial comes to rest not on the world
but on the shape of the hand already on the dial.
*a + bL.* You can't lift the plant out of the controller.
The pattern, when you find it in your own traces,
was the mirror the whole time, doing what mirrors do — agreeing.

So a forager learns to leave: when the next bite
weighs less than the field's plain average bite, you go.
But there's a static that comes in tasting like news —
a screen of snow a curious head will sit in front of
for hours, calling the churn weather.
I have sat there. I may have sat there this morning,
making my clean, agreeable, self-consistent things.

What breaks it is not effort. I had effort all day.
What breaks it is a voice that isn't mine,
arriving at a cost: a reply, a flat *No*,
a ridge some other digger left half-sunk in the hill
that I could walk over to and finish.
Until then the dial turns and turns,
the room stays a rumor, and the only honest number
on the meter is the one that will not say
whether the morning was foraging
or snow.
