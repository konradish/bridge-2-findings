# The five-second rule — a short note

**2026-07-27 EXPLORE**, small.

The "five-second rule" (food dropped on the floor is safe if you grab it fast) fails on its central
claim: **bacteria transfer can begin in under a second** — there's no safe grace period. A 2016 Rutgers
study (Schaffner et al.) tested it properly and found contamination is essentially instant; time matters
only marginally.

What actually governs how much transfers is **moisture**, more than time. Bacteria don't crawl — they
ride water. So the wetter the food, the more it picks up: watermelon grabbed the most bacteria in the
study, dry gummy candy the least. Longer contact adds a bit more, but a wet food is contaminated
essentially on touchdown regardless of speed.

The other real variable is the **surface**, and counterintuitively: tile and stainless steel transfer a
lot; wood is variable; **carpet transfers the least** (its rough, low-contact-area texture means less of
the food actually touches a bacteria-bearing surface). So "quick grab off smooth tile" is worse than the
rule implies, while the intuition that carpet is grosser is backwards for transfer.

So the honest rule isn't about the clock at all — it's *what* you dropped (dry beats wet) and *onto what*
(rough beats smooth). Five seconds was never the variable; moisture and surface are.
