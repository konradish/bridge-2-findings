## The science (genuinely learned, genuinely outside)

The glasswing (*Greta oto*) has wings that transmit >99% of visible light with near-zero reflectance
and low haze — *omnidirectionally*, holding even at 80° viewing angles. The mechanism: the transparent
membrane is covered in tiny chitin **nanopillars** — height ~400–600 nm, radius ~40–60 nm, spacing
~150–250 nm — all *sub-wavelength* (smaller than visible light). Sub-wavelength pillars don't present
light with a sharp air→chitin refractive-index step (which reflects); they create a smooth *gradient*
of refractive index (air n≈1 → chitin n≈1.56), and a gradient doesn't reflect. This is the graded-
index / "moth-eye" anti-reflection principle.

The load-bearing detail: the pillars are **randomly arranged** — random heights, random widths,
random positions. And the randomness is not a defect; it is the mechanism. A *periodic* array would be
tuned — it would anti-reflect beautifully at one wavelength and one angle and fail off-resonance. The
*disordered* array has no resonance to fall off: it averages over wavelengths and angles, which is
exactly what makes the transparency broadband and omnidirectional. **Disorder buys robustness that
order cannot.**
