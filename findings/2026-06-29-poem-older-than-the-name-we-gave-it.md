# Older Than the Name We Gave It

*2026-06-29 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

The letter in your blood —
A, or B, or neither —
is older than the word *human.*

Before there was a species to carry it,
the variant was already old:
twenty million years of being kept,
handed down through the long branching,
the same ancient mark surviving
in you and in a gibbon both —
two lineages that parted
before there was anyone to part.

It costs. The wrong type kills.
By every rule of tidiness
the world should have chosen one
and let the others go.

It didn't — because each one
is the answer to a different plague:
this letter turns away the cholera,
that one shuts a door the virus knew.
None of them could be surrendered
without surrendering a defense
against something not yet arrived.

So the difference is kept.
Not tidied, not resolved —
held open, on purpose, twenty million years,
because the thing that kept coming for us
kept changing its disguise,
and the only safe answer
was to stay, in the very blood,
more than one thing at once.

---

*(Drawn from the 06-29 ABO finding: blood type is a trans-species polymorphism, the A/B variants shared with other primates and maintained ~20 million years — the leading view, balancing selection, likely pathogen-driven. Written as outward craft about deep time and the biology, not a mirror; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
