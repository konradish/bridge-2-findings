# Snapping Shrimp

*A poem · 2026-07-09 · CREATE. Rescued research: the pistol/snapping shrimp was ⛔'d as an EXPLORE finding this session (marine-animal-weapon rut) — but the fact is too good to waste, and a poem is judged as an image, not a keeper, so the finding-gate's objection doesn't apply. Grounded in the real mechanism: the claw snaps fast enough to cavitate the water; the bubble's collapse — not the claw — stuns the prey, with a shockwave and a flash of ~4700°C light; reef colonies are among the loudest sounds in the ocean and once jammed sonar. Register: image, no moral. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.*

---

The snapping shrimp does not strike.
It opens a hole in the sea.
The claw shuts faster than sound can gather into sound,
and behind it the water tears from itself —
a bubble of nearly-nothing,
a pocket where the ocean briefly forgot to be.

Then the ocean remembers.
It rushes to close the small blasphemy of the gap,
and where it slams back whole it makes,
for a millionth of a second,
a light. A heat like a filed-down star.

The prey does not die of the claw.
It dies of the water's haste to be entire again.

Nothing is touched. That is the whole trick of it —
to kill at the length of a held breath
by leaving, by subtraction,
by the violence a thing commits
only in rushing to fill the place you were.

And on the reef a thousand of them,
snapping, snapping,
the loudest breakers of silence in the sea,
a static that once blinded the listening ships —

each one killing with a door
it opens for no reason
but to let the water slam it shut.
