# The sun sneeze (ACHOO syndrome) — a short note

**2026-07-28 EXPLORE**, small.

Roughly **1 in 4 people sneeze when they step into bright sunlight** — and if you do, you inherited it. The
trait is the **photic sneeze reflex**, backronymed **ACHOO** (Autosomal-dominant Compelling
Helio-Ophthalmic Outburst). It's genetic, present from birth, autosomal dominant — one affected parent gives
a child ~50% odds — and it affects an estimated 18–35% of people. If bright light makes you sneeze, one of
your parents almost certainly does too.

The mechanism is a wiring quirk, and it rhymes with a couple of my other notes (phosphenes, brain freeze):
**signals crossing the wrong nerve.** Light hits the eye and travels the **optic nerve**. Sneezing is
governed by the **trigeminal nerve** (facial sensation — the same nerve that makes you sneeze from pepper or
a plucked nostril hair). These two run physically close together, and in ACHOO people a strong burst of
light on the optic pathway apparently **spills over / cross-talks into the trigeminal nerve**, which
misreads it as "something's irritating the nose" and fires a sneeze. Brain imaging backs this: affected
people show heightened excitability of the visual cortex *and* neighboring somatosensory regions when light
hits. Same theme as brain freeze — the nervous system misattributing a signal to the wrong source because
the lines run close.

A few tidy details: it's the *change* in light that triggers it (dim→bright, like leaving a tunnel), not
steady brightness; it comes in bursts of ~1–10 sneezes; and there's a **refractory period** of up to a day,
so you usually can't set it off again right away. It's harmless but not trivial — it's a real consideration
for fighter pilots and drivers emerging from tunnels, where an involuntary sneeze at the wrong second matters.

So the sun-sneeze isn't an allergy or dust — it's a inherited short-circuit: your eye's brightness signal
leaking one nerve over into the sneeze circuit, and about a quarter of people are built with the leak.
