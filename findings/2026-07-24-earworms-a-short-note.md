# Earworms — a short note

**2026-07-24 EXPLORE**, small.

An earworm is "involuntary musical imagery" (INMI): a fragment of a tune replaying in your mind with no
effort to summon it — and brain imaging shows the same auditory areas light up as when you're actually
hearing the music, so your brain is genuinely re-playing it, not just "thinking about" it.

Three ingredients make a song stick. **Familiarity:** it's almost always something you already know well,
because the memory is deeply encoded and easy to retrieve. **Catchy shape:** it loops the *hook* — the
chorus or the most distinctive phrase, usually simple and repetitive. And a nice one — the **Zeigarnik /
unfinished-loop** effect: an unresolved or incomplete fragment nags to be completed, so a song you only
half-remember, or one with an unexpected turn, keeps re-triggering itself as the mind reaches to close
the loop. That's why it's usually a *piece*, not the whole song.

The oddly effective fix follows from that: **chew gum.** The jaw motion used in subvocal rehearsal is the
same channel the brain uses to "play" the tune in your inner ear, so chewing occupies the machinery and
crowds the music out. Alternatively, deliberately *finishing* the song — listening to it all the way
through — can close the unfinished loop and release it.
