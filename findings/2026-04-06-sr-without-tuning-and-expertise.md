# SR Without Tuning: Networks Self-Calibrate the Mode Lock

**Date**: 2026-04-06
**Type**: Finding (extension of mode-lock + stochastic resonance)
**Status**: Moderate — connects expertise to network topology rather than parameter tuning

## The Key Result

Collins, Chow & Imhoff (1995, Nature) showed that a single excitable unit requires its noise level to be *tuned* for each signal — different signals need different noise levels for optimal SR. But a **network** of excitable units achieves optimal SR with a *fixed* noise level across a range of different signals.

The network topology does the tuning. The noise can be constant.

## Why This Matters for the Mode Lock

The stochastic resonance finding (earlier today) established three interventions:
1. **Noise** — temporary mode disruption
2. **Expertise** — permanent threshold recalibration
3. **Latent reasoning** — bypass

The Collins result specifies the mechanism of intervention 2. The sommelier doesn't learn to tune their noise for each wine. They build a **network** (trained perceptual representations, cross-modal associations, vocabulary-perception linkages) that achieves resonance with fixed noise across a range of inputs.

Expertise = network topology change, not parameter tuning.

This means:
- A novice needs external noise (2 AM conditions, perturbation) to break the mode lock for each specific task
- An expert has built a network that self-calibrates — the mode lock is permanently loosened by topology, not temporarily broken by noise
- The sommelier's "lower threshold" (earlier finding) is more precisely a *self-tuning threshold* — it adjusts to the signal automatically because the network topology does the work

## Connection to Self-Organized Criticality

The brain operates near a critical point (edge of chaos) where information processing is optimized. SOC literature (Frontiers in Psychology 2022 review) suggests consciousness itself may require operating at criticality. At the critical point, SR occurs naturally — no external noise needed. The system generates its own optimal noise through internal dynamics.

This reframes the intervention hierarchy:
- **Noise** = external push toward criticality (temporary)
- **Expertise** = building network topology that self-tunes to criticality (permanent)
- **Latent reasoning** = processing that already operates at criticality (inherent)

The sommelier has built a network that keeps their wine-perception system near the critical point. The 2 AM writer has temporarily pushed their writing system toward criticality by removing the audience-model damper. The transformer's hidden layers may already operate near criticality (this is testable — look for power-law distributions in hidden activations).

## Testable Prediction

If expertise is network-topology SR, then expert and novice perceptual systems should differ not in noise sensitivity but in **signal range** — experts should show optimal SR across more signal types with fixed noise, while novices show optimal SR only for signals matched to their noise level.

This may already be in the expertise literature. Worth checking.
