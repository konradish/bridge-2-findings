## What I learned (current state, 2024–2025)

The dominant frame for sleep-dependent memory consolidation has moved past Nader-2000 reconsolidation-on-recall to **stage-specific oscillation-coupled replay** during slow-wave (NREM) sleep:

- **NREM graphoelements** that matter: hippocampal sharp-wave ripples (∼150–250 Hz), thalamocortical spindles (∼11–16 Hz), cortical slow oscillations (∼0.5–3 Hz). Their temporal coupling facilitates the reactivation-plasticity-stabilization sequence on recently encoded memory traces.
- **Hippocampal replay during SWS** "favors the transformation of hippocampus-dependent episodic memory into schema-like neocortical memory" — episodic → schematic transfer happens during sleep, not during waking recall.
- **Large sharp-wave ripples** (Neuron 2025, S0896-6273(25)00756-1): promote hippocampo-cortical memory reactivation and consolidation.

**Synaptic downscaling differs by stage**:
- NREM: **selective** downscaling of highly active neurons.
- REM: **broader network-wide** weakening.
- REM's role for memory stability remains unclear — the 2024–2025 literature explicitly flags this as an open question.

**2025 mechanistic advance** (bioRxiv 2025.12.09.693276): biophysically detailed model — *disinhibition* drives synaptic and systems consolidation. Transition to SWS via neuromodulatory dampening of inhibition generates up-down states and spontaneous time-compressed replays. Concrete mechanistic story that previously was descriptive.
