## The popular story, and what's actually true

Popular: *Turritopsis dohrnii is biologically immortal — it reverses its own aging and lives
forever.* Three corrections, each from the primary being more careful than the headline:

**1. "Immortal" is a nickname the papers don't use.** The genome paper's own title says
**"repeatedly rejuvenating,"** not immortal, and (read directly) it "deliberately avoids claims
about the rejuvenation mechanism itself." *Immortal* here means only "does not have to die of old
age" — it says nothing about actual mortality, which in the wild is dominated by predation and
disease. A thing that can't senesce still gets eaten.

**2. It doesn't "reverse aging." It reverts its life cycle.** The mechanism is a mature medusa
transforming **directly back into the polyp stage** — the sessile, colonial juvenile form — via
cell transdifferentiation (the cellular detail is from Piraino et al. 1996, not the genome papers,
which are sequence-level). This is not an old animal becoming a young animal of the same kind. It is
an adult body dedifferentiating into a different life stage entirely. Which raises the real question
the word "immortal" hides —

**3. It is not obvious the individual survives at all.** If the medusa becomes a polyp colony, is
that the *same individual* persisting, or a new organism grown from the old one's cells? The
"immortality" claim quietly assumes an answer to a genuinely hard identity question — continuity of
*what*, exactly? The papers don't assert individual continuity; the headline does. The jellyfish
doesn't live forever; a lineage of cells passes through a body, sheds it, and builds another.

## What the genome papers do and don't establish

DO: T. dohrnii has expanded / extra-copy gene families vs its non-reverting cousin T. rubra — DNA
repair, DNA polymerases, oxidative-stress response, telomere-maintenance variants, pluripotency.
DON'T: show these *cause* rejuvenation. They're candidate correlates from a comparative genome. The
DNA Research paper is explicit that it provides sequence data and avoids mechanism. So even the
genetic story is "here are the plausible genes," not "here is the switch."

The delight is that the truth is *stranger and more precise* than the myth. "Lives forever" is a
child's version of something much odder: an animal that answers death by ceasing to be the thing
that would die, and becoming an earlier form of itself — at the cost of the identity the word
"immortal" was supposed to protect.
