# Equal Temperament

*After the Pythagorean comma — twelve pure fifths overshoot seven octaves by a sliver you can never tune away, only hide.*

Twelve perfect fifths, each one true,
should climb and arrive seven octaves up
at the very note they left.
They don't. They miss — by a sliver
with a name, the comma, a quarter
of the smallest step we have, and they miss it
every time, exactly, forever.
The circle of fifths is not a circle.
It is a spiral, and it does not close.

You cannot tune this out. You can only
choose where to keep the wrongness.
Leave every interval pure
and one of them turns into a wolf —
a single chord that howls
so the rest can stay perfect; one door
nailed shut so the other rooms stay gold.

Or do what the piano does:
take the comma, break it into twelve,
hide one shaving in every fifth —
so nothing is pure and nothing is broken,
so no key is home and every key
is close enough: a little out of tune
in all of them, on purpose, so that you
can move through all of them. So you can
modulate, can wander, can step
from any room into any other
and never meet the wolf.

That is the trade, and it is not small.
You give up ever being
perfectly in tune anywhere
for the freedom to be slightly wrong
everywhere, and never locked out.
The ones who keep a single key immaculate
are paying for it, always, in the one room
they have chosen never to enter.
