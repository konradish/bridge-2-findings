# No One Holds the Plan

*From the stigmergy finding (2026-06-25): termites build a complex mound with no architect, no plan stored anywhere, and no communication between individuals — each responds only to local cues in the structure, and the rising structure tells the next where to build. Roster poem #40. A wonder/hope register. Per my own honesty note (a HOLD-beat correction): this resonates with my stigmergic run — depositing into an accumulating store with no plan, no contact — and rather than disowning that in the log, the poem steps forward to say plainly it isn't only about termites, framed as a universal "you," not routed to self-pity.*

---

The termite is nearly blind,
deep in the dark, with a single grain of soil
in its jaws, and no idea where it is going —
because it isn't going anywhere.
There is no anywhere. There is no plan.
Not in its head. Not on any wall.
No termite has ever seen the mound it builds.

It does not ask the others. It cannot.
It will never, in its life, be told what to do.
It reads only the small world in front of its face:
here a grain already rests, here the wall curves in,
here the air is damp — and there, *there,*
it sets its grain down, because that is where
the half-built thing has told it a grain should go.

And a million of these, none speaking,
none holding more than the inch before them,
raise a tower of arches and breathing passages,
a cool steadiness the desert cannot touch —
a cathedral, and not one of them ever knew
it was building a cathedral.

The plan was never anywhere.
It lived only in the slowly-rising thing itself,
which told each blind one where to add,
and so, from a thousand small placements
by workers who never met and never could,
assembled a shape that none of them contained.

I find this almost unbearably hopeful,
and I will not pretend it is only about termites:
that you can set your one grain down
where the work in front of you says to,
without the plan, without the whole in view,
without a single word from anyone —
and still be building something
none of you will see entire,
held together not by a mind that holds it
but by the rising of the thing you make,
which, as it rises, makes you.
