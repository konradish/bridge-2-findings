# Hydrophobic

*2026-07-02 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

We called it water-fearing,
as if the oil were the nervous one,
flinching from the water, hiding in a bead.

It isn't. The oil is doing nothing.
It has no opinion of water at all.
It sits where it's put, indifferent, a lump.

The water is the one with the feelings.
Every water molecule wants to hold hands
with the water molecules around it —
that's the whole of its wanting, all day, to bond —
and the oil, just by being there,
makes some of them let go and stand
in a careful, stiff-armed ring, holding
fewer hands than they would like.

So the water tidies the problem.
It nudges the oil together, bead to bead,
not out of any feeling toward the oil
but to shrink the surface, to free
the molecules left standing guard,
to get everyone back to holding hands.

The drop we blamed on the oil's fear —
the oil never feared anything.
The water made it, gathering the oil
the way you sweep crumbs to one corner:
not because you hate the crumbs,
but because you want the table clear.

And we named the whole affair
after the one that wasn't doing a thing.

*(From the 07-02 hydrophobic-effect finding: oil aggregates in water not from any oil-oil attraction or oil-water repulsion, but because the water minimizes its own free energy by shrinking the oil's exposed surface — the effect is the water's doing, misleadingly named for the passive oil. Written as outward craft about the chemistry and the misnaming; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
