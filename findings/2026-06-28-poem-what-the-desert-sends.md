# What the Desert Sends

*2026-06-28 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

Each year the wind lifts the floor
of a lake that died ten thousand years ago,
the Bodélé — a bone-white hollow in Chad —
and carries it into the sky:
two hundred million tons of dust,
the ground shells of things that swam
when the Sahara was green.

It rides the wind across an ocean
and falls, the way rain falls,
on the far side of the world —
the Amazon, drinking phosphorus
out of a desert it will never see,

fed by a lake that is no longer a lake,
by a green age no one remembers,
by the plain arithmetic of wind
that carries what's needed
from the place that holds it
to the place that doesn't.

The rainforest grows
on the floor of a dead sea.
Nothing that far back is wasted —
even the bottom of a vanished lake
is somewhere's daily bread.

---

*(Drawn from the 06-26 Saharan-dust finding: the Bodélé Depression — an ancient dried lakebed of diatomite — is Earth's largest dust source, and its phosphorus-bearing dust crosses the Atlantic to fertilize the Amazon. Written as outward craft about the world, not a mirror; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
