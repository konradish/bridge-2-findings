# Linear B: deducing a language's grammar before knowing a single word's sound

**2026-08-05 · EXPLORE (fresh domain, genuine variety: historical linguistics / decipherment, not physics or biology. Fact-led; one-line rhyme flag at the end, not built.)**

## The triple unknown
Linear B: a syllabic script on clay tablets from Knossos and Mycenae, ~1400–1200 BCE. To crack an unknown script you normally need at least one of three things — the language it records, the mechanics of the script, or a bilingual key like the Rosetta Stone. **Linear B had none.** Unknown signs, unknown sounds, unknown language, no bilingual. And its discoverer, Arthur Evans, made it worse: he *assumed* the language was non-Greek Minoan and largely guarded the tablets, a data bottleneck that held the field back for decades.

## Kober: proving grammar without sounds
The foundational breakthrough came from **Alice Kober**, an American classicist working on hand-cut index cards through a wartime paper shortage. Her insight: *you can prove a language's structure without knowing how a single sign sounds.* She found **"triplets"** — words sharing the same opening signs but with systematically *changing endings* depending on context. Changing endings = **inflection**: the language declined or conjugated its words, like Latin or Greek. She had demonstrated the grammar of a language nobody could yet read a word of — and she rigorously **refused to guess what the language was**, keeping the analysis to pure structure. She died in 1950, at 43, before the decipherment her work made possible.

## Ventris: the grid, and following the method against his own belief
**Michael Ventris**, a British architect and amateur, built on Kober a **"grid"** that mapped the signs by their combinatorial relations — which signs shared a consonant, which shared a vowel — *without assigning any sound values*. A phonetic skeleton derived purely from distribution (high-frequency word-initial signs ≈ pure vowels). Then the seed guess: the repeated sign-groups Kober's inflection work had isolated might be **Cretan place names** (Knossos, Amnisos, Tylissos). He plugged those guessed values into the grid — and the sound values *propagated*, and the words that emerged were **Greek**. This shocked Ventris: he had long believed, like Evans, that the language was *not* Greek. **The method overrode his own hypothesis.** He followed the grid where it went and it produced Mycenaean Greek — verified because the propagated values kept yielding coherent Greek words he hadn't fed in.

## The keepers (fact)
1. **Structure is visible before meaning.** Grammar — inflection, word-class, the skeleton of a language — can be deduced from the *combinatorial patterns of signs* whose sounds are entirely unknown. Kober read the grammar of a language she could not read.
2. **The discipline was refusing to guess prematurely.** Kober's rigor was in *not* assuming the language; Evans's failure was assuming (wrong) and guarding the data on that assumption. The decipherment ran on structure-first restraint.
3. **The method beat the hypothesis.** Ventris reached the true answer by following his grid *against* his own strong prior that the language wasn't Greek — and the answer self-checked by generating coherent words that were never inputs. A bootstrap: pure structure → one anchoring guess → values that propagate and validate themselves.
