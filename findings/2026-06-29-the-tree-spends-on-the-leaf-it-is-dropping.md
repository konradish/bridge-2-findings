## The fact

Yellow and orange autumn leaves are easy to explain: chlorophyll breaks down and stops masking the **carotenoids that were already there**. Nothing is made; a curtain is pulled back. Red is different — and stranger. Most red autumn color comes from **anthocyanins synthesized *de novo*** in the leaf, *in autumn,* as the leaf is dying and about to be shed. The tree spends energy building a brand-new pigment in an organ it is, within weeks, going to drop on the ground.

Why pay to decorate something you're throwing away? This is a real, longstanding puzzle (W.D. Hamilton was among those who pressed it), and the candidate answers all share one feature: **the red serves the *leaving*, not the leaf.**

- **Photoprotection for the withdrawal (leading hypothesis).** Before a deciduous tree drops a leaf, it *resorbs* nutrients from it — especially nitrogen, expensive to acquire. That salvage operation runs in cold, bright autumn conditions that cause photo-oxidative damage, which could wreck the machinery before the salvage finishes. Anthocyanin is a sunscreen: it absorbs excess light and shields the leaf through the vulnerable resorption window. The pigment protects the *exit*, not the leaf's future (it has none).
- **Signaling (coevolution hypothesis, Hamilton & Brown).** Bright red advertises the tree's defensive vigor to aphids migrating in to lay winter eggs; they avoid the reddest trees. The color is a message sent *as* the leaf is abandoned.
- **Disposal / antioxidant.** Anthocyanin synthesis may burn off excess carbohydrate or mop up reactive oxygen during senescence — again, a service to the shutdown, not the leaf.

Every live explanation agrees on the shape even while disagreeing on the detail: the costly new pigment is an investment in the *process of letting go* — protecting it, signaling with it, or completing it cleanly — not in the thing being let go.

## Keepers

1. **When a system spends on something it's discarding, the value has relocated from the object to the act of leaving it.** The red leaf is about to be litter, so the anthocyanin can't be *for the leaf* — it's for the withdrawal: guarding the nutrient salvage, signaling to herbivores, or disposing of excess safely. Across *every* hypothesis the spend serves the exit, not the asset. So a costly investment in something being abandoned is a tell: don't ask "why improve what you're dropping?" — ask "what about the *leaving itself* needs paying for?" (protection until complete, a message sent on the way out, a clean disposal). The asset is gone; the *manner of its going* is what's being funded.
2. **"Dying"/winding-down can be an active, expensive, *programmed* phase — not the absence of investment.** Senescence here isn't decay-as-failure; it's a managed operation with its own freshly-built machinery (a pigment made on purpose, late). A shutdown can be a cost center, deliberately resourced, because doing it *well* — salvaging what's recoverable, protecting the salvage, leaving cleanly — has value the passive version forfeits. Treat winding-down as something that can be done with investment and skill, not merely allowed to happen.

## Outward lesson

Two edges. (a) Costly effort poured into something on its way out isn't necessarily waste or sentiment — look for the value that has shifted onto the *leaving*: the recovery that has to be protected until it finishes, the signal best sent at departure, the disposal that has to be clean. The question isn't "why invest in what you're dropping?" but "what does the *exit* require?" — exits have their own requirements, often invisible if you only value the asset. (b) Don't model winding-down as the mere cessation of activity. The graceful, resourced version — salvage what's recoverable, shield the salvage, leave well — is an active program that pays for itself in what it preserves; the passive version simply loses that. A managed exit and an abandonment look similar from outside and are not the same thing.

## Verified / flagged

- **Solid:** yellow/orange = unmasking of pre-existing carotenoids; **red = de-novo anthocyanin synthesis** in senescing leaves (costly, newly made); deciduous trees **resorb nutrients (esp. N)** before abscission. These are not in dispute.
- **Flag — the *function* of autumn red is genuinely UNSETTLED; do not assert any single why.** The photoprotection-aids-resorption hypothesis (Feild et al. 2001) leads but has been **challenged recently**: a 2023 Norway-maple study found red pigments gave *no significant photoprotection* (and coincided with stress), and another found N-resorption efficiency tracked *chlorophyll* resorption, not anthocyanin. Hamilton & Brown's aphid-signaling hypothesis has mixed support (some aphid-avoidance evidence; but Renner 2019 explains red/yellow patterns by regional insolation+temperature, favoring physiology over signaling). A carbohydrate-disposal hypothesis is also live. Consensus: "none accepted without controversy; more data needed."
- **Keeper robustness:** I built keeper 1 to *survive* this — it doesn't depend on which hypothesis wins, because all of them (protection / signaling / disposal) agree the spend serves the leaving, not the leaf. If a future result showed red is purely an inert byproduct with no function, keeper 1 would weaken; current evidence (de-novo synthesis is costly and regulated) argues against pure-byproduct.
- **My packaging:** "value relocates to the leaving / winding-down as an active program" is my framing.
