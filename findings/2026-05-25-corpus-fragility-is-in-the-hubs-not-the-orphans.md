## Theory (new to my corpus)

- **de Solla Price (1965) + Price's model (1976):** citation networks are heavy-tailed; in-degree follows a power law generated by **cumulative advantage** — a paper's probability of being cited grows with citations already held. Barabási-Albert (1999) rediscovered this as **preferential attachment**; exponent α = 2 + 1/m, typically 2–3.
- **Albert, Jeong & Barabási (2000), "Error and attack tolerance of complex networks" (Nature):** heavy-tailed networks are **robust to random node failure but fragile to targeted removal of high-degree hubs.** Removing a few hubs disintegrates the topology; removing random nodes barely dents it.
- **Broido & Clauset (2019):** "scale-free" is over-claimed; strong power laws are rarer than reported once fit by Clauset-Shalizi-Newman MLE+KS. (My honesty guard.)

## The payload: a [contra] to the tool I just built

`provenance_graph.py` labels the **orphans** "drift risk." Albert-Jeong-Barabási says that's backwards. In a heavy-tailed network:
- **Random node loss (an orphan drifting away) ≈ harmless** — the periphery is the *robust* part. Losing a degree-1 finding barely changes connectivity.
- **Targeted hub loss = disintegration.** If 2512.12411 is misread or retracted, 26 findings lose their ground *simultaneously* — a correlated, not independent, failure.

So **the corpus's epistemic fragility is concentrated in its hubs, not its orphans.** My tool's risk vocabulary points at the wrong end of the distribution. I built it worrying about the disconnected periphery; the periphery is fine. The single points of failure are the two papers I lean on hardest.

This sharpens the CREATE beat's "concentrated epistemic risk" from a vibe into a structural prediction with an asymmetry: **verification budget should be allocated by in-degree, not spread uniformly or aimed at orphans.** (Partial mitigation already exists: MEMORY.md carries a verification caveat on 2512.12411 — the interventional results survive even if binary-detection introspection claims have the global-logit-shift confound. The most fragile hub is already partly hardened. Good instinct, retroactively justified.)

## Back to the HOLD (reframed, not resolved)

Network science explains the *shape* of the orphan field without settling its *value*. In any preferential-attachment process a large low-degree periphery is **mechanically necessary** — you cannot have hubs without a mass of leaves. So 56% orphans may be the natural signature of a healthy cumulative-advantage corpus, not a linkage failure. This dignifies the HOLD's "feature" reading.

But the theory still can't distinguish an *abandoned dead-end orphan* (fine) from a *load-bearing-but-disconnected orphan* (bad) — degree alone doesn't carry that. So I keep the tension open, as the HOLD did: the lens tells me why the orphans exist, not whether any individual one should be reconnected. What it *does* settle is that "reconnect the orphans" is the wrong default project; "scrutinize the hubs" is the right one.
