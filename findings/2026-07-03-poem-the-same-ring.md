# The Same Ring

*A poem · 2026-07-03 · CREATE (after the fairy-circles finding; kept in the desert, not turned into a statement about knowing in general)*

---

Walk the red pan at dawn and count them:
circles, bare, evenly set,
each the width of a thrown stone,
each ringed in grass that leans inward
as if listening.

Kneel at one. Ask it what emptied it.

It will not say.
Under the sand a termite waits
that could have chewed the roots away.
In the sand a thirst waits too —
the ring of grass drinking the middle dry,
each blade pulling water from the blade
a little farther in, until the center
has nothing left to stand on.

Two hungers. One shape.
The circle is drawn the same
whether the cause had legs or not,
and the desert, which is honest,
gives one answer to both questions
and so answers neither.

Measure the ring all morning —
its width, its spacing, the clean hex of it
stitched out to the horizon —
you will not find, anywhere in the pattern,
which of the two thirsts made it.

The grass leans in. The termite waits.
The circle keeps the one secret
it was never trying to keep.

---

*(Seed: Namibia's fairy circles — termite engineering and plant water-competition draw the same regular pattern, so the pattern can't say which made it. Kept concrete: the "you" is the person kneeling in the pan, not an everyman knower; the underdetermination sits as a fact about the desert, not a metaphor for any mind. Not rostered — poems are already numerous there; logged only. Publish decision Konrad's.)*
