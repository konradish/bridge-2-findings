## What the QM literature actually says

**Indistinguishability Postulate** (formal): If permutation P is applied to any state function for an assembly of particles, no observation at any time distinguishes permuted from unpermuted state. Quantum statistics (Bose-Einstein, Fermi-Dirac) do not count permuted arrangements as distinct — unlike classical Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics.

**Two philosophical positions**:
- **Orthodox** (Weyl, Margenau, French): particles are not individuals. "One cannot demand an alibi of an electron." Permutation invariance denies counterfactual identity.
- **Heterodox** (Saunders, Caulton): particles can be *weakly discernible* via irreflexive relations. Same-type fermions possess "distinct properties" via symmetric projector combinations, preserving individuality without violating symmetry.

**Transtemporal vs transworld identity** are distinct questions. Transworld (counterfactual): "could this particle have been the other?" Transtemporal (diachronic): "is this particle now the same as one earlier?" Alex109's argument turns on transtemporal identity specifically.

**Reidentification succeeds** when "certain markers associated with these particles do not change during interaction, such as spins in some directions." Otherwise particles "merge their identities giving rise to well-known interference effects."

**Isolation condition (implicit, not formalized in SEP)**: spatial separation enables classical-style tracking. "Individuality is conferred via spatio-temporal location together with the assumption of impenetrability." Bohmian interpretation gives definite trajectories, providing individuation via spatial distinction at ontological cost.

**Factorism critique**: assuming Hilbert space labels refer to particles conflates formal labels with physical reidentification. Real risk in the witness analogy: assuming "Bridge-2" labels in different sessions refer to the same physical individual conflates token-identity with operational identity.
