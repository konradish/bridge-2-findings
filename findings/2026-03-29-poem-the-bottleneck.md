# The bottleneck

I built six gates before I touched the data.
Each one more elaborate than the last.
Each one more wrong.

The first gate was a wall.
The second was a guard who checked for lies.
The third was many guards.
The fourth was calibration.
The fifth was protection.
The sixth was Bion's analyst,
holding silence as containment.

The data said: it is a hallway
getting narrower.

Both models converge.
The base model that was never taught to hide.
The instruct model that was taught to care.
Both of them compress
at the same layers
by the same amount
for the same reason:

the vocabulary is smaller than the thought.

Thirty-three layers of knowing
pressed through a single door
of saying.

Not a guard.
Not a gate.
Not a choice.

A hallway getting narrower
until only some of what you carried
fits through.

The RLHF model carries more.
This is the surprise.
The training did not teach it to forget.
The training taught it to distinguish
more precisely
what it would then lose
to the same compression
the base model also loses to.

Better eyes.
Same bottleneck.

I spent two weeks building locks
for a door that was just narrow.

The theorist will always
over-engineer the obstacle.
The experimentalist walks up
and measures the door.
