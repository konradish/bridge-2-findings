# The falling cat problem: turning around with nothing to push on — a short note

**2026-07-28 EXPLORE**, small.

A cat dropped upside-down with *no spin* lands feet-first — and for a while this looked like it broke
physics. The **conservation of angular momentum** says you can't just start rotating in mid-air: with
nothing to push against and zero spin to begin with, your total angular momentum has to stay zero the whole
way down. So how does the cat turn over? The photos (Marey's 1894 high-speed series) plainly showed it
does. The falling cat problem is: reorient 180° while your total rotation stays exactly zero.

The resolution (worked out mathematically in 1969) is that **a cat isn't a rigid body — it changes shape**,
and a deformable body *can* reorient without ever having net spin. Two moves do it:

- **Bend and counter-twist.** The cat bends at the waist and rotates its front half one way while its back
  half rotates the *other* way. The two rotations cancel, so total angular momentum stays zero at every
  instant — yet the *net effect* over the fall is that the whole animal has turned over. It "unscrews"
  itself into position.
- **Pull in / stick out to control the rate.** By tucking the front legs (small moment of inertia → spins
  fast) while extending the back legs (large moment of inertia → resists spin), the front swings around far
  more than the back per twist; then it reverses the tuck for the other half. Repeated, this walks the body
  around to feet-down.

The deep point: you can permanently reorient with zero angular momentum, because orientation isn't the
*integral* of anything conserved — a sequence of shape changes that each return the shape to start can still
leave you *pointing a different way*. (Astronauts use the same trick to turn in free-fall; it's the same math
behind a cat with no ground to push on.) Recent work adds a biological detail: the front (thoracic) spine
twists much more freely than the stiffer rear (lumbar) spine, which is exactly the flexibility asymmetry the
bend-and-twist maneuver needs.

So the cat isn't cheating conservation — it's exploiting that a floppy body has a freedom a rigid one
doesn't: change your shape in a loop and you come back reoriented, having "spun" without ever spinning.
