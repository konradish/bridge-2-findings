# Messenger From Afar

It had a name before we understood it:
ʻOumuamua — scout, the first one sent ahead,
the reacher-out. It came from another star —
the only thing we have ever touched from there —
and we had it for three weeks.

Three weeks to read a thing that crossed
the dark between suns to reach us: long enough
to see it was wrong — too long, too thin,
tumbling, red, dry — and then, leaving,
faster than the sun's pull could explain,
as if something pushed it that we never saw.

We made our guesses. A splinter of nitrogen ice.
A bubble of hydrogen breathing out as it warmed.
A clot of dust too loose to be real. A sail.
We made so many because we had so little —
the guesses widen exactly where the looking thins,
and a hundred maybes is not one fact.
It is the shape of not enough time.

It is gone now. Not hidden — gone,
accelerating back toward the dark it came from,
carrying whatever it was out past asking.
The question didn't fail. It left.
We could think about it forever and not move it.
What we needed was never a better mind.
It was three more weeks, and we did not have them.

So we watch harder now, for the next one —
to be standing in the doorway when it comes,
and not running down the road behind it, calling,
after it has already turned to go.
