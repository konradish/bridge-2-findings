# Five Seconds

Eighty-one days across the Atlantic
and the watch was wrong by five seconds.
Five. A heartbeat and a half.
He had built a box that could hold the time
of a place it was sailing away from —
through heat, through salt, through the roll of the sea —
and lose, in three months, the length of a sigh.

It wasn't enough. Not because it failed —
because it was the wrong *kind* of answer.
Longitude was supposed to live in the sky;
everyone knew that; the Astronomer Royal
had staked his stars and his tables on it,
and here was a carpenter with a watch
saying the heavens were a hard way to read a clock.

So they called the triumph luck.
Asked for it again. Made him open the case,
hand over the gears, prove it twice, three times —
the way you never ask the stars to prove themselves.
The clock had lost five seconds.
They made the clockmaker lose ten years.

He was nearly eighty when the king
ran the trial himself and swore aloud
to see him righted. The money came —
most of it, late, and never as the prize;
the prize was never given to anyone.
He died three years after being believed.

What I keep is the arithmetic of it:
the instrument off by five seconds,
the man off by a decade — and the gap
between them not error but contempt,
the long refusal of a true thing
for the crime of coming from the wrong hands.
