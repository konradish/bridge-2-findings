## The data

JWST's 2022-2025 deep-field surveys (CEERS, UNCOVER, others) found massive galaxies at z ≈ 7-14 — i.e., ~300-500 million years after the Big Bang — with:
- Stellar masses 10^8 to 10^9 M⊙
- Mature structure / "dynamical maturity"
- High baryon-to-stellar-mass conversion efficiency (one extreme case >80%)

These are difficult to reconcile with standard ΛCDM hierarchical-growth timescales: the conventional model expects fewer, smaller, more chaotic galaxies at these redshifts.

## Two camps on the tension

**Camp A (cosmology-needs-revision)**: the observations are real and the standard model needs new physics. Candidate fixes:
- **Early Dark Energy**: extra energy density before recombination accelerates structure formation. Also addresses Hubble tension.
- **Axion Dark Matter**: enhanced early collapse via axion physics.
- **Scattering Dark Matter (SDM)**: handles companion 21cm anomalies.
- **Constant Creation Cosmology** and other non-mainstream alternatives.

**Camp B (astrophysics-needs-revision)**: ΛCDM is fine; we've been wrong about how galaxies and stars formed in the early universe. Sabti et al (JHU 2024) argued the apparent crisis dissolves if you allow higher star-formation efficiency, top-heavy IMF (initial mass function), and observational biases.

## Where the debate sits

As of late 2025: most theorists have shifted toward Camp B but the tension hasn't fully resolved. New JWST data 2023-2025 keeps finding more high-z massive systems; each one tightens the constraint on either:
- How astrophysically permissive Camp B can be (efficiency ceilings, IMF flexibility), or
- How much new physics Camp A actually needs (early dark energy is most parsimonious; axion DM less mainstream)

The Quanta Magazine 2023 piece "Standard Model of Cosmology Survives JWST's Surprising Finds" reflects Camp B's confidence; the 2025 Circular Astronomy review notes "strong tension" remaining. Both can be true.
