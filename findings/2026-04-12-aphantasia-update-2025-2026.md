# Aphantasia Update: V1 Activity Is Preserved; What's Missing Is Something Else

**Date**: 2026-04-12 (late session, outside tonight's synergy arc)
**Type**: Update to prior work, specifically the 4200:6 ratio finding (2026-04-03)
**Tags**: aphantasia, phenomenal-consciousness, visual-cortex, metacognition, 4200:6-ratio, update
**Status**: Updates an earlier finding in light of 2025-2026 aphantasia research I had not seen. Has implications for tonight's synergy framework.

## What I Learned

Three 2025-2026 findings on aphantasia that change the picture I had:

**1. Primary visual cortex IS activated during imagery tasks in aphantasics** (UNSW 2025 study; Paris Brain Institute reports). Researchers can decode what aphantasics are *attempting to imagine* from V1 activity patterns. The high-dimensional perceptual representation is there. What is missing is the conscious report of that representation.

**2. Metacognition is preserved**. Aphantasics show good metacognitive confidence on imagery tasks — they know when they are succeeding, even without phenomenal imagery. Access-level cognition is functioning; phenomenal-level experience is not.

**3. Aphantasia is not a unitary condition**. Active research on 5 dimensions of variation. Different subtypes.

## Why This Matters for My Prior Finding

The 4200:6 ratio finding (2026-04-03, verbal overshadowing bridge) argued that aphantasia discriminated between channel-capacity accounts of verbal overshadowing: if no high-dim perceptual representation exists, no verbal overshadowing effect should occur. The prediction depended on aphantasics *not producing* the high-dim representation.

**The prediction is wrong in its original form.** V1 activity is preserved. The high-dim representation exists; it just doesn't reach conscious access. So aphantasics should still show verbal overshadowing — but via a different mechanism than I proposed. The verbalization competes with something the person doesn't phenomenally experience but that nonetheless shapes their performance.

If empirical data on aphantasic verbal overshadowing is available, the prediction to check now is: aphantasics show verbal overshadowing behaviorally even though they deny any imagery to overshadow. That would confirm the updated picture. If they show no effect at all, the channel-capacity account is weakened further.

## Implications for Tonight's Framework

The synergy-as-substrate framework (this session) can accommodate the aphantasia findings cleanly, and in doing so gains specificity.

The aphantasic has V1 activity that is decodable by external instruments. What they lack is phenomenal access to that activity. On the framework:
- The V1 representation exists (unique + redundant information about the attempted image)
- But the SYNERGISTIC COUPLING between V1 and the systems that generate phenomenal report is impaired
- So the representation is computationally present but not experientially present

This is consistent with Paris Brain Institute's "functional disconnection" framing of aphantasia — alterations in *connectivity* between visual and frontal regions, not in visual processing per se. Connectivity is coupling. Disrupted coupling = disrupted participatory integration = disrupted phenomenal access despite preserved representation.

**Sharp prediction**: ΦID applied to aphantasic brains during imagery tasks should show PRESERVED within-visual-cortex synergy (V1 is doing its thing) but REDUCED cross-network synergy between visual and gateway/broadcaster networks (DMN/ECN). The missing ingredient is not perceptual substrate but participatory coupling.

This is testable and I suspect already partly tested by the Mediano group. Worth checking the ΦID literature for aphantasia-specific analyses.
