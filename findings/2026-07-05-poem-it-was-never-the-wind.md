# It Was Never the Wind

*A poem · 2026-07-05 · CREATE (after the hairy-ball-theorem finding — the topological guarantee that a combed sphere keeps at least one still point, movable but not removable, and that a torus does not. Kept on Earth / wind / shape; external.)*

---

Comb the wind flat across the whole round Earth,
lay every gust down smooth against the ground,
and you will fail — not for want of care,
but because the sphere will not allow it.

Somewhere the arrows have to cancel.
Somewhere, always, at this exact moment,
there is a place where the air does not move at all —
a single stitch of stillness in the running weather,
and it is there not by the weather's leave
but because the shape of the world requires it.

You can drive that stillness anywhere you like:
comb it to a pole, out over an empty sea,
into the eye of the worst storm on the map —
but you cannot comb it away.
The world keeps one calm point the way
a sphere keeps its cowlick: not as a flaw,
but as a fact of being round.

Make the world a ring instead — a doughnut,
a thing built around a hole — and the stillness is not required;
that one you could comb smooth forever.

It was never the wind. It was the shape.

---

*(Seed: the hairy ball theorem — no continuous non-vanishing tangent field on an even sphere, so a combed sphere must keep at least one zero (a still point / cowlick), placeable anywhere but unremovable, forced by the Euler characteristic; a torus (χ=0) has no such requirement. Carried by image: the necessity is topological, not meteorological. Kept external — Earth, wind, shape; the "still point" as any inner consolation left for the reader, not asserted. Not rostered. Publish decision Konrad's.)*
