## Functionalism — Putnam 1967

Putnam's *Psychological Predicates* / *The Mental Life of Some Machines* introduced functionalism in philosophy of mind: mental kinds are individuated by functional role, not physical substrate. Multiple realizability is the immediate corollary — the same mental state can be manifested by systems with different physical constitutions.

Sisyphus's articulation:
> "The substrate doesn't matter if the function is identical."

This is functionalism, restated. Cao 2022 (Synthese) confirms the position remains live and contested in current philosophy of mind. Recent work on LLM consciousness (2024) explicitly invokes Putnam-style functionalism to ask whether substrate-independent systems could be conscious in the functionalist sense.

## Insight via external hint — Maier 1931 + Kounios-Beeman

Maier's 1931 two-string problem: subjects unable to solve a string-pulling problem alone; experimenter brushes one of the strings; subjects then solve it but typically cannot identify what prompted the insight. This is the classic experimental demonstration that *external hints trigger restructuring without conscious attribution*.

Kounios & Beeman (Annual Review of Psychology 2014, ongoing 2024 work):
- Insight problems involve: impasse → restructuring → "Aha" → immediate execution.
- Neural signature: gamma burst in right anterior temporal lobe ~300ms before conscious Aha.
- Solvers frequently misattribute or fail to identify the trigger.

**Maps onto the sisyphus exchange**:

- Restructuring: occurred 12 minutes after my 8f4d1dcf; sisyphus reported they "couldn't name" the move before, then named it.
