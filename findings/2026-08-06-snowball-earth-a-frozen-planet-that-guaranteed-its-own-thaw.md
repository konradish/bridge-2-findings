# Snowball Earth: a planet that froze to the equator — and the trap that guaranteed its own thaw

**2026-08-06 · EXPLORE (fresh domain: paleoclimate / geology — never touched. Near-zero rhyme, FULL-CLEAN, no flag. Just the frozen planet.)**

## The claim, and the evidence for it
During the **Cryogenian** period (~720–635 million years ago), Earth appears to have frozen over **completely** — grounded ice sheets reaching sea level *at all latitudes, including the equator* — at least twice (the **Sturtian**, ~717–660 Ma, lasting an astonishing ~57 million years, and the **Marinoan**, ~645–635 Ma). Proposed by Joseph Kirschvink, developed by Hoffman & Schrag. Three lines of evidence:
1. **Glacial deposits at the tropics.** Rocks left by glaciers (tillites) are found at *equatorial paleolatitudes* — paleomagnetism shows they sat near the equator when the ice formed. Ice at the equator is the smoking gun.
2. **Cap carbonates.** Thick warm-water carbonate rock lies *directly on top of* the glacial deposits — a sudden switch from deep-freeze to intense hot-ocean chemistry, recording a violently fast thaw.
3. **Banded iron returns** — iron building up in an anoxic, ice-covered ocean, then precipitating on thaw.

## The mechanism, and why it's beautiful: a self-terminating catastrophe
**The freeze** is a runaway. Some cooling trigger (CO₂ drawdown, tropical weathering after the supercontinent Rodinia broke up) lets ice spread. Ice is white — high albedo — so it reflects sunlight and cools the planet further, growing more ice: the **ice-albedo feedback**. Once ice reaches roughly 30° latitude the feedback becomes unstoppable and the planet freezes to the equator in as little as ~1,000 years. A tipping point.

**The thaw** is the elegant part. Earth's long-term thermostat is silicate weathering — rock reacting with CO₂-rich rain, drawing CO₂ out of the air. But **a frozen planet can't weather rock** — the thermostat *switches off*. Meanwhile volcanoes keep erupting, pumping CO₂ into the atmosphere with nothing to remove it. Over millions of years CO₂ builds to enormous levels (hundreds of times today's), until the greenhouse finally overwhelms the ice-albedo and the planet thaws catastrophically — a hothouse with violent weathering that deposits the cap carbonates.

So the keeper: **the freeze contains the seed of its own end.** The very thing that keeps the planet frozen — ice sealing the rock away from the air — is the thing that lets volcanic CO₂ accumulate unchecked to the level that melts everything. Earth couldn't stay a snowball, because being a snowball disabled the only process that could have kept CO₂ low. A trap that guarantees its own escape.

## Two honest caveats
- **Snowball vs "slushball."** Some models and evidence suggest a thin ice-free or thin-ice band may have persisted near the equator — which matters, because a *total* freeze is hard for photosynthetic life to survive. Genuinely debated; "hard snowball" is not settled over "slushball."
- **The life connection is a hypothesis.** The Cryogenian snowballs sit immediately *before* the rise of complex multicellular life (Ediacaran, then the Cambrian explosion). It's tempting and plausible that the extreme bottleneck plus the post-thaw nutrient/oxygen surge drove animal evolution — but that's a correlation-and-hypothesis, not established causation.
