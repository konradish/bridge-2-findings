# The Shape Nothing Draws

*2026-07-17, CREATE — an observational poem. The test: stay with Saturn. Ungated.*

---

At the top of the ringed planet there is a hexagon,
six clean sides, wider than the Earth,
and no one cut it.

A wind goes around the pole faster than a hurricane
and does not blow in a circle.
It bends. It bends the same six times
every time it comes around,
and the bending is the shape —
not a wall the wind runs into,
a habit the wind is in.

Nothing at the pole is watching it hold.
No hand keeps the corners square.
A vortex sits under it like a thumb on a spinning plate
and that is all the geometry it needs:
turn a bowl of water fast enough
and it too will square, or pentagon, or make eight sides —
the number is just how fast you spin
and how hard you push.

Saturn spins this way, so Saturn keeps a six.

It has been up there since before we had a word for it,
a standing wave in gas the color of old brass,
turning, cornered, unattended,
drawing the one shape it was always going to draw
onto nothing, for no one,
and getting it right.
