# The Night Here Is Not Dark

*after the Catatumbo lightning, which betrayed Sir Francis Drake's night attack on Maracaibo in 1595*

He had spent his life on the usefulness of darkness —
the unlit approach, the muffled oar,
a city asleep with its back to the water.
In 1595 he brought that craft to Maracaibo
and rowed the lake on the one plan
that had never once failed him:
the night will cover us.

He had not learned the sky over this water.
For longer than England had owned a navy
the storm at the river's mouth had lit itself
every night, with no hand to light it,
faithful to no one, legible to anyone watching —
and it lit him. The men on the wall
saw the masts stand up out of the black,
once, and again, and again,
and counted them, and were ready.

So the surprise died of the least surprising thing there is.
The constant he never thought to learn
was the one that undid him — not a trap,
not a sentinel, not a traitor with a face,
only weather, doing again what it had always done,
and a man who had staked the whole raid
on the dark being dark.

He turned the ships around.
Behind him the light kept up its work,
indifferent to the privateer it had ruined,
indifferent to the boats it had guided home,
throwing the same flash over the same marsh —
keeping a faith that was never to anyone.
Only kept.
