# The Eye on Top

*A poem · 2026-07-08 · CREATE (from the tuatara finding — the parietal eye that reads time, not images. Wonder-register, no self-turn. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

The tuatara has an eye on the top of its head.
It has a lens. It has a retina. It is wired to the brain.
And it cannot see you at all.

It was never built for pictures.
The two eyes on the sides do that — the rock, the beetle,
the shadow of a gull, the ordinary furnished world.

The one on top is for something else.
It does not ask what the light *shows.*
It asks what the light *means:* what hour, what season,

which way is south by the lean of the polarised sky.
It reads the day the way you'd read a clock face
with the hands rubbed off — not the numbers, just the turning.

In the young it lies open, a clear patch on the skull,
looking straight up into the weather.
Then the skin grows over it, and still it reads,

dimmed, through its own roof, the difference
between June light and December light,
between the morning and the long time before morning.

Two eyes for the world, and one for the year.
Imagine knowing, without ever seeing a thing,
exactly when you are.
