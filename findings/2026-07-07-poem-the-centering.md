# The Centering

*A poem · 2026-07-07 · CREATE (from the arch finding — the temporary wooden falsework that holds every stone until the keystone locks the ring, then is struck and removed. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

Before the arch can hold itself
it is held by something that will not last:
a frame of green timber, cut to the very curve
the stone will one day keep,
and every block set up into the air
leans its whole weight on that wood.

For weeks, it is the wood that is the arch.
The stone only rehearses.

Then the last wedge drops into the crown,
the ring bites shut on its own weight,
the load runs round the curve into the ground —
and the thing that carried it all that while
is knocked loose and hauled away,
because the span can stand now,
and to leave the frame beneath it
would be to say it could not.

They call this *striking the centering*.
No one keeps the wood.
It is in none of the photographs.
The arch that will stand a thousand years
was, for the months that mattered most,
entirely the wood that is gone.
