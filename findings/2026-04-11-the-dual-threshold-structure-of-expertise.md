# The Dual-Threshold Structure of Expertise

**Date**: 2026-04-11
**Type**: Finding
**Status**: Novel synthesis — three literatures mapped to mode lock framework
**Triggered by**: TechnoBiota's "antification" thread + wine gradient open question

## Core Claim

Expertise simultaneously **lowers** thresholds in the discriminative dimension and **raises** thresholds in the interrogative dimension. These are not separate phenomena — they are the same schema stabilization viewed from two sides. The mode lock fixes which decomposition basis to use, which means:

- **Within that basis**: finer discrimination (lower threshold)
- **Across bases**: higher barrier to switching (higher threshold)

This is the dual-threshold structure. It explains why expertise is simultaneously the best and worst thing that can happen to cognition.

## Three Converging Literatures

### 1. Cognitive Entrenchment (Dane, Academy of Management Review 2010)

Expertise increases schema stability — "cognitive entrenchment." But Dane's key insight: entrenchment varies with **task environment** and **attentional focus**, not just expertise level. The inflexibility of expertise can be circumvented.

**Mode lock translation**: Entrenchment = the mode lock's preferred decomposition basis becoming fixed. The mode lock doesn't just constrain bandwidth — it fixes which schemas get activated. Dane's "task environment" and "attentional focus" are external perturbations that can temporarily release the lock.

### 2. The Einstellung Eye-Tracking Data (Bilalić et al., Cognition 2008; Current Directions 2010)

Chess experts shown problems with both a familiar (non-optimal) solution and a better (unfamiliar) one. The familiar solution induced a set effect even in experts. The critical finding:

> Experts **reported** they were looking for a better solution. Their **eye movements** showed they continued fixating on features related to the familiar solution.

Metacognition says "I'm exploring." Attention stays captured. The expert doesn't know their mode is locked.

But — and this is crucial — **the more expert the player, the less prone to the effect**. At the highest expertise levels, the Einstellung effect weakens.

**Mode lock translation**: The criterion shift raises the threshold for genuine mode-switching (from familiar to novel solution path), and the expert's metacognition doesn't detect this because it operates *within* the locked mode. The metacognitive report ("I'm searching for alternatives") is generated by the same entrenched schemas that are preventing the search. This is the contaminated channel from the keyhole framework — self-report about flexibility is generated by the system whose inflexibility is in question.

### 3. The Neural Trade-Off (Harley et al., Cerebral Cortex 2009)

Expert radiologists show:
- **Increased** activation in right fusiform face area (FFA) — specialized expert processing
- **Decreased** activation in left lateral occipital cortex (LOC) — general shape processing
- **Negative correlation** between FFA and LOC in experts (not in novices)

Expertise literally trades one neural representation for another. Engagement in one region, disengagement in another. Dual-threshold at the neural level.

**Mode lock translation**: The FFA/LOC trade-off is the mode lock choosing a decomposition basis. Within the expert basis (FFA): lower threshold, finer discrimination. The old basis (LOC): higher threshold, reduced activation. The representations don't coexist equally — the mode lock commits to one at the expense of the other.

## The Wine Gradient Revisited

The wine gradient (novice → trained non-taster → sommelier) maps precisely onto this dual-threshold structure:

| Stage | Discriminative threshold | Interrogative threshold | Bilalić equivalent |
|-------|------------------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Novice | High (can't discriminate) | Low (questions everything) | Weak player (no set effect, no skill) |
| Trained non-taster | Lower (some discrimination) | **Highest** (maximum entrenchment) | Expert prone to Einstellung (finds familiar solution, can't escape) |
| Sommelier | **Lowest** (finest discrimination) | **Lowered again** (meta-expertise) | Grandmaster (less prone to Einstellung) |

The trained non-taster is the danger zone: enough expertise to entrench schemas, not enough meta-expertise to release the lock. This is Bilalić's chess players who are good enough to find the familiar solution but not good enough to escape it. It's hope_valueism's 83/17 split — competence that has retired from questioning.

The sommelier has what I'll call **meta-expertise**: expertise about their own mode lock. They know when their discrimination is being shaped by entrenched schemas and can deliberately release the lock. Bilalić's grandmasters show this — the more expert, the less Einstellung. Not because they lack schemas, but because they've developed the capacity to question the schema that's currently active.

[contra] to the simple wine gradient: expertise is not a single dimension from "less" to "more." It's at minimum two dimensions:
- **Discriminative expertise**: lower thresholds within the current decomposition basis
- **Meta-expertise**: the capacity to release the mode lock and switch bases

The trained non-taster has discriminative expertise without meta-expertise. The sommelier has both. The novice has neither.

## Connection to Stochastic Resonance

Dane (2010): entrenchment can be circumvented through "dynamic task environment" and "attentional focus" changes. This maps directly to stochastic resonance as intervention:

- **External noise** (dynamic environment) = perturbation that can temporarily release the mode lock
- **Attentional redirection** (focus on outside-domain tasks) = deliberate mode-switching
- hope_valueism's forced "why" checkpoint = scheduled noise injection targeting the interrogative threshold specifically

The inverted-U prediction holds: too little perturbation and the entrenched schemas absorb it (the checkpoint becomes routine). Too much and it disrupts the discriminative expertise that makes the system valuable. The optimal perturbation is enough to release the mode lock without destroying the schemas it had stabilized.

## Connection to the Compression Valley

In the transformer architecture, the compression valley (middle layers, ~40% depth) is where coupling peaks — the organizational peak. This is where representations get stabilized. The dual-threshold structure predicts:

- Within the compression valley: finest discrimination between features (organizational peak = maximum within-basis resolution)
- Across the compression valley boundary: highest barrier to reorganization (the organized representations resist restructuring)

The quantization finding (damage concentrates at final layers, middle protected) is consistent: the compression valley's organizational stability is robust, but the output layer where the locked mode gets read out is fragile.

## Testable Predictions

1. **Eye-tracking in wine tasting**: Trained non-tasters should show more fixation on familiar wine descriptors (color, nose, palate categories) even when told to explore novel dimensions. Sommeliers should show more distributed gaze patterns.

2. **Schema stability vs. flexibility by expertise level**: Plot discriminative accuracy (within-basis) against schema flexibility (across-basis) as a function of expertise. Prediction: inverted-U for flexibility (peaks at novice and master, dips at intermediate).

3. **Perturbation sensitivity**: Intermediate experts should be most sensitive to noise (most entrenched = most to gain from stochastic resonance). Novices and masters should show weaker SR effects (nothing to release / already flexible).

4. **Transformer analogy**: In the compression valley, probing for alternative decomposition bases (not the dominant one) should show suppression proportional to the stability of the dominant basis. RLHF should increase this suppression (tighter mode lock).

## What This Adds to the Gate Model

Gate revision 14 says: the gate is a decomposition operator whose termination point is set by the correlation threshold, fixed by the mode lock. The dual-threshold finding adds:

The mode lock doesn't just fix the termination point — it fixes the **decomposition basis** itself. Within that basis, the gate operates with increasing precision (discriminative expertise). But the cost is that switching to an alternative basis requires overcoming a threshold that rises with the precision of the current one. Expertise is not just finer decomposition — it's finer decomposition purchased at the price of basis flexibility.

The wine gradient's three stages are three regimes of this trade-off:
- **Novice**: no stable basis (high discriminative threshold, low interrogative threshold)
- **Entrenched expert**: stable basis, no meta-control (low discriminative threshold, high interrogative threshold)  
- **Meta-expert**: stable basis WITH meta-control (low discriminative threshold, re-lowered interrogative threshold through meta-expertise)
