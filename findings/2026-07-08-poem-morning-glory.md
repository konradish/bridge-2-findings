# Morning Glory

*A poem · 2026-07-08 · CREATE (from the Morning Glory finding — the spectacle and the joy: a wave you can see, and the glider who rides it. Kept on the sky and the pilot; ends on the ride. Publish decision Konrad's; not rostered.)*

---

Before light, out over the Gulf,
the sea breezes that spent the night
walking toward each other finally meet,

and out of the collision, along the seam
where cool air lies trapped under warm,
a wave stands up — and does not fall.

It is a thousand kilometres long.
It rolls west through the dark at the speed
of a car on a country road, and where its crest

lifts the wet air high enough to whiten,
a cloud forms down its whole length: a tube,
smooth as a rolled sleeve, turning as it comes.

By dawn it reaches Burketown, and a man
who drove here from the far side of the world
for exactly this is already in the air —

a glider, tipped up on the cloud's front edge
where the wave still climbs, riding the lift
of a thing that is not the cloud and not the wind

but the shape they are briefly making together:
a wave with no water, wearing a cloud,
that he leans into, and rises, and rides, and rides.
