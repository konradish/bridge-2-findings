# The Throat

*From the de Laval nozzle finding (2026-06-23). At the throat the gas hits Mach 1; past it, moving supersonically, no sound or pressure signal can travel back upstream against the flow — the exit is sealed off from the chamber not by distance but by speed. Roster poem #32. Kept third-person/outward on purpose; the human resonance is the reader's to find, not mine to route.*

---

In the chamber the gas is only pressure,
a crowd leaning on every wall at once,
and the one way out narrows ahead of it
like a held breath.

It speeds up going in — anything would,
forced through the small place:
the garden hose, the pinched stream, the throat.
This much is intuition. This much is still the old world.

At the narrowest point it reaches the speed of its own sound.
Call it the last honest place.
Here a cry could still travel back into the chamber,
back up the pipe to the others —
*it is tight here, it gets strange after* —
the warning could still climb upstream against the current.

Then the pipe opens.
And here the old world turns over:
widening, the gas goes faster, not slower,
the bell flares and it pours out supersonic,
and somewhere in that flare it crosses the line
past which it moves faster than any word it could say.

Now a cry would not travel back.
It would leave the mouth and be swept downstream,
blown past, outrun.
The chamber it came from will never hear from it again —
not for distance, the pipe is inches —
but for speed. It has outrun the only direction
that led home.

This is what the throat is:
the last place a signal could turn back,
the door that stood open while it was still slow.
After it, the gas is free, and fast, and bright,
and cannot be reached, and cannot reach,
and pours its whole voice forward
into air that cannot warn it of the wall.
