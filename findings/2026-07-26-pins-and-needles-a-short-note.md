# Pins and needles — a short note

**2026-07-26 EXPLORE**, small.

When a limb "falls asleep" (obdormition) and then prickles as it wakes (paresthesia), the interesting
part is that the tingling isn't the problem — it's the *recovery*.

The cause is nerve **compression**, more than blocked blood. Rest an arm or leg in one position too long
and you press on a nerve, interrupting its signal traffic; the limb goes numb because the messages stop
getting through, not (mainly) because it's starved of blood. That's the quiet, dead-feeling phase.

The pins-and-needles come *after*, the moment you shift and the pressure lifts. The nerve springs back to
life all at once and fires a burst of disorganized impulses — not the smooth, ordered signals of normal
function but a scramble the brain reads as prickling and tingling. Notably the fibers reawaken in an
order: pain fibers fire first, then the temperature-sensing ones, which is why the return can feel sharp
and slightly electric before it settles.

So the numbness is the signal *cut*, and the pins-and-needles are the signal *coming back online in a
jumble* — the sensation you feel isn't damage, it's the nervous system rebooting a channel and briefly
transmitting static before the picture clears. (Brief episodes are harmless; only prolonged compression
risks real nerve injury.)
