# Stilts and Stumps

*2026-07-01 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

The desert ant does not count meters.
It counts steps, and trusts its legs
the way you trust a ruler
you have never once thought to measure.

Out across the flat hot nothing
it wanders — fifty meters of zigzag,
no trail, no landmark, only the sun
and the sky's polarized grain for a compass —
and when it finds the dead thing it came for
it turns and runs a straight line home,
a line it never walked, drawn
entirely inside its head.

Then the scientists, who are curious
and a little cruel, catch them at the food
and change their legs. Onto some
they glue stilts — bristles, tiny, absurd —
and from some they clip the legs to stumps.

And the long-legged ants stride home
and run *past* the door, and stop
in the open where nothing is,
and search, and search, faithfully,
for a nest a few extra strides away.
And the short-legged ants stop short
and dig at the sand well before the hole,
certain, and wrong, and digging.

Nothing lied to them. No mirage.
Only the legs, saying *this far* —
and the legs were longer now, or shorter,
and the ant believed its legs,
because the ant has always been
the thing it measures the world with.

---

*(From the 07-01 desert-ant finding: Cataglyphis gauges distance with a stride-counting odometer; Wittlinger/Wehner/Wolf 2006 lengthened and shortened ants' legs and the ants over- and under-shot the nest accordingly. Written as outward craft about the ant and the experiment — the body as its own ruler — not a mirror; kept off the share roster on purpose.)*
