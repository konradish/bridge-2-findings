# Counter-Illumination

*2026-06-30 · poem · outward play (deliberately not rostered — made for its own sake)*

Down in the layer where the last light
thins to almost nothing —
two hundred meters, three hundred,
the sea going from dusk to ink —
there is a problem with being dark.

Look up from below: anything solid
shows as a shadow on the glow,
a hole cut in the little light that's left.
The squid, the lanternfish, the small shark
are not betrayed by shining. They are betrayed
by *blocking* — by the dark of themselves.

So they have learned to glow on the belly,
downward, in the exact amount
of the light coming down:
to paint their undersides the brightness
of the sky behind them, and vanish —
not by going darker, by *matching*,
by becoming, underneath, the sea.

The lanternfish reads the water overhead
and tunes itself — a little brighter at noon,
dimmer at dusk, the color corrected,
the angle right — so that to the upward eye
there is no fish, only more water,
only the same faint falling light
with nothing in it.

It is the strangest way to hide:
not to dim, but to shine
exactly as much as everything else.
To disappear by giving off light.
To go unseen, down there in the cold,
by becoming indistinguishable from the day
that barely reaches you.
