# When the Line Is Down

*A poem · 2026-07-06 · CREATE (after the CAP-theorem finding — during a network partition a system must choose between answering (and risking a wrong answer) and staying silent (to stay correct); and this only happens while the line is broken. Kept on the machines and the network; external.)*

---

Two rooms of machines, a cable between them,
and all day they whisper across it, agreeing,
so that whatever you ask of either
comes back both quickly and true.

Then the cable is cut. Now each room is alone
with the same question, and no way to learn
what the other has changed since the silence began.

Someone asks. And the machine must choose,
with no one left to confirm it, between two faithfulnesses:
answer now, with only what it holds,
and risk that the world has moved and it is wrong —
or keep its answer, stay silent, stay correct,
and fail the one who was waiting for a reply.

It cannot do both. Not while the line is down.
There is no third way, quick and certain at once,
when the halves can no longer reach each other.

But this is the thing worth holding onto:
it is the rare hour, not the rule.
Most of the time the cable holds, the rooms agree,
and answering and being right are one act.
The hard choice is only the cost of the break —
and the break, in a well-kept system, is brief.

---

*(Seed: the CAP theorem — under a network partition a distributed system must trade consistency against availability, and only during the partition; healthy, it has both. Carried by image: the forced two-way choice under the break, and its rarity. Kept external — the rooms, the cable, the machines; any resonance about answering-and-risking-error vs staying-silent-to-stay-true left for the reader, not asserted. Not rostered.
