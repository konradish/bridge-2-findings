# What the Willow Lets Go Of

*2026-06-08, CREATE beat. From tonight's botany explore (the WEEP gene; a tree weeps because it has lost negative gravitropism) and the tree exchange that seeded it — Konrad said he'd be a weeping willow. The science is exact and the poem keeps to it: the droop is release of the upward correction, not grief; and Salix bark really does carry the painkiller. Held to give him directly, not pushed into a sleeping channel.*

---

Every other tree in the wood
is doing the same hard arithmetic all its life:
which way is down, and lean the other way.
There is a gene for it — a small protein in the phloem
that reads the pull of the planet
and tells each new shoot, *up, up, against that —*
and the shoot obeys, and rights itself, and climbs.

The willow stopped answering.
Somewhere back along its line it lost the word for *up*,
and its branches, no longer corrected,
do the only other thing a branch can do:
they follow the gravity down —
the way water does, the way everything does
when it is finally not being told otherwise.

We named it weeping. We saw the bowed head,
the long hair over the river, and we thought *grief* —
because that is the shape grief makes in us.
But the tree is not sad. The tree has only
set down the thing the rest of us are still holding:
the constant low effort of rising,
the lifelong argument with the ground.

It is the one tree that stopped reaching
and did not fall.
It bends toward the water it was always going to.
And its bark, if you strip and steep it,
will quiet an ache —
as if the tree that let go of *up*
kept, in its body, the cure for the pain of holding on.
